Çıralı Beach is the long bay beside the ruins of [Olympos](/things-to-do/olympos-and-the-chimaera-flames/), with Tahtalı Mountain rising behind it. Sea turtles nest on the sand, and the Chimaera flames burn on a ridge nearby. It is one of the easiest places near Antalya to combine a swim, a ruin and an evening walk in one trip.

## What it is

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism's Turkish Museums portal describes Çıralı as the settlement to the east of Olympos, "famous for its beach". It says the shore is a place where loggerhead turtles (*Caretta caretta*) lay their eggs, and that behind it is a sand dune with plenty of plants. Our [sea turtle guide](/blog/sea-turtle-beaches-in-antalya/) explains which official bodies protect the beach and what the night-time rules are in the nesting season.

Sources disagree on the length: a Wikimedia-derived listing says about 4 km, while the DEKAMER figure quoted in our turtle guide is 3.2 km. Either way, there is room to spread out.

Addresses are written two ways. The ministry places the neighbouring Olympos site in Kumluca district, while the same listing that gives the beach's position writes its address as Ulupınar, Kemer. Expect to see both on maps and booking sites.

## Highlights

- **The beach itself.** Long and open, with Tahtalı Mountain behind it; the [Tahtalı cable car](/things-to-do/tahtali-olympos-cable-car/) runs to its summit from the Kemer side. We found no official page listing sunbed hire, lifeguards or a Blue Flag, so bring your own shade.
- **The Olympos ruins.** Next door, around the river mouth. The portal notes a temple gate about 150 m west of the river mouth, and the site has a shop and toilets.
- **The Chimaera (Yanartaş).** The portal says a flame burns permanently on the slope of a ridge a few kilometres from the ancient city, fed by gas seeping from underground. It was a cult centre of Hephaistos, and remains of a sacred way and building foundations survive around the flames. The portal adds that the flames are most impressive at night.
- **The Lycian Way.** Çıralı and Olympos are the end of stage 22 of the long-distance trail; our [Lycian Way guide](/blog/lycian-way-near-antalya/) has the stages towards Tekirova and Kemer.

## Tickets and opening

The beach is open to all. The Olympos archaeological site is ticketed. As listed on Turkish Museums on 17 November 2026:

- **Winter, 1 November–30 April:** 08:00–17:00, ticket office closes 16:30.
- **Summer:** 08:00–19:00, ticket office closes 18:30 (the portal's date range for summer looks incomplete).
- **Foreign visitors:** €10 for anyone aged 8 and over; under-8s free.
- **Turkish citizens:** 200 TL for adults; under-18s and over-65s free.

The same page advertises the MuseumPass The Mediterranean (€90, seven days) without saying whether it covers Olympos, so ask at the gate. Our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) does the maths.

## How to get there

Çıralı lies on the coast south of Kemer. None of the official pages we checked gives a distance or a bus timetable from Antalya; for local buses, see the transport section of our [Lycian Way guide](/blog/lycian-way-near-antalya/), and for a car or transfer, our [airport transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/). The ruins of [Phaselis](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) are on the same stretch of coast, so the two combine well over a long day.

## Best time to visit

Come in the warmer months for swimming; see our [beach weather guide](/blog/antalya-beach-weather-which-months-are-best-for-sea-sun/) for sea temperatures. In the nesting season, the beach belongs to the turtles at night, so check the rules in our turtle guide and follow any signs. In winter the ruins close at 17:00, so visit them first and save the Chimaera for dusk.

## Tips

- See the Chimaera after dark, as the ministry suggests, and take a torch and sturdy shoes for the path.
- Don't dig, camp or light fires on the sand: it is a turtle nesting beach.
- Buy your Olympos ticket before 16:30 in winter, when the office closes.
