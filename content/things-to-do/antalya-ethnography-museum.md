The Antalya Ethnography Museum fills two neighbouring 19th-century mansions in Kaleiçi, the walled old town, with the everyday and decorative objects of Seljuk and Ottoman Antalya. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism says the museum was created by arranging the two Ottoman-period houses and opened to visitors in 2019. It belongs to the Antalya Museum Directorate. Entry is free.

## What the Antalya Ethnography Museum shows

The ministry's pages split the collection between the two houses.

**The Lower Mansion** holds Turkish-Islamic works:

- finds from excavations in Kaleiçi itself;
- Seljuk tile fragments from the [Aspendos Theatre](/things-to-do/aspendos-theatre/), from the period when the Seljuks used the theatre as a palace;
- Ottoman ceramics from İznik, Kütahya and Çanakkale, and handmade glass;
- upstairs, Ottoman weapons, everyday objects and calligraphy.

**The Upper Mansion** is about daily life in Ottoman-period Antalya: architectural fragments, Döşemealtı carpets and weaving looms, furnished period rooms that show how a household lived, and displays on the life of the Yörük, the region's nomadic herders.

**The garden** is part of the visit. Antalya Metropolitan Municipality lists a 13th-century Seljuk tower inscription, conquest inscriptions, Seljuk and Ottoman tombstones (many from the 19th century), and cannons with their cannonballs.

## Highlights

- **The Aspendos tiles.** Fragments that tie the Roman theatre to its later life as a Seljuk palace. Pair them with a trip to the theatre itself.
- **Döşemealtı carpets and looms.** The carpet-weaving tradition of the plateau north of the city, shown with the looms that make it. Our [Döşemealtı district page](/destinations/dosemealti/) covers the area.
- **The period rooms.** The quickest way to picture life inside the Ottoman houses you walk past in Kaleiçi.
- **The inscriptions garden.** A quiet corner of tombstones and stone texts.

## Where it is

Two official addresses are in use, both in Kılınçarslan Mahallesi, Kaleiçi. Turkish Museums and the ministry's museum directorate page give **Civelek Sokak No. 20**. The ministry's museum portal (muze.gov.tr) gives **Mermerli Banyo Sokak No. 17**, near Mermerli and the old harbour. The map pin on this page comes from muze.gov.tr. Use it, or ask for the Etnografya Müzesi once you are in the lanes.

## How to get there

No official page gives transport details. The museum is inside Kaleiçi, so walk in from Kalekapısı or [Hadrian's Gate](/things-to-do/gate-of-hadrian/). Our [Kaleiçi walking route](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/) passes nearby, and the [Kaleiçi old town page](/things-to-do/kaleici-old-town/) covers the area.

## Tickets and opening hours

**Entry is free.** Turkish Museums lists every ticket category as free, and the museum is not on the MuseumPass list, which fits with free entry.

The two official portals disagree on hours:

- **Turkish Museums:** winter (1 November–30 April) 08:30–17:30, with the ticket desk closing at 17:00; summer 08:30–19:00, desk 18:30. Its summer row is labelled "1–31 October", which looks like a data error.
- **muze.gov.tr:** 08:30–19:00 every day, box office until 18:30, with no seasons.

On a winter visit, plan to arrive by 17:00. Neither portal lists a closing day. Phone the directorate on +90 242 244 64 01 if you need to be sure.

## Best time to visit

Go when it opens at 08:30, before your day in the old town fills up, or save it for a wet afternoon: it is one of the stops in our [rainy-day museums guide](/blog/antalya-museums-rainy-day/). With two houses and a garden, it is a compact visit that fits easily into a Kaleiçi walk.

## Tips

- **Check the hours on the day.** The two official portals give different closing times in winter.
- **Don't skip the garden.** The inscriptions and tombstones are outside, not in the houses.
- **Accessibility:** no official page describes step-free access, and these are historic houses, so ask the directorate before you go if you need it.
- **Free in Kaleiçi:** combine it with the free Mevlevihane Museum at the Yivli Minare complex; our [Antalya on a budget guide](/blog/antalya-on-a-budget/) has more free sights.
