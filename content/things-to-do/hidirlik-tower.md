Hıdırlık Tower (Hıdırlık Kulesi) is the squat stone tower on the cliffs at the south-western corner of Kaleiçi, right where the old town runs into [Karaalioğlu Park](/things-to-do/karaalioglu-park/). It was built as a Roman tomb, later joined the city's defences, and is now where much of Antalya comes to watch the sun go down. Since July 2026 a new cliff-top terrace and glass walkways around its base have put the ruins excavated beside it on show.

## What Hıdırlık Tower is

The building has two storeys: a square lower block and a round drum on top. Koç University's AKMED research centre calls the base "almost square". The Ministry of Culture and Tourism's French-language tourism site gives the height as 14 metres.

Antalya Metropolitan Municipality says it was probably a monumental tomb of the 1st or 2nd century AD. Twelve fasces are carved beside the main door. These were the rods and axe carried before senior Roman officials, and they are the main reason the tomb is usually tied to the consul Marcus Calpurnius Rufus and his family. AKMED is more cautious. It dates the tower to the late 1st or 2nd century, adds a question mark, and names a second candidate, M. Petronius Umbrinus. It also records two other names for the tower: Maiden's Tower and Deliktaş.

The tower had several later lives. In the Byzantine period it was tied into the walls and used as a defence tower, the municipality says. AKMED adds a Seljuk phase as a pavilion. Under the Ottomans it served as a watchtower and a military store.

**Was it a lighthouse?** The sources disagree. The ministry's French page says it probably defended the citadel and also helped ships get their bearings at night. In August 2026 TRT, the state broadcaster, said it had been used as a lighthouse. Neither the municipality nor AKMED mentions one. The dates differ too: Anadolu Agency reports in 2020 and 2023 placed it in the 2nd century, and the 2023 report credited Emperor Hadrian.

## Highlights

- **The fasces.** They flank the lower door, which faces into the old town. The municipality puts this entrance on the east side; AKMED says north-east, with six reliefs on each side.
- **The plan inside.** According to AKMED, a short flight of steps leads to a corridor and a burial chamber with three large niches. A stone stair of about 30 steps climbs to the top.
- **The excavations.** The metropolitan municipality began digging around the tower in June 2020, supervised by the Antalya Museum Directorate. Anadolu Agency reported an ancient bath with geometric mosaics and underfloor heating, a columned building, stretches of city wall and an old ice factory. The finished project has glass panels over the remains and a terrace on the cliff edge.
- **The view.** TRT describes the terrace as a place to sit and watch the sea, the Beydağları mountains and the sunset.

## How to get there

The tower stands at the seaward end of Hesapçı Sokak, which the municipality identifies as ancient Attaleia's main street, running from [Hadrian's Gate](/things-to-do/gate-of-hadrian/). The gate and the tower are about 600 metres apart as the crow flies, so walk down this lane. It is Stop 5 on our [Kaleiçi walking route](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/). From Karaalioğlu Park, head for the end that meets the old town.

On 21 July 2026 Antalya Ulaşım suspended the Nostalji tram after technical faults and accidents. Buses now cover its Zerdalilik–Müze route. On 24 November 2026 its news page showed no restart.

## Best time to visit

Come in the late afternoon and stay for sunset, when the light is low over the gulf. Muratpaşa Municipality held a fashion show here at sunset in October 2026. Check times for each month in our [sunrise and sunset guide](/blog/sunrise-sunset-daylight-hours-antalya/).

## Tickets and opening hours

As of 24 November 2026, no official page publishes a fee or opening hours. This is an open-air monument that you see from the park and the terrace around it. No source we found says visitors can go inside, so plan to see it from outside.

The terrace took a while to open. In February 2026 the local paper Antalya Körfez reported it was finished but still shut, with the municipality citing year-end budget rules and drainage work. News reports (Sputnik Türkiye and Tourism Today) covered the opening on 21–22 July 2026; we found no official opening notice. Muratpaşa Municipality now uses it for events: a concert and a fashion show in October 2026, and a COP TOWN talk on 18 November 2026. Parts may be closed off when something is on.

## Tips

- **Read the door first.** Find the fasces before you turn to the view.
- **Keep children close.** The terrace sits on the cliff edge.
- **Make it a loop.** Walk down Hesapçı Sokak, take in the tower, then follow the park back east.
