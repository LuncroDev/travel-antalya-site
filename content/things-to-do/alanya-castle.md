Alanya Castle covers the whole rocky headland above Alanya's harbour. Its walls run for 6.5 km, from the Red Tower at the water's edge up to the inner citadel at the top. Give it half a day: the views alone are worth the climb, and the history is layered deep.

## What the castle is

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism says the fortress may date back to the 3rd century BC, which makes it one of the few defences of that age still standing. Most of what you see is Seljuk. In the 13th century Alanya was a capital of the Anatolian Seljuk Sultanate, and the castle took its present shape then. Sultan Alaeddin Keykubad I built both the Red Tower and the shipyard down by the harbour.

## Highlights

- **The Red Tower (Kızılkule).** Built in 1226 on the orders of Alaeddin Keykubad I, it is octagonal and 33 metres tall, named after the colour of its stone. The ground floor holds a small ethnography museum.
- **The shipyard (Tersane).** The ministry says this Seljuk dockyard, cut into the rock by the sea near the Red Tower, is the only surviving example of its type from that period. It built and repaired ships and made sails, and stayed in use until the 1960s. Today it is a ship and maritime museum.
- **The inner citadel (İç Kale).** At the top is an open-air site with a bedesten (a covered market hall), the remains of the Seljuk palace, Byzantine churches and chapels, baths and dungeons. The ministry counts nearly 400 cisterns across the castle, which held its water supply.
- **Süleymaniye Mosque.** A 13th-century mosque rebuilt by Sultan Süleyman the Magnificent.
- **Akşebe Sultan Masjid and Tomb.** Dated 1230 and named after one of the castle's first commanders.
- **The lighthouse.** A lighthouse dating from 1720 guided ships here for many years.

The ministry's leaflet suggests looking for a hidden symbol and an inscription on the walls and gates as you go.

## How to get there

Alanya is 138 km east of Antalya by road, according to the Highways Directorate (KGM) distance table. A road climbs the hill too, but the municipality is cutting traffic inside the walls (see below), so the easier way up is the **cable car (teleferik)**. Alanya Municipality opened it in October 2017. It runs on a 900-metre line from the Damlataş area, above Damlataş and Cleopatra beaches, to the Ehmedek Gate. The municipality said its 17 cabins can carry 1,130 people an hour.

The municipality has also been making the castle a walking zone, with fewer cars and buses inside the walls. Its 2023 update said the path from the cable car to Ehmedek and on to the castle gate was finished. The last section, down to the Red Tower, was still being built. Check on the day which routes are open.

## Best time to visit

Spring and autumn are the most comfortable months for the climb. In summer, go early or late. The ministry kept the castle open until 21:00 from 1 May to 31 August 2026, and until 19:30 in September, so a sunset visit is possible in season. In winter the days are shorter and the site closes earlier. Our [Antalya weather by month](/blog/antalya-weather-by-months-what-to-expect-before-you-visit/) guide helps you pick a season.

## Tickets and opening hours

When we checked on 10 November 2026, the ministry's page listed Alanya Castle as open every day from 08:00 to 17:00, with the box office closing at 16:30. The entry fee shown was €12, and an audio guide is offered. The ministry's leaflet gives 08:30 to 19:00 from April to October. Hours change with the season, so check the [official Alanya Castle page](https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?SectionId=ALN01&DistId=ALN) before you go. The Red Tower and the shipyard are separate stops at the bottom of the hill; ask at the ticket office what your ticket covers.

## Tips

- **Wear good shoes.** The paths are steep, and the ministry warns that walking the full circuit of the walls is tiring.
- **Plan one way down on foot.** Ride the cable car up, then walk down through the old quarter.
- **Bring water and a hat.** Much of the hill has little shade.
- **Look out to sea.** The ministry singles out the view over Cleopatra Beach.
- **Don't climb on the walls**, and leave stones and fragments where they are.
