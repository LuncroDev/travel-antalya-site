The Antiphellos Ancient Theatre is the most visible piece of the Lycian port city that lies under modern Kaş. Its stone rows step down the slope towards the sea, and from the top you look straight across to the Greek island of Meis (Kastellorizo). The theatre and the town's rock tombs are free to wander and sit within walking distance of the harbour. We used the Antalya culture directorate's Antiphellos page, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's Kaş guide and the national cultural inventory, all read on 27 November 2026.

## What Antiphellos was

The culture directorate describes Antiphellos as an ancient Lycian harbour town whose remains spread through Kaş and west along the Çukurbağ Peninsula. Its Lycian name was **Habesos** (Habesa), and "Antiphellos" means "opposite Phellos". Phellos was the main city, up on the Felen plateau about 12 km inland according to the ministry's Kaş guide, and Antiphellos served as its port. The directorate dates the first settlement to the 6th century BC and says the town minted its own coins from the mid-2nd century BC. Hellenistic walls on the peninsula still face Meis.

## The theatre

The theatre stands on a rise of the peninsula west of the town centre. The directorate calls it one of the only theatres in Anatolia that faces the sea. It is carved from local stone with **26 rows of seats**, has the typical features of a Hellenistic theatre and **no stage building**, so nothing blocks the view to the water.

The ministry's Kaş guide puts the capacity at **about 4,000** and says the theatre dates from the 1st century BC and was repaired in the 2nd century AD. The national cultural inventory tags it to the 2nd–3rd centuries BC instead, so treat the exact date as uncertain.

No official page we found gives opening hours or a fee, or mentions a ticket office. Go in daylight, wear shoes with grip, as the stone seats are worn smooth, and bring water in summer. Late afternoon is the best time for photographs, when the light falls on the seats and the island beyond.

## The Lion Tomb and the rock tombs

Antiphellos is a city of tombs as much as a theatre. The best known is the **Lion Tomb** (Aslanlı Lahit), also called the King's Tomb, which stands where it was made on **Uzun Çarşı**, Kaş's busy shopping street. The directorate dates it to the 4th century BC. It is a Lycian sarcophagus on a tall base, with two lion heads projecting from the corners of its lid. The directorate says it carries an eight-line Lycian inscription that has not been deciphered; the cultural inventory counts nine lines in the Lycian B dialect. Either way, it is worth the short walk up from the harbour.

North-east of the theatre is a **Doric-style rock tomb**, known locally as Akdam. The ministry's Kaş guide describes a house-type tomb about 3.5 m high, carved in the 4th century BC, with **24 female figures dancing hand in hand** cut into the wall inside. More rock tombs are cut into the hillside above the town, and Lycian sarcophagus lids lie scattered around the harbour. Our guide to [Lycian rock tombs](/blog/lycian-rock-tombs/) explains how to read them.

## How to get there

The theatre is in Kaş itself, in the [Kaş district](/destinations/kas/), on the road that runs west from the town centre towards the Çukurbağ Peninsula. By the national inventory's map points, it is about 700 m west of the Lion Tomb as the crow flies, an easy walk from the harbour. There is no need for a car; if you are driving down for the day, our [Antalya to Kaş day trip](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/) covers the coastal route, and [Kaputaş Beach](/things-to-do/kaputas-beach/) is on the way from Kalkan.

## Best time to visit

Summer afternoons on the bare stone are hot, so spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons for clambering over the seats. The seats look out to sea, which makes the theatre a favourite spot at sunset. Bring a hat: there is little shade on the stone.

## Tips

- **Combine it with a walk:** start at the harbour, climb Uzun Çarşı to the Lion Tomb, then head west to the theatre for sunset.
- **Respect the stones:** sitting on the seats is common, but don't climb on loose walls or tombs.
- **Book dinner afterwards:** our [Kaş meze and meyhane guide](/blog/kas-meze-and-meyhane-guide/) lists restaurants a short walk away.
