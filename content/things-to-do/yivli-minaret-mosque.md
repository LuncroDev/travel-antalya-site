Yivli Minaret Mosque rewards a closer look than the quick skyline photograph it usually receives. The red-brick tower and low-domed prayer hall give a [Kaleiçi walk](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/) a different architectural focus from the Roman remains and harbour. Treat it first as a place of worship, and fit sightseeing around the people using it.

## What makes it distinctive

Türkiye's UNESCO Tentative List submission places the complex beside Kalekapısı Square, along Cumhuriyet Street in the old town. It describes a 38-metre minaret with eight rounded vertical flutes, a square stone base and blue and turquoise tile decoration. Look at the change from the substantial base to the narrower, patterned shaft rather than only at its height.

The submission associates the minaret with the Seljuk ruler Alaaddin Keykubad I. It dates the rebuilding of the prayer hall to 1373 and describes six domes supported by twelve columns with reused ancient capitals. The minaret and prayer hall should therefore not be read as one unchanged building from a single moment. [The official submission explains the architecture](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6119/).

## Is it a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

It is on Türkiye's UNESCO Tentative List, submitted in 2016. That is not the same as inscription on the World Heritage List. The distinction matters when comparing tours: a listing as a future candidate should not be advertised as an already awarded World Heritage designation.

## A respectful visit

Approach without assuming that sightseeing access is available at every moment. Check the entrance notice and follow staff guidance. If worship is underway, wait or continue your walk and return later. There is no need to squeeze an interior visit into the middle of a service to complete an itinerary.

For entering a mosque, plan modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees, and bring a scarf if you may need to cover your hair. Remove shoes where directed. Keep voices low, silence your phone and avoid walking in front of people praying. Ask before photographing anyone, and follow any restrictions on interior photography.

These are visitor-planning suggestions, not a verified timetable or a claim about a specific day's access arrangements. An exterior architectural stop is still worthwhile if the interior cannot be visited. Do not plan on climbing the minaret: this guide does not verify public access to it.

## How to look at the building

Begin with a wide view that includes both the tower and the roofline. Then move closer where permitted to compare the brickwork, stone base and fluted surface. If the prayer hall is welcoming visitors, take time to look up at the domes rather than photographing only the doorway.

For a simple photography exercise, make one broad picture of the setting and one detail study of the building. Avoid blocking the entrance while checking your screen. Keep a respectful distance from worshippers, and remember that a quiet visit is more important than an uninterrupted image.

## Where it fits in an old-town day

Use Yivli Minaret as a short, flexible stop rather than the anchor for a tightly timed tour. Continue into the old town for its streets and harbour, or use our [Kaleiçi walking route](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/) to connect several sights. [Hadrian's Gate](/things-to-do/gate-of-hadrian/) offers a useful architectural contrast, but deserves its own stop rather than being treated as another part of the mosque complex.
