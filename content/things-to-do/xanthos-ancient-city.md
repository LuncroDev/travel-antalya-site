Xanthos was the leading city of ancient Lycia, and its ruins sit on a hill above the village of Kınık, inland from Patara in the far west of Antalya province. It is a site for people who like their history with a story attached. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism sums it up as the place where the Lycian spirit of freedom is felt most strongly. Give it two hours, and pair it with Patara Beach on the same day.

## What Xanthos is

The ministry's site page tells the city's story through two sieges. Herodotus wrote that the people of Xanthos refused to surrender to the Persians even though they were badly outnumbered, and chose death instead. Centuries later, the city was razed by Rome for the same refusal to give in. Homer, the ministry adds, named a commander of the Lycians at Troy as a man of Xanthos.

Xanthos is on the UNESCO World Heritage List together with **Letoon**, the Lycian sanctuary nearby. Letoon lies across the provincial border in Muğla; the ministry lists it under that province, not Antalya.

## Highlights

- **The monumental tombs.** The ministry says Xanthos is the only place where 15 monumental tombs of this kind have been found. They are the image most people take home.
- **The Inscribed Tomb Monument.** At about 4 metres tall, it carries the longest surviving inscription in the Lycian language.
- **The Harpy Monument.** Its reliefs show human and siren-like figures, and the ministry notes that the monument was taken to England.
- **Missing monuments.** The Nereid Monument, the Harpy reliefs and the Lion Tomb were shipped to England in pieces in the 1800s, the ministry notes. Knowing that helps you read the gaps.
- **The theatre.** The theatre dates from the Roman period.
- **The Vespasian Arch.** This was the city's southern entrance.
- **The walls and acropolis.** The city walls date to the 5th century BC. On the acropolis are a palace, a building thought to be a Temple of Artemis, and the remains of a Byzantine monastery.
- **The old water system.** The ministry says the city's canals, fed by the İnpınarı spring (the "eye of the water"), are still used to irrigate the fields around the site.

## How to get there

The site's address is Kınık Mahallesi, on the Fethiye road (Fethiye Karayolu), in Kaş district. Plan to come by car or with a local tour from Kalkan, Kaş or Patara. Our [Patara guide](/blog/patara-beach-and-ancient-city/) explains how the Xanthos valley runs inland from that beach, and our [Antalya to Kaş day trip](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/) covers the drive along the coast from the city. Antalya city is a long way off, so treat Xanthos as part of a stay in the west rather than a day trip from Antalya.

## Best time to visit

Spring and autumn are the most comfortable months for walking the hilltop. In high summer, go when the gates open or late in the afternoon, and bring water and a hat for the hilltop. Winter visits are quiet and pleasant on a dry day.

## Tickets and opening hours

When we checked on 10 November 2026, the ministry's Turkish-language page for Xanthos listed the site as open every day from **08:00 to 20:00**, with the ticket office closing at **19:30**. Those match the extended summer hours the ministry set from 1 May to 1 October 2026, so winter hours may be shorter; an English version of the page showed broken "00:00" times. Check the [official page](https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?DistId=MRK&SectionId=XAN01) on the day.

The ministry's fee table lists entry for foreign visitors at **€3**, paid in Turkish lira at the exchange rate. Xanthos is on the site list for the **MuseumPass The Mediterranean**, which costs €90 for seven days; our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) explains whether it pays. The Müzekart is valid for Turkish citizens.

## Tips

- Wear proper shoes: the paths cross uneven stone and the acropolis is a climb.
- Read up on the Lycian tombs before you go. With much of the finest sculpture taken to England, the setting and the inscriptions are the main event.
- Combine it with a swim. Patara's long beach is close by, and the ministry's list puts Patara on the same pass.
