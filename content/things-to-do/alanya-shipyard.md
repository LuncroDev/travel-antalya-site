Alanya's Tersane brings the harbour's working history into focus. Where the [Red Tower](/things-to-do/alanya-red-tower-kizilkule/) expresses defence, the shipyard invites you to think about building, maintaining and sheltering vessels. Combine the two rather than treating the waterfront only as a viewpoint.

## Why the building matters

The municipality's monument service dates the shipyard to 1228, under Alaeddin Keykubad. Its five waterfront bays form one of the clearest architectural signatures of Seljuk Alanya. [Official monument overview](https://isletmeler.alanya.bel.tr/alanya-oren-yerleri/).

The city's detailed account describes a structure about 57 metres long and 40 metres deep, with stone walls and brick arches and vaults. Openings in the vaults admitted light and allowed smoke to escape. Different floor levels responded to the rocky site, reducing the need to cut the bedrock.

This was not simply a place to park boats. Repairs, equipment work, storage and administration also belonged to the complex. The municipal description identifies the building's present role as a maritime museum. [Municipal shipyard history](https://www.alanya.bel.tr/S/427/Tersane).

## A useful way to visit

First look at the whole waterfront façade, then pay attention to the depth of the bays and the relationship between water and sheltered working space. Imagine the practical requirements of a vessel under repair, rather than reading the arches only as a picturesque backdrop.

Our recommendation is to reserve a harbour-focused part of the day for the shipyard and tower. Add [Alanya Castle](/things-to-do/alanya-castle/) if you have the time and energy for a larger outing, or use [Alanya Museum](/things-to-do/alanya-museum/) for another perspective on the town's past. The [Alanya day itinerary](/blog/alanya-in-a-day/) helps keep those choices realistic.

## Before you set out

Current opening hours, ticket prices and access restrictions were not verified for this guide. Confirm them locally before relying on an interior visit. Ask specifically about the approach and internal route if uneven surfaces or steps would affect your plans; do not infer accessibility from the view across the water.

Wear footwear suitable for sightseeing and leave enough time to return without rushing. Avoid treating the historic waterfront as an unrestricted swimming or climbing area: follow the signs and staff directions on the day.
