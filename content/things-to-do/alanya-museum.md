The Alanya Museum is the town's archaeology museum, and it fits neatly into a day around [Alanya Castle](/things-to-do/alanya-castle/). Its entrance is in the town centre on Damlataş Caddesi, where the road up to the castle begins, and [Cleopatra Beach](/things-to-do/cleopatra-beach/) is on the same side of town. It is small enough to see in an hour, and entry is €4. The star is a bronze statue of Heracles, 52 cm high, which has a hall to itself.

## What the museum is

The ministry's Turkish Museums portal says the museum has been open since 1967, in a building designed by the architect İhsan Kıygı. English Wikipedia adds that its first exhibits came from the Museum of Anatolian Civilizations in Ankara. The ministry renovated it in 2012. Until then it showed both archaeology and ethnography. Today the halls run from the Palaeolithic to the Ottoman period, and Turkish Museums traces the town itself back to ancient Korakesion.

## Highlights

- **The bronze Heracles.** The museum's symbol: a hollow-cast bronze of the hero, 52 cm tall, shown on its own in the Heracles hall.
- **The Ship and Maritime hall.** Finds from the Roman to the Ottoman periods. Turkish Museums lists an Eros mosaic from Syedra, a bronze Pegasus that was once part of a ship, a relief honouring the emperor Trajan, an amphora raised from the seabed and a naval cannonball from the reign of Abdülhamid.
- **The Alanya Castle hall.** Finds from the excavations on the castle hill. Look for a star-shaped tile from the Seljuk palace inscribed with the titles of Sultan Alaeddin Keykubad, and a lead seal bearing his portrait, found at Karaköy Castle.
- **Inscriptions and coins.** A corridor holds an inscription from Laertes that Turkish Museums dates to 625 BC, beside Roman bronze diplomas. A coin section covers the cities of the region across several periods.
- **The gallery.** Stone pieces from the Roman, Byzantine, Seljuk and Ottoman periods: ossuaries, column capitals, gravestones, Islamic inscriptions and a marble sarcophagus with garlands and Erotes, found under the sea off Okurcalar.
- **The Agriculture Corner.** In the garden, a display on Roman olive-oil making and old farm tools.

**The Heracles date differs between official pages.** The ministry's Turkish-language leaflet dates the statue to the 2nd century AD. The English page on Turkish Museums says the 2nd century BC. Our [rainy-day museums guide](/blog/antalya-museums-rainy-day/) follows the leaflet.

## How to get there

The address is Saray Mahallesi, İsmail Hilmi Balcı Caddesi No. 2, 07400 Alanya. Turkish Museums places the entrance in the town centre on Damlataş Caddesi, at the start of the road to the castle, so you can walk there from central Alanya. If you fly in, the Ministry of Transport puts Gazipaşa–Alanya Airport 40 km from Alanya. Coming from Antalya, our [Alanya Castle](/things-to-do/alanya-castle/) page gives the road distance, and our [airport transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/) covers the coast road. Neither official page mentions parking.

## Best time to visit

Any time of year, but it earns its place on a hot afternoon or a wet winter day, when the castle walls are less appealing. Go before the castle if you want the context first: the castle hall shows what came out of the ground up there. Pair it with a morning on the beach in summer, or with the castle and the harbour in winter, as in our [Alanya in a day](/blog/alanya-in-a-day/) plan.

## Tickets and opening hours

On 15 November 2026 the two ministry sources gave the same winter hours but different summer ones:

- **Turkish Museums:** winter (2 October to 30 April) 08:30–17:30 every day, ticket desk closing at 17:30. Summer (2 June to 1 October) 08:30–21:30. The page gives no hours for May.
- **The ministry's leaflet:** 1 October to 1 April 08:30–17:30, desk closing at 17:00; 1 April to 1 October 08:30–19:00, desk closing at 18:30.

So in winter, aim to be at the desk by 17:00. **Fees, as published on 15 November 2026:** foreign visitors aged 8 and over pay **€4**, and children under 8 enter free. Turkish citizens pay 200 TL; under-18s and those aged 65 and over enter free, as do university students of art history, archaeology and museum studies. The museum is on the ministry's list for the MuseumPass The Mediterranean; our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) works out whether the pass pays off. Check the [Turkish Museums page](https://www.turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/1950-antalya-alanya-museum/1950/4) before you go.

## Tips

- **Families:** the museum has a children's activity room with game-based archaeology lessons, an education area and a playground.
- **Access:** Turkish Museums lists wheelchair access and toilets.
- **Combine tickets in your head:** the castle is a separate €12 ticket, so the museum adds little to a castle day.
- **Photo:** the entrance photo above dates from 2010; the hours on the gate sign are out of date.
