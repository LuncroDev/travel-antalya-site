Sillyon (Silyon in Turkish) is an ancient city on a steep, flat-topped hill in the Serik district, between [Perge](/things-to-do/perge-ancient-city/) and [Aspendos](/things-to-do/aspendos-theatre/). It has no ticket office or visitor centre, and parts of it have slid off the hill in landslides. What remains is one of the most atmospheric ruin walks near Antalya: Hellenistic walls, a stadium at the foot of the cliffs and a medieval Turkish masjid on the summit. We used the Pamukkale University excavation team's own site, read on 26 November 2026.

## What Sillyon is

The excavation team places Sillyon in ancient Pamphylia, on a hill 235 metres high that rises sharply above the Antalya plain. Today it sits in the Kocagözler area of Yanköy, Serik, about 8 km north of the Antalya–Alanya highway. The team gives it as about 12 km from Perge and 17 km from Aspendos.

The site's history page traces the name to "Šalluša" in Hittite texts. The city served the Persians as a garrison base, and when Alexander the Great came to Pamphylia in 334 BC, the page says, his force could not take it. It later minted its own coins and grew most under Rome, in the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD; the inscriptions of a local benefactor, Menodora, date from that time.

The city became a Byzantine bishopric. Under Turkish rule it was a fortress known as Karahisar-ı Teke, with a masjid of the 13th–14th centuries. In 1415, the history page says, Sultan Mehmed I made Firuz Bey governor of Antalya and placed his son Hamza Bey at Karahisar-ı Teke. The fortress faded in the late 16th and 17th centuries.

## The ruins today

Pamukkale University has worked here since a survey permit in 2018, under Assoc. Prof. Murat Taşkıran. The excavation seasons it reports ran from 31 March to 23 December 2021 and from 11 August to 21 December 2022. In April 2026, Hürriyet Daily News reported that the Hellenistic western walls had been rebuilt with original stones and that work on the Hellenistic tower, begun in 2025, was continuing.

A university news item from 2019 says the hill's geology leaves the city exposed to constant landslides, and the team's route page notes buildings near the theatre destroyed by them. The acropolis edges are cliffs, and warning signs were put up in 2021 and 2022.

## Highlights on the visiting route

The team recommends starting on the western slope. Its route, in order:

1. **Stadium** at the foot of the hill.
2. **Main City Gate**, with an Ottoman fountain and masjid just above the stadium.
3. **Roman baths and a nymphaeum**, just north of the gate.
4. **Bastion 1** and the necropolis on the south-west, west and north-west slopes.
5. **The Hellenistic tower** in the fortification wall of the lower city, which the team says stands to roof level.
6. **Main Street**, through the Acropolis North Gate onto the summit.
7. **Kale Masjid**, which the team calls the earliest Turkish-Islamic building in the region.
8. **Rock-cut shops** and a residential area with panoramic views.
9. **The temple terrace, bouleuterion/odeon and theatre**, where the landslides have done the most damage.
10. **The South Gate and Bastion 2**, with a view across to Perge.
11. **The Kastron**, the largest building, with a Hellenistic structure and the bishop's palace behind it.

## How to get there

Brown signs point to Sillyon from three junctions on the Antalya–Alanya highway: Abdurrahmanlar (about 12 km), Yukarıkocayatak, and the Belek junction (about 8 km, the shortest). The road ends in the village of Kocagözler, and **from there it is on foot only**. A track runs north from the village past the stadium to the fountain and baths, and a path to the stadium starts in front of Kocagözler Mosque.

There is no public transport mentioned, so come by car or taxi. From the [Belek hotels](/hotels/?area=Belek) it is a short drive.

## Tickets and opening hours

Neither the excavation team nor any official page we found publishes a fee or opening hours. The national cultural inventory lists the site as "accessible". Treat it as an open archaeological site: go in daylight, and expect closed areas around active digs.

## Tips

- **Wear proper shoes.** The climb to the acropolis is steep and stony, and the edges are sheer.
- **Respect the fences.** The landslide zones around the theatre are unstable.
- **Go in the cooler months.** There is no shade or water on the summit; October to May is easiest.
- **Bring water and food.** No café or toilets are mentioned at the site.
