The Gate of Hadrian (Hadrian's Gate), known in Turkish as Üçkapılar ("Three Gates"), is the easiest ancient monument in Antalya to see: it stands where the old town walls meet the modern city, a short walk from Kalekapısı Square and its clock tower. The arch was raised in honour of the Roman emperor Hadrian, who visited the city of Attalia around 130 AD, and it originally stood outside the walls as a ceremonial entrance.

Three arched passages are framed by Corinthian columns and richly carved marble. The central arch was once wide enough for carts; you can still see grooves worn into the paving by wheels. Two towers flank the gate: one is a Roman-era structure, the other was added later when the gate was integrated into the defensive walls.

**Practical notes**

- Walking through is free and the gate is open at all hours, since it is part of the street.
- Early morning and late evening are quietest, and the stone looks best in low light.
- Combine it with a walk down Hesapçı Sokağı into Kaleiçi, the Yivli Minaret and the old harbour; it is about 15 minutes on foot to the harbour.
- Tram and bus stops at Kalekapısı and Hadrian's Gate are nearby, so there is no need for a taxi.
