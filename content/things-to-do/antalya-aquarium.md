Antalya Aquarium is the city's big indoor attraction, on Dumlupınar Bulvarı in Konyaaltı, about 70 m from [Konyaaltı Beach](/things-to-do/konyaalti-beach/). Its centrepiece is a walk-through tunnel under one huge tank, and around it sit themed aquariums, a reptile house and a cold room full of real snow. It is the obvious plan for a rainy day or a hot afternoon, and for families. We checked the aquarium's own website on 19 November 2026; the facts below come from it.

## What it is

The aquarium opened on 15 August 2012, after the foundations were laid in November 2011. The site covers 30,000 m², with 15,000 m² under cover. The Turkish home page counts 40 themed aquariums, 250 species and 7.5 million litres of water, and says the main tank alone holds more than 5 million litres.

The tunnel is 131 m long and 3 m wide. The aquarium calls it the largest tunnel aquarium in the world; that is its own claim, and the site cites no record body.

## Highlights

- **The aircraft wreck.** The tunnel opens beside a full-size wreck of an Italian Savoia-Marchetti SM.79 "Sparviero", the bomber in our photo. The aquarium says the 1935-model plane took off from an air base on Rhodes and went down off Meis (Kastellorizo) after being hit. It doesn't say whether this is the original aircraft or a reconstruction.
- **The main tank route.** After the wreck come a tropical reef display and an underwater cave with viewing windows, then a sunken "Atlantis" and Mediterranean scenes, and a pirate-wreck viewpoint with tiered seats for watching the sharks. The longest stretch passes a submarine while sharks and rays glide overhead.
- **The themed aquariums.** Separate rooms cover the Indian Ocean, Red Sea, Atlantic and Pacific; underwater caves; the Nile, Amazon and Asian rivers; a dark corridor of morays; Turkish seas, including a tank for the Demre river mouth; and a living coral reef with clownfish, seahorses, lionfish and octopus. A cylindrical tank shows local Antalya sea life.
- **Feeding times.** The English home page lists fish feeding at 12:00 and shark feeding at 13:30.
- **Snow World and the Ice Museum.** An indoor hall of 1,500 m² kept at about −5°C, with igloos, a "house of St Nicholas" and a snow café. Snow machines add fresh snow at night. Warm clothing is handed out at the entrance and is included in the ticket.
- **WildPark.** A tropical reptile house on the terrace floor with snakes, large insects and other small creatures. No species count is given.
- **Extras.** A wax museum (Face 2 Face), an eight-minute XD "ride" cinema and a VR 360 room. Food comes from McDonald's, Mado and a rooftop café.

The aquarium's FAQ suggests one to two hours for the tanks, about an hour for Snow World and 20–30 minutes for WildPark, or three to five hours for everything.

## Opening hours

The official pages don't agree, so plan around the most common figure and check before you go:

- **10:00–20:00 every day** on the English and Turkish home pages, the FAQ and the contact page.
- **09:00–20:00** on the visiting-hours page, and 09:00–22:00 in the Snow World page footer.
- **Last entry:** the Turkish home page says the ticket desks close one hour before closing time; the visiting-hours page says to arrive at least 30 minutes before.

The FAQ adds that hours change with the season, but no seasonal timetable is published.

## Tickets

The site sells tickets online and through its own ticket shop, but on 19 November 2026 it printed no current price list. The age bands are adult (12 and over), child (3–12) and free under 3. The only figure on the site is in the Turkish FAQ, which quotes 45 USD for adults and 35 USD for children, payable in lira. That page is undated and still mentions COVID rules, so treat it as a rough guide and check the price on the ticket page.

What the site does state:

- **Local prices.** Lower prices apply to Turkish citizens and to foreigners with a Turkish residence permit.
- **Online tickets** are valid for 10 days and can be refunded.
- **Discounts.** There are no individual student discounts; school and other groups get reduced rates from 15 people.
- **MultiPass.** A 365-day card for repeat visits, sold for "a small difference" over a day ticket. Its price isn't published.
- **Disabled visitors.** The Turkish FAQ and the English accessibility page give different discount rules, so ask at the desk. Wheelchairs are lent free on a first-come basis.

## Getting there

The aquarium is on Dumlupınar Bulvarı No. 502, Arapsuyu, Konyaaltı. Its transport page lists buses only, all stopping at the 5M Migros store a short walk away:

- **From the bus station (otogar):** TK36 or VF02.
- **From the airport:** 600 or 800.
- **From Lara:** KL08, LF09 or LF10.
- **From Kemer:** any Antalya-bound bus, getting off opposite the [Hotel Su](/hotels/hotel-su-aqualand/).

The page is undated, so check the lines on the day. Our [AntalyaKart guide](/blog/antalyakart-guide/) explains how to pay. Drivers get three open-air car parks, with disabled spaces, and the aquarium lists parking as free.

## Tips

- Personal photos and video are allowed, but no flash. Outside food, drink and chewing gum aren't.
- Go early on a wet winter day: everything here is indoors, so it fills up when the weather turns. Our [rainy-day museums guide](/blog/antalya-museums-rainy-day/) lists other options.
- Teachers working in Türkiye entered free during the aquarium's Teachers' Day offer (13–24 November 2026), with no Snow World clothing handed out during the campaign. Look for similar dated offers on the Turkish home page.
