Kurşunlu is a waterfall outing rather than a beach excursion. In Antalya's [Aksu district](/destinations/aksu/), the main cascade drops into a wooded setting with smaller pools beyond it. Choose it when you want to spend part of the day looking at water, trees and rock rather than fitting another major archaeological site into your schedule.

## What to see

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism describes an 18-metre main waterfall and seven small pools connected by lesser cascades within a two-kilometre canyon. That canyon measurement is not a promise of a two-kilometre signed visitor circuit: follow the route information at the entrance rather than treating it as a walking distance.

The area opened to visitors in 1986 and became a nature park in 1991. The ministry lists viewing terraces, play areas and parking, and highlights nature walking and observing plants and animals. The appeal is the combination of the cascade and its surroundings, not simply reaching a single photograph spot. [Read the ministry's park description](https://www.kulturportali.gov.tr/turkiye/antalya/gezilecekyer/kursunlu-selalesi).

## How to plan the visit

Our suggestion is to leave a relaxed part of the morning or afternoon available rather than arriving with a tight onward booking. Start by checking which paths and viewing areas are open that day. See the main waterfall first, then decide how far to continue according to your energy, the ground conditions and the time you have left.

Wear shoes with grip and keep your hands free on uneven sections. A woodland setting does not make every path level or accessible. If travelling with a wheelchair, a pushchair or someone who finds steps difficult, ask the park about the usable route before setting out. Do not assume the whole walk is step-free just because the entrance has visitor facilities.

For children, make the visit a slow observation walk. Look for shapes in leaves, listen for birds and pause before moving to the next viewpoint. Keep children beside you near water and edges; do not leave the marked visitor route to get closer to an animal or improve a photograph.

## Water, photographs and expectations

Use the photographs here to recognise the place, not to predict the waterfall's exact appearance on your travel date. They are licensed real photographs, not a live report. Rainfall and local conditions can affect a nature visit, so check the forecast and ask about any restrictions after severe weather.

Plan this as a sightseeing walk, not a swimming stop. Follow posted rules, avoid entering the pools and carry out your litter. Bring drinking water rather than relying on a particular refreshment stand being open. A small bag and a cloth for your camera are more useful than bulky beach equipment.

## Getting there and combining stops

The ministry places the waterfall in Fettahlı, Aksu. Confirm the destination as Kurşunlu Şelalesi, not Düden: Antalya has several different waterfall outings. Check your return transport before leaving the city, especially if you are not driving. This guide does not promise a particular bus route, taxi fare or journey time.

If you also want archaeology, consider [Perge](/things-to-do/perge-ancient-city/) as a separate stop in Aksu. Leave enough time for each rather than buying a packed excursion on the assumption that every advertised stop gets a long visit. Ask an organiser exactly how much time is allocated to the waterfall and whether park admission is included.

## Before you leave

Check current admission, last entry, path access and facilities with the park. We have not verified a current visitor tariff or timetable, so none is quoted here. The [official nature-tourism listing](https://ekotaban.tarimorman.gov.tr/alan/5472) is another starting point for park information.
