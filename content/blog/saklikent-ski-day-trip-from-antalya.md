Saklıkent Ski Center sits in the Bey Mountains above Antalya, and on a good winter day you can see snow from the beach at Konyaaltı. It is a small, modest ski area, not an Alpine resort, but it is the only one this close to the Mediterranean coast and it makes an unusual winter day trip. This guide sets out what the published sources say, where they disagree, and why you should check conditions before you drive up.

## Saklıkent at a glance

- **Where:** on the north slope of Bakırlı Mountain in the Beydağları range, in Konyaaltı district.
- **Distance:** the Ministry of Culture and Tourism says it is 50 km from the centre of Antalya, reached in about one hour by bus, taxi or hotel vehicle. A local guide site gives 50 km from the city centre and 65 km from the airport.
- **Height:** the ministry gives the skiing areas as 2,000–2,400 m; the guide site says 1,900 to 2,400 m. The sources round differently, so read the figures as "around 2,000 m".
- **Season:** the guide site says about 10 December to 10 April. Ski-resort directories say "mid December to early April". Neither is a promise; the season follows the snow.
- **Hours:** the guide site says 09:00–19:00, while a ski directory lists 09:00–16:00 and notes that times vary with conditions, the day of the week and holidays. Plan to arrive in the morning and leave early enough to descend the mountain road before dark.

## Is there snow? Be realistic

Snow is the big variable. In late December 2024, Daily Sabah reported that Saklıkent opened after snow reached 67 cm and that the slope supervisor had measured up to 120 cm in places; the resort was founded in 1971. That was an early, good season.

Other years are thinner. Visitors who reviewed the resort on Snow-Forecast describe it as occasionally getting enough snow and often closed for lack of it, and rate it 2.9 out of 5 from 18 votes. That is a small sample, but it fits the area's mild climate. The Ministry says the snow depth is 0.5–1 m with a short season.

The practical advice is simple:

1. Do not book anything non-refundable around a ski day.
2. Check snow and piste status on the day. The guide site recommends official channels or the social-media accounts of the Saklıkent Konut Yapı Kooperatifi, the cooperative associated with the resort.
3. Have a backup plan on the coast. We suggest a few below.

## What you will find on the mountain

Sources agree on a small, simple layout. The guide site lists a chairlift to the summit (the Karakaya chairlift, 800 people per hour), a baby lift for beginners, and four colour-coded pistes: a short yellow beginner slope, a blue intermediate run, a long red and a steep black. Slope lengths differ between sources (Daily Sabah quotes 4,500 m and 3,200 m pistes, the guide site 4,000 m for the red), so we would not rely on any one number.

Also on site, according to the guide site: ski lessons, snowboard facilities, equipment rental, a sledge run (only sleds rented from the resort are allowed on the marked sledging piste) and a summit café with views. Entry to the resort is free; lifts, slopes and rentals are charged separately. None of the sources we read published current prices, so ask on the day and do not budget from old figures.

## How to get there

- **By car:** the guide site says to follow the Uncalı direction, then the Çakırlar–Doyran junction. It says snow chains are a legal requirement and a practical necessity, because the mountain road can be icy even when Antalya city is sunny. If you are renting a car, ask the rental company about chains before you go.
- **By bus:** the guide site lists a Line 500 bus from the Antalya bus terminal, daily between 08:30 and 15:30. Older sources mention weekend shuttles. Check the current timetable before relying on public transport.
- **By tour or transfer:** hotels and tour operators sell day-trip transfers.

## What to wear and bring

It can be 15°C on the beach and below freezing at 2,000 m. Wear waterproof layers, gloves, a hat and sunglasses, and bring a change of footwear for the drive down. Our [what to wear in Antalya guide](/blog/what-to-wear-in-antalya-in-different-months-weather-based-guide/) explains the coastal side of the day.

## A day that mixes snow and sea

Local sources like to promote "skiing in the morning, sea in the afternoon", and it is most realistic in late February and March, when the ski season is still on and the coast is mild. A swim is optional and depends on the weather; see [can you swim in Antalya in winter?](/blog/can-you-swim-in-antalya-in-winter-weather-sea-temperature-guide/) for sea temperatures.

A sensible plan: leave early, ski or sledge until early afternoon, drive down, then end the day at [Konyaaltı Beach](/things-to-do/konyaalti-beach/) or in [Kaleiçi](/things-to-do/kaleici-old-town/).

## If there is no snow

Antalya's other mountain option is the [Tahtalı cable car](/things-to-do/tahtali-olympos-cable-car/) near Olympos, which climbs to 2,365 m. Check its operating status before you go. ## FAQ

### When is the ski season at Saklıkent?

Typically from about mid December to early April, depending on snow. Some years open late, as in 2024, when the resort opened at the end of December.

### How far is Saklıkent from Antalya?

About 50 km from the city centre and roughly one hour by road, according to the Ministry of Culture and Tourism. The airport is around 65 km away.

### Is there an entry fee?

A local guide says entry to the resort is free and that lifts, lessons and rentals cost extra. We found no current price list, so ask on arrival.

### Do I need snow chains?

The guide site says chains are a legal requirement and recommends them for the mountain road. Check with your rental company.
