MuseumPass The Mediterranean is the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's regional pass for southern Türkiye. It costs **€90**, lasts **seven days from your first visit**, and lets you into each covered museum or archaeological site **once**. More than 20 of its sites are in Antalya province, from Perge and Aspendos to Myra and Xanthos. Whether it saves you money depends on how many paid sites you can realistically reach in a week. This guide lists what the pass covers, what is closed at the moment, and when it pays. All figures come from the ministry's own pages, checked on 10 November 2026.

## What MuseumPass The Mediterranean includes

The ministry's product page sets out the basics:

- **Price:** €90.
- **Validity:** 7 days, starting from your first museum visit, not from the day you buy it.
- **Entries:** one entry to each museum or site on the list.
- **Coverage:** more than 40 museums and archaeological sites in **Antalya, Mersin, Adana and Denizli**.
- **Exclusions:** it is not valid at the Hierapolis Ancient Pool in Denizli, and MuseumPass cards are not accepted for night-museum openings.

The ministry's directive on museum entry adds some rules worth knowing. All transactions are made in Turkish lira, so the euro price is converted when you pay. Passes and tickets priced in foreign currency **cannot be sold to Turkish citizens**, who use the separate Müzekart. You must keep your pass for the whole visit. A pass that is misused is cancelled without refund, and the holder cannot buy a new one for a year.

Families should note one more rule: the directive gives **free entry to foreign children aged 8 and under**, so do not buy a pass for a young child.

## Which Antalya sites does the pass cover?

The ministry's English list of MuseumPass The Mediterranean sites includes these in Antalya province:

- **Antalya city and nearby:** Antalya Museum, the Atatürk House Museum, Karain Cave, [Perge](/things-to-do/perge-ancient-city/) and [Termessos](/things-to-do/termessos/) (the site and national park).
- **Belek and Side:** [Aspendos](/things-to-do/aspendos-theatre/), the Side Museum, the Temple of Apollo and Athena, and the Side theatre. See our [Side Ancient City](/things-to-do/side-ancient-city/) page.
- **Alanya:** [Alanya Castle](/things-to-do/alanya-castle/), the Alanya Museum, the Alanya Atatürk House and Museum, and Syedra.
- **Kemer coast:** [Phaselis](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) and [Olympos](/things-to-do/olympos-and-the-chimaera-flames/).
- **Lycia (Demre, Kaş, Finike):** [Myra and the St Nicholas Museum](/blog/myra-and-st-nicholas-church-demre/), Andriake and the Lycian Civilisations Museum, Simena, Limyra, Arykanda, [Patara](/blog/patara-beach-and-ancient-city/) and [Xanthos](/things-to-do/xanthos-ancient-city/).
- **Inland:** the Elmalı Museum.

The Kaleiçi Museum in Antalya's old town is not on the list. It is run by Koç University, not the ministry; our [rainy-day museums guide](/blog/antalya-museums-rainy-day/) covers it.

## What is closed right now

A long list is only useful if the sites are open. When we checked, the ministry's own pages showed several gaps:

- **Antalya Museum** is closed. Its page shows "Closed", and the ministry's fee table marks it closed too. See our [Antalya Museum page](/things-to-do/antalya-museum/) for the background.
- **Perge** is open, but its Turkish-language page says the **ancient theatre is temporarily closed** until restoration work is finished.
- **Side theatre:** closed for restoration since November 2024, according to the ministry, with no reopening date yet. The fee table also notes that the theatre is closed. See our [Side Ancient City](/things-to-do/side-ancient-city/) page.
- **Lycian Civilisations Museum** at Andriake is marked closed in the ministry's fee table, although the Andriake site is still listed.
- **Alanya Atatürk House** is marked closed (it is free in any case).

Check each site's page on the morning you go. The pass does not refund days lost to a closure.

## Does the pass pay? Three example weeks

The pass breaks even once your single tickets add up to more than €90 within seven days. Here is how that works out on three common trips, using the prices above.

### A Belek or Side stay with day trips east

Perge (€11), Aspendos (€15), the Side archaeological site and museum (€7 + €5) and Alanya Castle with the Alanya Museum (€12 + €4) come to **€54**. That is well short of €90. **Buy single tickets.**

### A Lycian coast week from Kaş or Kalkan

Phaselis (€10), Olympos (€10), Myra (€13), the St Nicholas Museum (€17), Patara (€15), Xanthos (€3), Simena (€4) and Andriake (€4) add up to **€76**. Still below €90. **Single tickets win**, unless you add sites further east.

### A touring week across the whole province

Combine the two: Perge, Aspendos, Side, Alanya Castle and Museum, Termessos, Karain, Phaselis, Olympos, Myra, St Nicholas, Patara and Xanthos. That is **€130** at single-ticket prices, so the pass saves about **€40**. It is a lot of driving, though. Our [7-day Antalya itinerary](/blog/7-day-antalya-itinerary/) shows a gentler version of the same week.

**The rule of thumb:** the pass pays only when you cover both ends of the province in the same seven days. The five priciest sites, St Nicholas, Aspendos, Patara, Myra and Alanya Castle, add up to €72 on their own; add Perge and Phaselis and you pass €90. If you stay in one area, it rarely pays.

## How to buy and use it

- **Online:** the ministry's product page has a purchase link, which adds the e-card to a basket. The basket allows up to 10 products per order.
- **In person:** the ministry says you can buy at a sales point with your identity document.
- **On the day:** keep the pass with you for the whole visit. Each site lets you in once.
- **Timing:** the seven days start with your first visit, so you can buy before you travel and start the clock on day one of your sightseeing.

The ministry's pages do not say whether the pass lets you skip ticket queues, so do not count on it.

## MuseumPass Türkiye: the alternative

If Antalya is one stop on a longer trip, compare the national pass. The ministry sells **MuseumPass Türkiye** for **€165**, valid for **15 days** from first use and covering more than 300 museums and sites, again with one entry each. It suits a two-week loop that also takes in places such as Istanbul or Cappadocia, which have their own regional passes too.

## Opening hours

Hours change with the season. From 1 May to 1 October 2026 the ministry kept many Antalya sites open into the evening. On 10 November 2026, the site pages we opened showed mixed hours: Aspendos and Phaselis listed **08:00–17:00** with the ticket office closing at 16:30, while Olympos, Patara and the St Nicholas Museum still showed later closing times. Treat late closing times in winter with caution, and aim to arrive by mid-afternoon.

## FAQ

### How much is MuseumPass The Mediterranean?

€90, charged in Turkish lira. It is valid for seven days from your first museum visit, with one entry at each site.

### Does MuseumPass The Mediterranean include Aspendos and Perge?

Yes. Both are on the ministry's list, with single tickets of €15 for Aspendos and €11 for Perge. Perge's theatre is temporarily closed for restoration.

### Is the MuseumPass worth it for a week in Antalya?

Only if you tour the whole province. A week based in Belek or Side, or on the Lycian coast alone, costs less in single tickets. A touring week that takes in both ends, from Perge and Aspendos to Myra, Patara and Xanthos, comes to about €130 in single tickets.

### Can Turkish citizens or children use the pass?

The ministry says passes priced in foreign currency cannot be sold to Turkish citizens, who use the Müzekart instead. Foreign children aged 8 and under get free entry, so they do not need a pass.
