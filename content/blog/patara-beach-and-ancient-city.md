Patara Beach is one of the longest beaches on the Mediterranean coast of Türkiye, and it comes with a ruined city behind the dunes. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism describes an 18 km stretch of sand, and the city behind it was once the capital of the Lycian League. It is also a first-degree sea-turtle nesting beach, which is why it closes at night all summer. This guide explains how to plan a day at Patara: the ruins, the beach rules, opening hours and where to stay.

## Where Patara is

Patara is in Kaş district, at Gelemiş, on the western edge of Antalya province. The ministry says it was the only opening to the sea in the Xanthos valley, the river valley that runs inland to Xanthos. The protected area around it reaches over the provincial border into Fethiye, in Muğla.

From Antalya, Kaş is 187 km by road, according to the Highways Directorate (KGM) distance table. Patara is further west along the same coast. Carruba Boutique Hotel in Kaş puts Patara about 40 km from the town. That makes Patara a long day out from Antalya and an easy one from Kaş or Kalkan. ## Patara Beach

### What the beach is like

The Ministry of Environment's page on the Patara Special Environmental Protection Area describes the main beach as 18 km long from east to west and about 500 m wide. Behind it are wide, shifting dunes; strong onshore winds blow the sand inland, and sometimes whip up sandstorms. GoTürkiye says the moving sand buried much of the ancient city and calls Patara Beach one of the longest and most beautiful on the Mediterranean coast.

With 18 km of sand there is room to spread out, unlike at small coves such as Kaputaş. The official pages describe no beach facilities, so bring shade, water and food.

### Why the beach closes at night

Patara is a first-degree nesting beach for two species of sea turtle, the loggerhead (*Caretta caretta*) and the green turtle (*Chelonia mydas*), according to the Ministry of Environment. The nesting strip runs for about 7 km and averages 25 m wide.

To protect the nests, Patara is one of 21 nesting beaches where night access is banned in season. Habertürk reported in May 2026 that the ban runs from **20:00 to 08:00, from 1 May to the end of September**. It said vehicles, tents, camping, fires and barbecues are banned outright. The 2026 fine for an individual who breaks the rules was given as 699,245 TL. Two state bodies manage the beaches: the nature and national parks directorate (DKMP) and the directorate for protected natural assets.

During the day, keep away from any fenced or marked nests and take your rubbish with you. In summer, plan to leave the beach before 20:00.

## Patara Ancient City

### What Patara was

The ministry says Patara was the capital of the Lycian League, and that Hittite texts of the 13th century BC already mention a place called *Patar*. In Roman times it was a major port for storing grain and shipping it from Anatolia to Rome, and it was known for an oracle of Apollo. Lycia became a Roman province in 43 AD.

Two famous names are linked to the city. The ministry says **St Nicholas**, the saint behind Santa Claus, was born here, and that **St Paul** is said to have sailed from Patara on his way to Rome. Nicholas later became Bishop of Myra, near Demre; our [Myra and St Nicholas Church guide](/blog/myra-and-st-nicholas-church-demre/) covers that site.

### What to see in the ruins

- **The triumphal gateway.** The entrance to the city. The ministry says inscriptions suggest the regional governor built it in 100 AD.
- **The necropolis.** West of the arch, with Lycian-style sarcophagi.
- **The theatre.** At the southern end of the city, built against Kurşunlu Hill and rebuilt after an earthquake in 147 AD.
- **The Bouleuterion.** The assembly building beside the theatre, where the league's meetings took place. GoTürkiye calls it the restored town hall.
- **Kurşunlu Hill.** Climb it for the view over the site, the dunes and the sea. The ministry lists the Baths of Vespasian, a Corinthian temple, the main street and the old harbour buildings in this part of the city.
- **The granary.** Built in the 2nd century AD by Emperor Hadrian and his wife Sabina. The ministry also notes water channels that brought water from a cliff about 20 km away.

GoTürkiye adds a lighthouse and a colonnaded main street to the list. The site is spread out, so allow at least two hours.

### Patara opening hours and tickets

When we checked on 10 November 2026, the ministry's page listed the Patara site as open every day from 08:00 to 19:00, with the box office closing at 18:30, and showed an entry fee of €15. The ministry's leaflet gives seasonal hours instead: 08:30 to 19:00 from April to October, and 08:30 to 17:30 from October to April. An audio guide is offered. Check the [official Patara page](https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?SectionId=PTR01&DistId=PTR) on the day.

The ministry's page does not say whether you need a site ticket to reach the beach. Ask at the entrance, and keep your ticket if you plan to move between the ruins and the sand.

Patara is on the ministry's list for the **MuseumPass The Mediterranean**, along with nearby Xanthos, Myra and the St Nicholas Memorial Museum. If you are touring the Lycian sites, compare the pass with separate tickets.

## When to go

- **May, June and September** suit most people: long days for both the ruins and the beach, and fewer people than in high summer. The night ban applies.
- **July and August** are hot. Walk the ruins early, swim at midday under shade, and leave the beach before 20:00.
- **October to April** is quiet. The night ban is over, but the site closes earlier and the sea cools. On a windy day the dunes can be harsh.

## A day at Patara

- **09:00:** arrive when the site opens. Walk from the triumphal gateway to the theatre and Bouleuterion, then climb Kurşunlu Hill.
- **12:00:** head for the beach. Swim, rest and eat your packed lunch.
- **16:00:** go back for the granary and main street if you missed them, or drive on to Kalkan for dinner.

## FAQ

### How long is Patara Beach?

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism and the Ministry of Environment both give 18 km. The Ministry of Environment adds that the beach is about 500 m wide, with wide, shifting dunes behind it.

### Can you go to Patara Beach at night?

Not in season. Habertürk reported in May 2026 that night access to Patara and other turtle-nesting beaches is banned from 20:00 to 08:00 between 1 May and the end of September, with a fine of 699,245 TL in 2026.

### What are the Patara ruins opening hours?

When we checked on 10 November 2026, the ministry listed the site as open daily from 08:00 to 19:00, with the box office closing at 18:30. Its leaflet gives 08:30 to 17:30 in winter. Check the official page before you go.

### Was St Nicholas born in Patara?

Yes. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism says St Nicholas was born in Patara. He later became Bishop of Myra, today's Demre, where his church still stands.
