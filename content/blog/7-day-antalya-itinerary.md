This 7-day Antalya itinerary covers the three things most people come for: the old city, the Roman ruins and the coast. It starts with four nights in Antalya city and day trips east and north, then moves west along the coast to Çıralı and Kaş for the last three nights. You can drive it in a hire car or piece it together from tours and buses. Every day links to our detailed pages, and we flag the sites that are closed or partly closed as of 10 November 2026.

Before you go, read [how to get to Antalya](/blog/how-to-get-to-antalya-flights-airport-transport-options/) and check the [best time to visit](/blog/what-is-the-best-time-to-visit-antalya-for-ideal-weather/). Spring and autumn suit this plan best, because it involves a lot of walking on open ruins.

## Day 1: Kaleiçi and the old harbour

Start where Antalya started. Walk through [Hadrian's Gate](/things-to-do/gate-of-hadrian/), the triple Roman arch built for the emperor's visit, and drop into the lanes of [Kaleiçi](/things-to-do/kaleici-old-town/), the walled old town around the harbour. The small Kaleiçi Museum, run by Koç University, is the best indoor stop here; check its opening days in our [rainy-day museums guide](/blog/antalya-museums-rainy-day/), because it closes one day a week.

In the late afternoon, walk out to [Karaalioğlu Park](/things-to-do/karaalioglu-park/) for the clifftop views. For dinner, stay in the old town at [Seraser](/restaurants/seraser-fine-dining/) or [Mermerli](/restaurants/mermerli-restaurant/). Sleep in one of the [Kaleiçi hotels](/hotels/?area=Kalei%C3%A7i) if you want to walk everywhere.

**Skip this:** the Antalya Museum. The ministry lists it as closed while a new museum is built; see our [Antalya Museum page](/things-to-do/antalya-museum/) for updates.

## Day 2: Perge and Aspendos

These two Roman cities lie east of the city, towards Belek, and make a natural pair.

- **[Perge](/things-to-do/perge-ancient-city/)** is open, but the ministry's Turkish page says its **ancient theatre is temporarily closed** until restoration is finished. The colonnaded street, agora and gates are still the main event.
- **[Aspendos](/things-to-do/aspendos-theatre/)** has the famous theatre. On 10 November 2026 the ministry listed it as open every day from **08:00 to 17:00**, with the ticket office closing at **16:30**.

Do Perge in the morning and Aspendos after lunch, but watch the ticket-office time in winter. Back in the city, eat like a local with the köfte and tahini piyaz at [Ertan Usta](/restaurants/ertan-usta-aksu-kofte-piyaz-kepez/) in Kepez, which is closed on Fridays.

## Day 3: Side and Manavgat Waterfall

Head further east to [Side](/things-to-do/side-ancient-city/), where ruins sit right among the shops and the Temple of Apollo stands on the waterfront. Be ready for a gap: the **Side theatre has been closed for restoration since November 2024**, according to the ministry, with no reopening date yet. The Side Museum and the archaeological site are listed separately in the ministry's fee table.

On the way back, stop at [Manavgat Waterfall](/things-to-do/manavgat-waterfall/), which is at its strongest after winter rain. If you would rather swap ruins for adventure, replace this day with rafting in [Köprülü Canyon](/things-to-do/koprulu-canyon/).

## Day 4: Termessos and an afternoon on the beach

[Termessos](/things-to-do/termessos/) is the mountain city that Alexander the Great is said to have left alone. It sits inside a national park north-west of the city, and the ruins are unrestored and spread over a steep hillside. Go early: it is a real climb. The ministry's page listed the ticket office closing at 17:00 when we checked, and the foreign-visitor ticket is €3 in its fee table.

Come down for a lazy afternoon at [Konyaaltı Beach](/things-to-do/konyaalti-beach/), with the mountains behind you, or visit the [Düden Waterfalls](/things-to-do/duden-waterfalls/). In the evening, look at what's on: our [events listings](/events/) cover concerts and theatre through the winter.

## Day 5: Phaselis, the Kemer coast and Olympos

Pick up your car and drive south-west along the coast. [Phaselis](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) is the ruin you can combine with a swim: an old port city under pine trees, with its harbours as bathing bays. On 10 November 2026 the ministry listed it from 08:00 to 17:00.

If the sky is clear, ride the [Tahtalı Olympos cable car](/things-to-do/tahtali-olympos-cable-car/) for the view. Then continue to Çıralı. Explore the valley ruins of [Olympos](/things-to-do/olympos-and-the-chimaera-flames/) in the afternoon and walk up to the Chimaera flames after dark, when they show best.

Sleep in Çıralı: [Olympos Lodge](/hotels/olympos-lodge-cirali/) has a garden that runs down to the beach. The lodge puts Çıralı about 80 km from Antalya Airport by the southern coast road.

## Day 6: Myra, St Nicholas and Kaş

Continue west to Demre. Our [Myra and St Nicholas guide](/blog/myra-and-st-nicholas-church-demre/) covers both: the rock-cut Lycian tombs and theatre at Myra, and the church linked to the saint who became Santa Claus. In the ministry's fee table, the St Nicholas Museum is the most expensive ticket in the province, at €17. The Lycian Civilisations Museum at nearby Andriake is marked closed in the same table.

Drive on to Kaş for the night. On the Çukurbağ Peninsula, [Suna Sun Hotel](/hotels/suna-sun-hotel-kas/) is a small 14+ hotel with its own swimming platform, and [Elia](/restaurants/elia-restaurant-kas/) serves seafood meze with a sunset view. In town, see [Carruba](/hotels/carruba-boutique-hotel-kas/) and [Naturel](/restaurants/naturel-restaurant-kas/). Our [Kaş district guide](/destinations/kas/) has the rest.

## Day 7: Patara and Xanthos

Finish with Lycia's best pair. [Patara](/blog/patara-beach-and-ancient-city/) has a huge beach next to the ruins of an ancient port, and [Xanthos](/things-to-do/xanthos-ancient-city/), inland at Kınık, was the leading city of the Lycians, famous for its monumental tombs. Stop at [Kaputaş Beach](/blog/kaputas-beach-travel-antalya/) on the way if the sea is calm.

From here it is closer to fly home from Dalaman than to drive back to Antalya: Suna Sun Hotel puts Dalaman Airport about 150 km from Kaş, and the drive from Antalya Airport at about three hours. Check whether your airline flies from Dalaman before you book this way.

## What the week costs in tickets

Using the ministry's foreign-visitor fees, the paid sites in this plan add up to about €109: Perge €11, Aspendos €15, Side €12 (archaeological site and museum), Termessos €3, Phaselis €10, Olympos €10, Myra €13, St Nicholas €17, Patara €15 and Xanthos €3. The **MuseumPass The Mediterranean** costs €90 for seven days from the first visit, so on this route it saves you a little. Our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) explains the rules.

Fees are charged in Turkish lira at the day's rate. Foreign children aged 8 and under get in free, according to the ministry's directive.

## How to adapt the itinerary

- **No car?** Stay in Antalya city all week. Days 1–4 work with tours, taxis and buses; replace days 5–7 with a tour to the Kemer coast and our [Antalya to Kaş day trip](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/).
- **With children:** keep the ruins short and add beach time. Our guide to [Antalya with kids](/blog/is-antalya-good-for-families-things-to-do-in-antalya-with-kids/) has ideas.
- **Golfers:** swap day 3 for a round in Belek. In winter, a floodlit round is an option; see [night golf in Belek](/blog/night-golf-in-belek/).
- **Prefer Alanya?** Replace day 3 with [Alanya Castle](/things-to-do/alanya-castle/), which takes half a day on its own.
- **Rain:** swap an outdoor day for our [rainy-day museums](/blog/antalya-museums-rainy-day/) plan.

## Practical tips

- **Check hours each morning.** Winter hours are shorter than summer ones, and several site pages showed different times in English and Turkish when we checked.
- **Start early.** Ruins like Termessos and Xanthos are exposed hilltops; morning is cooler in summer and gives you more light in winter.
- **Expect lira charges.** Site fees are set in euros but charged in Turkish lira.
- **Check the season dates of small hotels.** Neither Suna Sun nor Olympos Lodge publishes them, so ask before you build the trip around them.

## FAQ

### Is 7 days enough for Antalya?

Yes, for the highlights. A week covers the old town, the big Roman sites east of the city, Termessos, the Kemer coast and the Lycian ruins around Kaş. You will not see everything, but you will see the best of each area.

### Where should I stay for a week in Antalya?

This plan uses four nights in Antalya city, one in Çıralı and one in Kaş. If you would rather not move, stay in the city all week and take day trips; browse [hotels by area](/hotels/).

### Do I need a car for this Antalya itinerary?

It helps from day 5 onwards, when the route runs down the coast. Days 1–4 can be done with tours, taxis and buses from the city.

### Is the MuseumPass worth it for this itinerary?

Just about. The paid sites on this route come to about €109 in single tickets, against €90 for the MuseumPass The Mediterranean. Remember that the theatres at Perge and Side are both closed for restoration.
