Sea turtle beaches in Antalya are some of the best stretches of sand on the coast, and from May to September they come with rules. Loggerhead turtles (*Caretta caretta*) and some green turtles (*Chelonia mydas*) haul out at night to lay eggs, and the hatchlings head for the sea in midsummer. That's why several of the province's favourite beaches close at night, keep vehicles out and ask hotels to dim their lights. This guide lists the official nesting beaches, the season, the rules that apply to visitors and what to do if you meet a turtle. We used the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry's nature directorate (DKMP), the Ministry of Environment, Türkiye's 2026 reports to the Bern Convention and news reports, all opened on 16 November 2026.

The same beaches appear in the national circular on protecting sea turtles (No. 2009/10), which lists Olympos–Çıralı and Tekirova–Belek as pairs. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism's Antalya office also calls the beaches of Belek, Manavgat, Tekirova, Çıralı, Olympos, Adrasan, Kumluca and Kale "first-degree" nesting areas. Nationally, 2026 news reports count 21 nesting beaches. Five of the nesting areas, Patara and Belek among them, lie inside special protected areas run by the Ministry of Environment; DKMP looks after the rest.

### The two big ones: Belek and Patara

- **Belek:** the Ministry of Environment's page for the Belek Special Environmental Protection Area says a strip of about 13.3 km, from the mouth of the Acısu stream to the Sarısu, is the turtle breeding area, and calls it a nesting beach of first-degree importance. DEKAMER, a university-linked rescue centre, measures the wider Belek beach at 29.3 km. Most of the big [Belek resorts](/destinations/serik/) sit along it.
- **Patara:** the ministry calls Patara a first-degree nesting area for both loggerheads and green turtles. Türkiye's February 2026 report to the Bern Convention counted 757 nests there in 2025 (756 loggerhead, one green) and more than 40,000 hatchlings.

DKMP says 144,334 hatchlings reached the sea from Antalya's beaches in 2023, up from 61,691 the year before.

## When is nesting season?

Sources give slightly different dates, so plan for the widest window:

- **2026 news reports** (Habertürk, Sözcü, Hürriyet Daily News) describe the night ban as running from 1 May to the end of September.
- **Türkiye's Patara report** to the Bern Convention gives the nesting season as 15 May–30 September.
- **The 2009/10 circular** keeps animals and motor vehicles off nesting beaches from May to September, and limits boat speeds near the shore from 1 April to 30 September.

DKMP says nesting starts in April–May and hatching ends in August–September. In 2026, a Belek turtle group told DHA that the first nest was found on 12 May and the first hatchlings reached the sea on 11 July.

From October to April the night closures don't apply, but the beaches are still protected areas.

## The rules for visitors

### Off the sand from 20:00 to 08:00

This is the rule most visitors meet. The Patara government report says public access to and use of the beach are prohibited between 20:00 and 08:00 in the nesting season, and that all human activity stops after 20:00. News reports in May 2026 said the same night ban applies on all 21 nesting beaches, managed by DKMP and the Ministry of Environment's protected-areas directorate. Hürriyet Daily News reported it as 8 p.m. to 8 a.m. through the end of September.

### No vehicles, tents, fires or barbecues

The circular bans animals and motor vehicles on nesting beaches from May to September. The May 2026 reports add tents, camping, fires and barbecues, citing Article 9(a) of the Environmental Law (No. 2872). At Patara, the government report also bans horse riding, and a barrier at Karadere keeps vehicles out from May to September.

### Lights off at night

The Patara report says artificial lighting on the beach is strictly prohibited. The circular asks for lighting and noise to be kept down, and says fixed piers may be used only for swimming and sunbathing, with no lights and no night use. Back in 2017, DKMP told beach businesses to darken their premises between 22:00 and 04:00 and use red or yellow low-pressure sodium lamps (reported by Hürriyet Daily News). Hatchlings head for the brightest horizon, so a torch or phone light can lead them the wrong way.

### Sunbeds, umbrellas and nests

At Patara, the report says sunbeds, umbrellas and pavilions aren't allowed in the nesting zone, which it describes as a band 25–60 m back from the sea, and that furniture in potential nesting areas is removed every evening at 20:00. Nests are protected with cages, either raised frames or flat mesh under the sand. On hotel beaches, leave sunbeds where the staff put them and never move them into marked or caged areas. An environmental group told DHA in July 2026 that a 35 m sunbed-and-umbrella rule was not being respected, but we found no official page stating that distance.

### Don't feed or crowd turtles

DKMP's Antalya office says feeding sea turtles is banned and that you shouldn't go closer to nests than the permitted distance. It doesn't give a number, so stay well back from any cage or marker.

### Boats and water sports

The circular limits boats to 8 knots within one mile of nesting beaches from 1 April to 30 September, and puts land-based water-sports stations at least 65 m back from the shoreline. At Patara, the government report says fishing is banned within one nautical mile.

### Fines

The May 2026 reports put the 2026 fine for entering a nesting beach at night at 699,245 TL for individuals, and three times that for companies. The amount is set for each year, so expect it to change in 2027.

### A note on enforcement

Rules and practice don't always match. A complaint filed with the Bern Convention in 2026 by MEDASSET, a conservation group, says only a small share of Patara's sunbeds were collected in the evening, and reports cars and tent camping at its edges. If you see a problem, tell the beach information office or the gendarmerie rather than intervening.

## Where you can still enjoy these beaches

Daytime visits are fine and are what most people do. Some ideas:

- **Patara:** go for the day and leave by 20:00; our [Patara guide](/blog/patara-beach-and-ancient-city/) covers the ruins and the beach road.
- **Çıralı:** a 3.2 km beach by DEKAMER's figures, below the Olympos ruins. In July 2026 a local cooperative told DHA it had counted 108 nests. Stay at the [Olympos Lodge](/hotels/olympos-lodge-cirali/) and walk the beach early.
- **Belek:** most resorts own a section of the nesting beach. Ask your hotel how it handles nests and night lighting.
- **Tekirova and Phaselis:** Bostanlık Koyu near [Phaselis](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) closes in season for nesting.

For every beach in the province, see our [best beaches in Antalya](/blog/best-beaches-in-antalya/) guide.

## If you find a turtle or hatchlings

No official turtle hotline is published for Antalya; DKMP's regional site lists only general numbers. Our advice, based on the rules above:

1. Keep your distance and turn off torches and phone lights.
2. Don't touch, lift or feed the turtle, and don't carry hatchlings to the sea.
3. Tell the beach information office, project volunteers or hotel staff. At Patara the government report mentions information offices on the beach and gendarmerie patrols.
4. For an injured animal or an emergency, call 112, Türkiye's single emergency number, and say where you are.

## FAQ

### Which beaches in Antalya have sea turtles?

DKMP lists nine nesting beaches in Antalya province: Patara, Kale (Demre), Kumluca, Çıralı, Tekirova, Belek, Kızılot, Demirtaş and Gazipaşa. Belek and Patara are the largest.

### Can you go on Patara or Belek beach at night?

Not in the nesting season. Public access is banned from 20:00 to 08:00, roughly May to September depending on the source. Daytime visits are allowed.

### When do sea turtles hatch in Antalya?

From about mid-July to September. In 2026 the first Belek hatchlings reached the sea on 11 July, according to a local turtle group quoted by DHA.

### What is the fine for entering a turtle beach at night?

News reports in May 2026 put it at 699,245 TL for individuals and three times that for companies.
