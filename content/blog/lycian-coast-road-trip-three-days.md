A Lycian coast road trip is the best way to see the ancient cities west of Antalya without squeezing each one into a long day trip. In three days you drive the coast from Phaselis, just past Kemer, to Xanthos and Patara beyond Kaş, sleeping in Çıralı and Kaş on the way. This plan strings together our pages for every stop. On 23 November 2026 we opened the ministry's two official portals, [Turkish Museums](https://www.turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/1966-antalya-myra-archaeological-site/1966/4) and [muze.gov.tr](https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?DistId=MYR&SectionId=MYR01), for each site. They often disagree on winter hours, so we give both and plan around the shorter set.

## The road trip at a glance

| Day | Route | Main stops | Sleep |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Antalya, Kemer, Çıralı | Phaselis, Olympos, the Chimaera flames | Çıralı |
| 2 | Çıralı, Kumluca, Finike, Demre, Kaş | Myra, St Nicholas Church; Limyra or Kekova if time allows | Kaş |
| 3 | Kaş, Kaputaş, Kalkan, the Eşen valley | Kaputaş, Xanthos, Patara; Letoon as an extra | Kalkan or Kaş |

The Highways Directorate (KGM) distance table puts Kemer 44 km from Antalya, Kumluca 94 km, Finike 112 km, Demre 140 km and Kaş 187 km. The stops, not the kilometres, fill the days.

What to make of it:

- **Plan to the shorter hours.** Arrive after the later opening time and buy your ticket before the earlier desk closing. muze.gov.tr shows one set of hours with no seasons.
- **Some date rows are misprinted.** On Turkish Museums, Patara's winter row starts on 1 October, overlapping its summer row, and Letoon's summer and winter rows both read 1 April to 1 October.
- **Other fees.** Under-8s from abroad go free. Turkish adults pay 200 TL; Turkish citizens under 18 or over 65 go free. The St Nicholas fee is from Turkish Museums; muze.gov.tr shows none.
- **Check first:** the Lycian Civilisations Museum at Andriake, Demre (see Day 2); official pages disagree on whether it is open. No page we opened gives hours or a fee for the Chimaera flames.

## Day 1: Phaselis and Olympos

### Morning: Phaselis

Drive south from Antalya on the coast road through Kemer. The official figures for the last stretch differ: Antalya Metropolitan Municipality puts Phaselis bay about 12 km from Kemer, the provincial culture directorate says 16 km, and Kemer Municipality says 20 minutes by car. There is a car park at the entrance, according to the municipality.

Phaselis sits on a small peninsula among the pine and cedar forest of the Beydağları Coastal National Park. Turkish Museums says settlers from Lindos on Rhodes founded it around 691/690 BC. It had three natural harbours. Walk the main street down to the South Harbour, then look for the aqueduct, which the ministry calls one of the best-preserved structures in the city. Turkish Museums lists a café, a shop and toilets. Both portals agree on the hours: 08:00 to 17:00, desk closing 16:30. See our [Phaselis Ancient City](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) page.

On a clear morning you could add the [Tahtalı cable car](/things-to-do/tahtali-olympos-cable-car/); our [Kemer to Olympos guide](/blog/kemer-to-olympos-coast-road/) sets out its conflicting winter hours.

### Afternoon: Olympos and the Chimaera

Continue to Çıralı. The metropolitan municipality puts it 28 km from Kemer, with a 3.5 km beach and restaurants, cafés and bars close by. The ministry notes that loggerhead turtles lay their eggs on this beach, so read our [sea turtle guide](/blog/sea-turtle-beaches-in-antalya/) and the [Çıralı Beach](/things-to-do/cirali-beach/) page before you walk the sand.

The ruins of [Olympos](/things-to-do/olympos-and-the-chimaera-flames/) are at Yazır, in Kumluca district. Turkish Museums gives winter hours of 08:00 to 17:00 (desk 16:30); muze.gov.tr shows 08:00 to 19:00. Get there by 16:30 to be safe.

At dusk, go to the Chimaera. Turkish Museums describes a permanent flame on the south slope of the Yanartaş ridge, fed by gas that has seeped from the ground for thousands of years, at a site once sacred to Hephaistos. Bring a torch for the path down.

### Sleep in Çıralı

[Olympos Lodge](/hotels/olympos-lodge-cirali/) is in Çıralı. Its website describes the lodge as open all year, but confirm your dates before you book. Our [Çıralı and Olympos guide](/blog/cirali-and-olympos-where-to-stay-eat-walk/) covers eating and walking.

## Day 2: Demre, Myra and St Nicholas

### An early extra: Limyra

Drive west through Kumluca to Finike. muze.gov.tr places [Limyra](/things-to-do/limyra-ancient-city/) 9 km east of Finike, so it makes a short first stop if you leave Çıralı early. [Arykanda](/things-to-do/arykanda-ancient-city/), which Turkish Museums lists as free, is a separate inland detour. Save it for our [Finike, Limyra and Arykanda plan](/blog/finike-limyra-arykanda-one-day/).

### Myra and St Nicholas Church

At [Myra](/things-to-do/myra-ancient-city/), Turkish Museums describes a well-preserved Roman theatre at the southern foot of the acropolis, with rock tombs, some carved with reliefs, on the slopes on both sides. The portals disagree: Turkish Museums gives 08:30 to 17:30 from 1 November to 31 March (desk 17:00), muze.gov.tr 08:00 to 17:00 (desk 16:30). The fee is €13.

Then visit [St Nicholas Church](/things-to-do/st-nicholas-church-demre/), the Byzantine church that the ministry links with the saint. Turkish Museums lists winter hours of 08:30 to 17:30 (desk 17:00), a €17 ticket and an audio guide. muze.gov.tr gives 08:30 to 19:00. ### Check first: Andriake and the Lycian Civilisations Museum

[Andriake](/things-to-do/andriake-and-the-lycian-civilisations-museum/) was Myra's harbour, on the coast at Çayağzı, and its Roman granary now holds the Lycian Civilisations Museum. The official pages don't agree on whether you can go in. Both Turkish Museums pages still say the museum and site closed on 17 February 2025 "until the works are completed", yet the ministry's 2026 night-museum list included the museum for summer evenings. The ministry's fee table gives €4 for the joint ticket but marks the museum closed. Call the Demre Museum Directorate on 0242 871 60 01 before you add it; our Andriake page sets out what each source says.

### Afternoon: Kekova, if the boats run

Turkish Museums says Simena can be reached only by sea, and calls Üçağız the first land entry point to the Kekova region. [Simena Castle in Kaleköy](/things-to-do/simena-castle-in-kalekoy/) is listed at €4, with the same winter hours as Myra. No official page we found gives a winter boat timetable. Read our [Kekova boat trips guide](/blog/kekova-boat-trips/), ask in Üçağız, and if the sea is rough, drive straight on to Kaş.

### Sleep in Kaş

[Suna Sun Hotel](/hotels/suna-sun-hotel-kas/) says it takes guests aged 14 and over and is about 4 km from the town centre. [Carruba](/hotels/carruba-boutique-hotel-kas/) and [Casa Bi](/hotels/casa-bi-kas/) are also on our [Kaş hotels list](/hotels/?area=Ka%C5%9F). For dinner, see [Naturel](/restaurants/naturel-restaurant-kas/) or [Elia](/restaurants/elia-restaurant-kas/), and the [Kaş district guide](/destinations/kas/) for the town.

## Day 3: Kaputaş, Xanthos and Patara

### Kaputaş on the way west

The metropolitan municipality puts [Kaputaş Beach](/things-to-do/kaputas-beach/) 20 km from Kaş and 7 km from Kalkan, reached by 187 steps down from the roadside. In winter it is a quick stop for the view. Swim only if the sea is calm.

### Xanthos at Kınık

The provincial culture directorate says [Xanthos](/things-to-do/xanthos-ancient-city/) stands on two hills above the plain beside the Eşen Çayı, the ancient Xanthos river, at Kınık in Kaş district. Turkish Museums calls it the administrative centre of the Lycian Union, with traces going back to the 8th century BC. Look for the Harpy Monument west of the theatre, a 4th-century BC Lycian sarcophagus and the colonnaded street. The ministry's heritage office says Xanthos and neighbouring Letoon joined the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1988. Turkish Museums gives 08:30 to 17:30 in winter (desk 17:00) and a €3 ticket.

### Extra: Letoon, over the line in Muğla

Letoon is not in Antalya province. Turkish Museums gives its address as Kumluova in Seydikemer, Muğla. The heritage office puts it 4 km from Xanthos and calls it the religious centre of Lycia, with temples to Leto, Apollo and Artemis. Turkish Museums lists €3 and 08:30 to 17:30, but its date rows are muddled (see above), so check before you go.

### Patara: the league's assembly hall

Turkish Museums places Patara at Gelemiş, in Kaş district, east of the Eşen stream, which forms the Antalya–Muğla border. Its centrepiece is the restored bouleuterion, which the ministry calls the parliament building of the Lycian League. It dates to the late 2nd or early 1st century BC, held about 1,400 people and was restored by Türkiye's Grand National Assembly. The ministry also describes a lighthouse about 500 m from the coast, built on Nero's orders in AD 64/65.

Turkish Museums shows 08:30 to 17:30 (desk 17:00), muze.gov.tr 08:00 to 19:00 (desk 18:30); both give €15. The metropolitan municipality says the beach is 12 km long. Our [Patara Ancient City](/things-to-do/patara-ancient-city/) page covers the ruins, and the [Patara beach guide](/blog/patara-beach-and-ancient-city/) explains the night rules on the sand.

### Where to end the trip

Kalkan is the closest base: [True Blue Boutique Hotel](/hotels/true-blue-boutique-hotel-kalkan/) puts Patara Beach 17 km (26 minutes) away, and [Lures Hotel](/hotels/lures-hotel-kalkan/) is on our [Kalkan hotels list](/hotels/?area=Kalkan). Flying home from Dalaman? Suna Sun Hotel puts that airport about 150 km from its hotel near Kaş. For the drive back to Antalya, see our [airport transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/).

## Tickets: single entries or the MuseumPass?

The six main sites (Phaselis, Olympos, Myra, St Nicholas, Xanthos and Patara) cost €68 in single tickets. Adding Limyra, Simena and Letoon brings it to €78.

The MuseumPass The Mediterranean costs €90 and lasts seven days, according to Turkish Museums. The ministry's list for the pass includes every ticketed site on this route in Antalya province, but not Letoon. For this road trip alone, single tickets are cheaper. The pass pays off if you add other sites from its list in the same week, such as Aspendos, Perge or Termessos. Our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) runs the numbers.

## Tips for a winter Lycian coast road trip

1. **Start early every day.** Several ticket desks close at 16:30; see our [daylight hours guide](/blog/sunrise-sunset-daylight-hours-antalya/).
2. **Check both portals the night before.** They often disagree.
3. **Keep Day 2 flexible.** Kekova depends on the boats and the sea.
4. **Pack a torch** for the Chimaera, and shoes with grip for the ruins.
5. **Want it slower?** Our [7-day Antalya itinerary](/blog/7-day-antalya-itinerary/) spreads these sites over a week; the [Antalya to Kaş day trip](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/) squeezes Demre into one day. Walkers should read the [Lycian Way guide](/blog/lycian-way-near-antalya/).

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for a Lycian coast road trip?

Three days cover the main sites from Phaselis to Patara, with nights in Çıralı and Kaş. Add a fourth for Kekova by boat, or for Limyra and Arykanda near Finike.

### Are the Lycian sites open in winter?

Yes. On 23 November 2026 no site on the main route showed a closure notice. Turkish Museums gives winter closing times of 17:00 to 17:30; muze.gov.tr shows longer hours for some. The exception is the Lycian Civilisations Museum at Andriake in Demre: official pages disagree on whether it is open, so call before you go.

### Is Letoon in Antalya?

No. Turkish Museums places Letoon in Seydikemer, Muğla, and the ministry puts it 4 km from Xanthos. It is not on the MuseumPass The Mediterranean list.

### How much do the tickets cost?

€68 for the six main sites for a foreign visitor aged 8 or over, with St Nicholas (€17) the dearest. The €90 MuseumPass The Mediterranean only pays if you add more sites that week.
