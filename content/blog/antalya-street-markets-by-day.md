Antalya's street markets are where the city does its weekly shop. Every neighbourhood has a **semt pazarı**, a market that sets up on the same street on the same day each week, selling fruit, vegetables, cheese, olives and household goods. They are not tourist bazaars, which is exactly why they are worth an hour of a holiday. This guide lists every market on the official list of **Muratpaşa**, the central district that covers Kaleiçi, Lara and most of the city east of the old town, day by day.

The list comes from Muratpaşa Municipality's market page, which we read on 22 November 2026; the site carries a 2026 footer. It names **30 markets**: 18 marked as open-air (*Açık Alan*) and 12 as covered (*Kapalı Alan*), counting one market whose label looks like a slip (see below). It gives the day and the streets for each, but **no opening hours**. For souvenirs and malls, see our separate [Antalya shopping guide](/blog/antalya-shopping/).

## How Antalya's street markets work

A semt pazarı takes over a street, a square or a market site for the day. The ones the municipality lists as covered (*kapalı*) are under a roof, which is useful on a rainy winter day. Each market is named after its neighbourhood (*mahalle*), and the address is given by the numbered streets (*sokak*) it fills.

A few things on Muratpaşa's list are worth knowing before you use it:

- **Some markets appear twice.** The Sigorta covered market runs on Wednesday and Sunday, and the Şirinyalı open-air market on Thursday and Saturday, on overlapping streets.
- **One site, two names.** The Değirmenönü market (Friday) and the Meydankavağı market (Sunday) are both listed at the corner of 1591 Sokak and 12. Cadde in Meydankavağı.
- **One odd label.** The Dedeman market is called an open market (*Açık Semt Pazarı*) but listed as covered. It is on 1480 Sokak in Yeşilbahçe, the street where, according to a municipal news item from 24 December 2021, Muratpaşa opened a covered market with a Saturday market and eight units open all week.
- **No hours.** The page doesn't say when stalls open or close. Morning gives you the widest choice.

## Which market is closest to your hotel?

Muratpaşa is big, so pick by area rather than by name:

- **Lara and Kundu.** The **Kundu Sunday market** (Güzeloba, 2369 Sokak) and the **Güzeloba covered market** on Havaalanı Caddesi (Friday) are the ones in the hotel belt east of the city; Şirinyalı has markets on Thursday and Saturday. See our [Lara & Kundu hotels](/hotels/?area=Lara%20%26%20Kundu) and the [Lara public beaches guide](/blog/lara-public-beaches/).
- **The district's namesake.** The Friday **Muratpaşa market** fills six streets in Kızılsaray, and the **Varlık** markets run on Thursday (open-air) and Sunday (the producers' market in Bahçelievler). Check the distance from your hotel on a map, then pair a morning at the market with our [Kaleiçi walking route](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/).
- **Yeşilbahçe and Meltem.** The Wednesday **Portakal Çiçeği** covered market and the Saturday **Dedeman** market are in Yeşilbahçe; Meltem's Thursday market is by Muratpaşa Lisesi on Hamidiye Caddesi.

If you're staying in Konyaaltı, that district runs its own markets. Konyaaltı Municipality's market page could not be opened on 22 November 2026, so we haven't listed them here.

## Getting there

Most markets are in residential neighbourhoods rather than near the sights, so a short taxi ride or public transport is the easy way. Antalya Ulaşım says its trams take the **AntalyaKart** or a contactless bank card; our [AntalyaKart guide](/blog/antalyakart-guide/) explains fares, top-ups and buses. Use the street numbers in the tables with a map app: the sokak numbers are how locals find them too.

## What to buy, and how

What's on the stalls changes with the season, which is the fun of it. In winter, look for the citrus that Antalya is known for; our [Finike oranges guide](/blog/finike-oranges-antalya-winter-citrus/) explains what's in season. A few tips:

- **Walk the whole market first.** Prices and quality vary from stall to stall, and the best produce goes early.
- **Buy by the kilo, or ask for less.** Produce is usually priced per kilo; you can ask for half a kilo (*yarım kilo*) or a few pieces.
- **Carry small notes in lira and a bag.** Not every stall will have change for a large note.
- **Taste before you buy cheese and olives.** It's normal to ask.
- **Keep it friendly.** This is a neighbourhood shop, not a souk; a little haggling over a large order is fine, but most prices are what they are.

For a picnic, combine market fruit, cheese and olives with bread from a bakery. Our guide to [Turkish breakfast in Antalya](/blog/turkish-breakfast-in-antalya/) explains what goes on the table, and [Antalya on a budget](/blog/antalya-on-a-budget/) has more ways to eat well for less.

## Covered markets for rainy days

Twelve of the thirty markets are listed as covered, so you can still shop on a wet winter day. Wednesday has three (Fener, Sigorta and Portakal Çiçeği), Thursday two (Sedir and Yenigün), Friday two (Güzeloba and Kızıltoprak), Saturday two (Konuksever and Dedeman) and Sunday three (Çağlayan, Sigorta and the Varlık producers' market). The Varlık market on Sunday is the only one the municipality calls a producers' market (*üretici pazarı*), so it's the one to choose if you want to buy from growers.

## FAQ

**Which day has the most markets in Antalya?**
Wednesday, with six on Muratpaşa's list. Monday has just one, in Etiler.

**What time do Antalya's markets open?**
The municipality doesn't publish hours. Go in the morning for the best choice.

**Is there a market near Lara?**
Yes. The Kundu market sets up on Sundays in Güzeloba, and the Güzeloba covered market on Fridays. Şirinyalı has markets on Thursday and Saturday.

**Are there covered markets for bad weather?**
Twelve of the thirty markets are listed as covered, with at least two every day from Wednesday to Sunday. Monday and Tuesday have only open-air markets.
