A Side day trip from Antalya packs a Roman port town into one easy outing. Walk in along the old main street, spend an hour in a museum built inside a Roman bath, eat by the harbour, then finish at the Temple of Apollo and Athena as the light goes. This guide is the timed plan: how to get there, what is open in November 2026, and what to add on the way back. For the site itself, read our [Side Ancient City](/things-to-do/side-ancient-city/) page; here we focus on the day. Hours, fees and closures come from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, Manavgat Municipality and the airport operator, all checked on 12 November 2026.

## Side day trip at a glance

- **Where it is:** Side is a neighbourhood of Manavgat district. The ministry places it about 7 km south-west of Manavgat town, on a peninsula 350–400 metres wide.
- **Distance:** Manavgat Municipality puts Side about 75 km from Antalya.
- **Ticketed sights:** the Side Museum. The theatre is also ticketed, but it is closed.
- **The temple:** the ministry publishes no separate hours or fee for the Temple of Apollo and Athena.
- **Time needed:** there is no official figure. Our plan fills about seven hours on site.
- **This month only:** night visits at the archaeological site from 9 to 20 November 2026.

## What is open in Side in November 2026

### The theatre is closed for restoration

Plan around one big gap. The Side theatre has been closed to visitors for restoration since November 2024, with no reopening date. Two ministry sources give different days for the start of the closure, so we only say November 2024. The theatre's page on muze.gov.tr marks its ticket "closed", and the ministry's list of temporarily closed sites keeps the theatre section of the Side archaeological site shut until the works are done. GoTürkiye's Side page still talks about performances in the theatre and says nothing about the closure, so go by the ministry.

### Side Museum hours and tickets

The ministry lists the museum as open every day. Its two websites both give 08:30–17:30 for the winter months, but not the same ticket office closing time:

- **muze.gov.tr:** 08:30–17:30, box office closes at 17:00. Ticket €5.
- **Turkish Museums (turkishmuseums.com):** winter (2 October–30 April) 08:30–17:30, box office 17:15; summer (1 May–1 October) 08:30–21:00, box office 20:45. Foreign adults €5, Turkish citizens 200 TL, free for foreign children up to 8.

The two muze.gov.tr pages also disagree on price. The museum's page asks €5 and says the ticket covers the theatre too. The theatre's page asks €7, marked closed, and says its ticket covers the museum. With the theatre shut, ask at the desk what your ticket includes. Prices change, so check before you go.

### Night visits, 9–20 November 2026

The Antalya culture directorate has announced night visits at the Side archaeological site from 9 to 20 November 2026, as part of the COP31 climate conference programme. Entry starts at 17:30, the ticket office closes at 19:30 and the site closes at 20:00. The notice gives a 200 TL fee for Müzekart holders with a Turkish ID number. It publishes no fee for foreign visitors and does not say which monuments are lit.

### MuseumPass

The MuseumPass The Mediterranean costs €90 and is valid for seven days from your first visit, with one entry per site. Its list names the Side Museum, the Side theatre and the "Side Apollon – Temple of Athena". The ministry says no MuseumPass is valid for night visits. For Side alone, single tickets are far cheaper; our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) shows when a pass pays off.

## Getting from Antalya to Side

### By minibus

The airport operator says Antalya's intercity bus station (Otogar) is 4 km north of the city centre, and that minibuses on the Aspendos, Side, Manavgat and Alanya routes leave from there. Follow the AntRay tram signs between the Otogar and the centre. No official source we found publishes timetables or fares for the Side minibuses, so ask at the Otogar.

### By car

Manavgat Municipality says Manavgat is 70 km east of Antalya on the Antalya–Alanya road, and Side is about 7 km from Manavgat town. No official drive time is published.

Parking needs a plan. In a news item based on a traffic decision of 25 December 2019, the municipality said the entrance to old Side is closed to all vehicles. It pointed drivers to a free 500-space car park at the Side bus station, with train-style shuttles to the peninsula. The fares and hours in that item are years old, so check them when you arrive.

### Transfer or tour

The municipality puts Manavgat 52 km from Antalya Airport. Our [airport transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/) compares taxis, hotel transfers and buses. A tour or a hired driver is the simplest way to add Aspendos or the waterfall to the same day.

## A timed walking plan for Side

The times below are our suggestion for a winter day, built around the museum's published hours. They are not official. The peninsula is small: the municipality says about 400 metres wide and 1 km long. Wear good shoes: the paving is stone, and you walk everywhere.

### 10:00 – Through the gate and down the main street

GoTürkiye says that, like other Pamphylian cities, Side was planned on a grid around a grand boulevard, which starts at the Great Gate and crosses the peninsula. Colonnaded porticoes once lined both main streets, with rows of shops behind them. GoTürkiye also notes that some of today's shops have glass floors over the ruins underneath. Look down as you walk.

### 10:45 – The theatre, from the outside

The street brings you to the theatre, south of the agora where the peninsula narrows. The ministry dates it to the 2nd century AD. The lower seats lean on the slope while the upper rows stand on vaults, a design the ministry calls rare in Anatolia. The three-storey stage building carried Antonine-period ornament, and a frieze on its first floor told the life of Dionysus from west to east. In the 3rd century a high wall turned the orchestra into an arena. In the 5th century chapels went up inside, and the theatre became an open-air church.

### 11:15 – Side Museum in the Agora Bath

The museum fills a Roman bath of the 2nd century AD, altered in the 5th and 6th centuries. It was restored in 1959–61 and opened as a museum on 14 October 1962. The ministry says it was the first museum in Türkiye set up in a village. Most finds come from the excavations led by Arif Müfid Mansel from 1947 to 1967. A 2024 renovation under the ministry's Heritage for the Future project renewed the lighting, panels and garden.

What to look for, following the culture directorate's description:

- **Hall 1:** terracotta figurines from around the 8th century BC, and a case of scales and weights that tells the story of Side's trade.
- **Hall 2:** statues of gods, friezes of sea creatures in procession, and an ancient money box holding 405 coins.
- **Hall 3:** statues, busts and sarcophagi carved with Erotes.
- **The garden:** gravestones, altars, Nike statues, game boards, milestones and inscriptions in the local Sidetic script.
- **New display:** a statue of Apollo from nearby Lyrbe, moved here from the Antalya Museum.

We would allow about an hour.

### 12:30 – Lunch near the harbour

The old town has plenty of restaurants. Two have their own pages on this site: [Side Orfoz Restaurant](/restaurants/side-orfoz-restaurant/), for seafood in the harbour area, and [Karma Side](/restaurants/karma-side/), a waterfront restaurant with seasonal menus and a lounge feel. In winter, check opening hours before you walk over.

### 14:00 – The harbour and the Temple of Apollo and Athena

Walk to the end of the colonnaded street. The ministry places the temples of Apollo and Athena here, just inside the entrance to the ancient harbour. It says they went up during the Pax Romana to honour the city's chief god and goddess. In antiquity, Side was the most important port of Pamphylia. The ministry's theatre page also tells you to look for the Medusa head carved on a temple.

### 15:00 – The beaches either side

The ministry notes sandy beaches on both sides of Side: Sorgun to the east and Ilıca to the west, along the Kumköy coast.

### Sunset – Back to the temple

The culture directorate calls sunset behind the temple columns one of the most romantic views on the Mediterranean, and the theatre page recommends it too. November sunsets come early, so check the time on the day. If you are here between 9 and 20 November, stay on for the night visit from 17:30.

## Side in a few dates

- **The name:** "pomegranate" in the ancient Luwian language, according to the ministry.
- **7th century BC:** Side becomes a settlement.
- **334 BC:** the city opens its gates to Alexander the Great without a fight.
- **5th century AD:** Side becomes the metropolis, or chief bishopric, of Pamphylia.
- **About 1150:** the population leaves, the municipality says, after centuries of Arab raids, fire and earthquakes.
- **1895–97:** Cretan migrants settle the tip of the peninsula and found the village of Selimiye. That is the municipality's date; the ministry says the early 20th century.
- **August 2026:** the ministry announced a 390-metre monumental street uncovered between the Gymnasium and the East Gate, with a Herakles Gate showing his Twelve Labours. It did not say whether visitors can walk it yet.

## FAQ

### Is a Side day trip from Antalya worth it while the theatre is closed?

Yes. The museum, the colonnaded street, the harbour and the Temple of Apollo and Athena are all still there to see. You lose the theatre's interior, but you can still see its outer walls from the street.

### Is the Side theatre open?

No. The ministry says it has been closed to visitors for restoration since November 2024, until the works are finished. No reopening date has been published.

### How do I get from Antalya to Side by public transport?

Take the AntRay tram or a city bus to Antalya's Otogar, 4 km north of the centre. The airport operator says minibuses on the Side and Manavgat route leave from there. Timetables and fares are not published, so ask at the station.

### How much does the Side Museum cost?

The ministry's museum page lists €5 for foreign visitors, and Turkish Museums lists 200 TL for Turkish citizens. The theatre's page gives €7 for a ticket that it says also covers the museum, so check at the desk.
