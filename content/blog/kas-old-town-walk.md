Most people see Kaş from a boat: Kekova, the sunken city, a swim off the stern. But the town itself is an ancient city, and a Kaş old town walk takes in more Lycian monuments than many paid sites. In an unhurried morning or afternoon you can see a 4th-century BC royal tomb on the main shopping street, a theatre that faces the sea, a rock tomb carved with dancing girls and the walls of the old harbour town. This guide uses the Antalya culture directorate's Antiphellos page, the ministry's Kaş guide and the national cultural inventory, all read on 28 November 2026.

## Antiphellos: the city under Kaş

Kaş stands on the site of **Antiphellos**, a Lycian harbour town. The culture directorate gives its Lycian name as **Habesos** (Habesa); the cultural inventory, quoting Pliny, spells it Habessos. "Antiphellos" means "the place opposite Phellos", and Phellos — "stony land" — was the main city, up on the Felen plateau about **12 km** from Kaş according to the ministry. Antiphellos was its port. The directorate says the town was settled by the 6th century BC, and its ruins run along the Çukurbağ Peninsula west of the centre.

None of the pages we used gives opening hours, fees or a ticket office for these monuments. They sit in streets and on open ground in a living town, and the inventory lists them as accessible. Treat them with care: don't climb on tombs or loose walls.

## The route at a glance

1. **Kaş harbour** — the start, with sarcophagus lids along the waterfront.
2. **Uzun Çarşı and the Lion Tomb** — up the old shopping street.
3. **The temple remains** — on Hastane Caddesi, back towards the harbour.
4. **Antiphellos Theatre** — west of the centre, facing the sea.
5. **The Doric rock tomb (Akdam)** — north-east of the theatre.
6. **The Hellenistic walls** — at the start of the peninsula, facing Meis.
7. **Optional climb:** the rock tombs on the slope above town.

As a guide to scale: by the inventory's map points, the theatre is about 700 m west of the Lion Tomb as the crow flies. The whole route is on foot, with a few short climbs.

## Stop 1: the harbour

Start on the waterfront by the harbour. The culture directorate notes Lycian sarcophagi around the harbour and inside the line of the old walls. Look for the heavy, pointed lids typical of Lycian sarcophagi. Our guide to [Lycian rock tombs](/blog/lycian-rock-tombs/) explains the different tomb types you'll meet on this walk.

## Stop 2: the Lion Tomb on Uzun Çarşı

Walk up **Uzun Çarşı**, the old shopping street that climbs from the harbour. On it stands the town's best-known monument, the **Lion Tomb** (Aslanlı Lahit), also called the King's Tomb (Kral Mezarı). The directorate dates it to the 4th century BC, and the inventory notes that it now stands in Kaş's busy centre.

The directorate describes a single-block sarcophagus with lion heads projecting from the corners of the lid. The cultural inventory adds the detail: a classic Lycian sarcophagus raised on a tall base (a hyposorion), with a chest carved to imitate timber and a rounded lid. Look for the **Lycian inscription**: the directorate counts eight lines, the inventory nine, written in the Lycian B dialect. Both agree it has never been fully read.

## Stop 3: the temple remains

Head back down towards the harbour. The ministry's Kaş guide notes a **temple on Hastane Caddesi**, built of neatly cut stone on Roman-period foundations. The directorate describes an unidentified temple where the walls approach the harbour, south-west of a church that was later turned into a mosque. The two pages may mean the same building, but neither says so.

## Stop 4: Antiphellos Theatre

Follow the road west out of the centre towards the peninsula. The **[Antiphellos Ancient Theatre](/things-to-do/antiphellos-ancient-theatre/)** sits on a rise with its seats facing the water and the Greek island of Meis (Kastellorizo).

The directorate counts **26 rows** of seats and notes there is **no stage building**, which is why the view is so open; it calls the theatre the only sea-facing example of its kind in Anatolia. The ministry's guide gives a capacity of **about 4,000** and dates it to the 1st century BC, with repairs in the 2nd century AD. The inventory says the orchestra and seating are very well preserved.

This is the place to come back to at sunset.

## Stop 5: the Doric rock tomb (Akdam)

From the theatre, look **north-east** up the slope. The ministry describes a Doric-style, house-type tomb cut from the natural rock, called **Akdam** locally, about **3.5 m** tall and dated to the 4th century BC. Inside, a frieze shows **24 young women dancing hand in hand**.

## Stop 6: the Hellenistic walls

The directorate places stretches of **Hellenistic wall** at the point where the peninsula begins, on the side facing Meis. The inventory mentions the sea walls in part still visible and an acropolis overlooking the island. Walk out along the peninsula road for the view back over the bay.

## Optional: the rock tombs above town

The directorate and the ministry both mention **rock tombs cut into the hillside** behind and north of the town. You can see them from the harbour; reaching them means a climb on rough ground, best done in cool weather with proper shoes.

## Beyond the town: Phellos

If the walk leaves you wanting more, the mother city is within reach by car. The ministry's Kaş guide puts **Phellos**, on the Felen plateau, about 12 km from Kaş and says it was the more important of the two cities in the 4th century BC. It mentions a sarcophagus decorated with reliefs there. No official page gives directions, hours or facilities, so go with a local driver or guide, and take water.

## Practical tips

- **When to go:** mornings for the Lion Tomb and Uzun Çarşı before the shops get busy; late afternoon for the theatre and the light on Meis.
- **What to wear:** grippy shoes — the theatre's seats are polished smooth — and a hat in summer, as there is little shade.
- **Winter:** Kaş is quieter out of season, which suits this walk; just check that the cafés you plan to stop at are open.
- **Getting here:** coming from Antalya for the day? Our [Antalya to Kaş day trip](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/) covers the coast road. From Kalkan, Kaş is a short drive east past [Kaputaş Beach](/things-to-do/kaputas-beach/).

## FAQ

### Is there an entrance fee for the Antiphellos theatre?

None of the official or inventory pages we checked mentions a fee, ticket office or opening hours. The theatre is on open ground at the edge of town.

### Where exactly is the Lion Tomb?

On Uzun Çarşı Caddesi, the old shopping street that climbs from the harbour, in the busy centre of town.

### How long does the Kaş old town walk take?

Allow a relaxed half-day with stops, or longer if you climb to the rock tombs. The distances are short; the heat and the hills set the pace.

### Can I see the dancing girls in the Akdam tomb?

The ministry describes the frieze inside the tomb, but no official page says whether the interior is open. If it is closed, the carved façade is still worth the short climb from the theatre.
