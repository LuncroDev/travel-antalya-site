Summer gets the headlines in Antalya, but winter is when the province's oranges come into their own. From December to February the Finike orange, a protected local variety, is at its best, and the roadside stalls and neighbourhood markets fill with fruit. This guide explains what is special about Antalya's citrus, when the season peaks, and how to taste it on a winter visit.

## Why oranges matter in Antalya

Orange is one of Antalya's most important agricultural products and, in the words of the official GoTürkiye tourism site, one of the region's natural symbols. GoTürkiye says about 23% of Türkiye's orange production comes from Antalya. Among the varieties grown here, Finike oranges are the best known, and they have been registered as a Geographical Indication (GI) product.

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism's page on Finike says the district is known for the ruins of Limyra and Arykanda and is "also famous for its oranges". The town of Finike lies west of Antalya on the Lycian coast; see our [Finike district guide](/destinations/finike/).

## What makes a Finike orange different

GoTürkiye describes Finike oranges as seedless, juicy and sweet, grown on a fertile plain of alluvial soil with the unique local weather. A reference article on the GI registration identifies the fruit as the Washington Navel variety grown in the Finike region, and says it reaches its best condition for eating and export in **December, January and February**. GoTürkiye gives the same window: ready from December until February.

Even so, the harvest starts earlier. In mid-November 2025, a trade-news item reported that the season's first harvest had begun in Finike with about 250,000 tonnes expected for the year, sold both at home and abroad. That is last year's figure and the 2026 numbers may differ, but it shows how the season opens before the winter peak.

## What it costs (and why we don't promise prices)

A USDA attaché report published through India's APEDA gave average Turkish retail prices as of December 2025 of 40–55 TL per kg for a Washington orange and 10–40 TL per kg for a juicing orange. Prices change with the harvest and inflation, so treat that as a snapshot, not a budget. In a market, ask the price per kilo before you fill a bag.

## Where to taste Antalya's winter citrus

- **Markets and roadside stalls.** Fruit stalls and the city's weekly neighbourhood markets sell oranges, mandarins and lemons in season. We have not verified specific market days, so ask your hotel which district market is on.
- **Breakfast tables.** Citrus jam is part of Antalya's food culture, according to GoTürkiye. Our [Turkish breakfast in Antalya](/blog/turkish-breakfast-in-antalya/) guide covers where to find it.
- **Local dishes.** For the savoury side of the region, see [what food is Antalya known for?](/blog/what-food-is-antalya-known-for-local-dishes-to-try/) and the [Antalya piyaz guide](/blog/antalya-piyaz-tahini-bean-dish/).
- **Pomegranate on 21 December.** GoTürkiye notes the tradition of *nar kırma*, breaking open a pomegranate, on 21 December, the start of winter, as a symbol of abundance.

## A winter day trip to Finike

Finike pairs oranges with ruins. Our [Finike, Limyra and Arykanda day plan](/blog/finike-limyra-arykanda-one-day/) is a full day with winter opening hours and fees, and you can read about each site on its own page:

1. [Limyra](/things-to-do/limyra-ancient-city/), the Lycian city known for rock tombs.
2. [Arykanda](/things-to-do/arykanda-ancient-city/), a hillside site of terraces above the valley.
3. A harbour walk and lunch in Finike. Seafood and citrus desserts are typical, but we have not verified individual restaurants there.

Finike hosts orange-themed celebrations and festivals each year, according to GoTürkiye. We could not find a confirmed 2026–27 date, so do not plan a trip around one without checking with the municipality.

## Tips for visitors

1. **Buy for eating, not just juicing.** Ask the stallholder which is which.
2. **Wash and peel.** Fruit from a roadside stall is not pre-washed.
3. **Check your airline's rules.** If you want to take fruit home, check customs and agricultural rules for your destination.
4. **Come hungry for the whole winter table.** Our [Antalya in winter](/blog/antalya-in-winter/) guide explains what else is on.

## FAQ

### When is orange season in Antalya?

The harvest starts in autumn and the Finike orange is best from December to February.

### What is a Finike orange?

A seedless, sweet Washington Navel orange from the Finike plain with Geographical Indication status.

### Is there an orange festival in Finike?

GoTürkiye says festivals are held each year. We have not found 2026–27 dates.
