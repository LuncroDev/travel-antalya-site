Alanya in a day is very doable if you base the day on the castle peninsula. Everything worth seeing sits around one rocky headland: the castle on top, the museum and Damlataş Cave at its western foot, Cleopatra Beach beyond, and the harbour with the Red Tower on the eastern side. This plan strings them together in a loop, with timings that work in winter as well as summer. Facts come from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, its Turkish Museums portal, Alanya Municipality and the Ministry of Transport, all checked on 15 November 2026.

## Alanya in a day at a glance

- **Alanya Castle:** €12 for foreign adults. Winter hours vary by source (see below), so be at the gate by 16:00.
- **Alanya Museum:** €4. In winter, 08:30–17:30.
- **Cable car to the castle:** runs from the Damlataş area. No current official timetable or fare is published.
- **Damlataş Cave:** at the Cleopatra Beach end of town. No official hours or fee are published.
- **Red Tower and shipyard:** at the harbour. No separate official hours found.
- **Time needed:** the plan below runs from 09:00 to about 17:30, lunch included.
- **Getting there:** the Ministry of Transport puts Gazipaşa–Alanya Airport 40 km from Alanya. From Antalya, see our [airport transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/).

## Before you start

Alanya is a long way from Antalya for a day trip. If you are staying in Antalya or Belek, leave early, or break the drive with [Side](/blog/side-day-trip-from-antalya/) on the way back. Better still, sleep in Alanya the night before: browse our [Alanya hotels](/hotels/?area=Alanya), such as [Alaiye Resort](/hotels/alaiye-resort/), and the [Alanya district guide](/destinations/alanya/).

Wear proper shoes. The castle paths are steep, and the ministry's own leaflet warns that walking the full 6.5 km of walls can be tiring.

## 09:00 – Up to the castle by cable car

Start at the bottom station in the Damlataş area. Alanya Municipality opened the cable car on 11 October 2017: a 900 m line from the Damlataş social facilities to the castle's Ehmedek Gate, with 17 cabins carrying up to 1,130 people an hour. At the opening it announced half price for students, over-65s and groups of more than 20, but it has published no current fare or timetable. If it isn't running, a road climbs the hill; take a taxi up and walk down.

## 09:30 – Alanya Castle

From Ehmedek, walk up to the inner citadel (İç Kale). Our [Alanya Castle](/things-to-do/alanya-castle/) page covers the history in detail. The highlights for a short visit:

- **The walls:** 6.5 km of them, mostly Seljuk, ringing the whole headland.
- **The cisterns:** the ministry counts up to 400, which held the castle's water.
- **The views:** the ministry singles out the view down over Cleopatra Beach.
- **The lighthouse:** dated 1720, it guided ships for many years.

**Tickets and hours.** Turkish Museums gives the fee as €12 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over (200 TL for Turkish citizens). For winter (2 October to 30 April) it lists 08:00–18:00 on weekdays and 08:00–17:00 at weekends, with the ticket desk closing at 17:00. The ministry's leaflet gives 08:00–17:00 from 1 October to 1 April, with the desk closing at 16:30, and 08:30–19:00 from April to October. The safe rule in winter is to buy your ticket before 16:30. Turkish Museums lists a café, a shop and a car park.

## 11:30 – Down to the Alanya Museum

Walk or ride down to the [Alanya Museum](/things-to-do/alanya-museum/), whose entrance is on Damlataş Caddesi where the castle road starts. An hour is enough. The star is a 52 cm bronze Heracles in a hall of its own; the ministry's leaflet dates it to the 2nd century AD. There is also a hall of finds from the castle excavations, including a Seljuk palace tile with the titles of Sultan Alaeddin Keykubad, and a hall of objects from the sea.

**Tickets and hours.** €4 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over. Both ministry sources give winter hours of 08:30–17:30; the leaflet has the desk closing at 17:00. The museum is on the MuseumPass The Mediterranean list, but the castle and museum tickets together come to €16; our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) shows when the pass pays off.

## 12:30 – Lunch

Two options with castle views:

- **On the castle road:** [Kale Panorama Restaurant](/restaurants/kale-panorama-restaurant/), if you would rather eat before coming down.
- **On the harbour street:** [Güverte](/restaurants/guverte-restaurant-alanya/), on İskele Caddesi, faces the castle and the Red Tower. Its site does not publish opening hours, so call ahead.

If you eat at the harbour now, swap the afternoon around and do the Red Tower before the beach. [Ravza](/restaurants/ravza-restaurant/), in town, is another choice.

## 14:00 – Damlataş Cave and Cleopatra Beach

Back on the west side of the headland, **Damlataş Cave** sits at the end of the beach. Alanya Municipality says it was found in 1948, when a quarry was opened to cut stone for the harbour. A 50 m passage, up to 15 m high, leads inside, and the air stays at about 22°C and 95% humidity all year. The municipality says the air is known for helping asthma sufferers, and that some patients follow a 21-day course of sitting in the cave under a doctor's supervision. Neither the municipality's cave page nor its list of historic sites gives opening hours or a fee, so check at the entrance.

Then walk onto **[Cleopatra Beach](/things-to-do/cleopatra-beach/)**, the long town beach on the west side of the peninsula. In summer and early autumn this is your swim; in winter it's a walk along the sand with the castle above you. Our [best beaches in Antalya](/blog/best-beaches-in-antalya/) guide compares it with the other beaches along the coast, and [can you swim in Antalya in winter?](/blog/can-you-swim-in-antalya-in-winter-weather-sea-temperature-guide/) covers the sea in the cooler months.

## 16:00 – The Red Tower and the shipyard

Cross to the eastern side of the headland and the harbour. Two Seljuk monuments stand by the water:

- **The Red Tower (Kızılkule).** The ministry's leaflet says Alaeddin Keykubad had it built in 1226: an octagonal tower 33 m high, with an ethnography museum on its lower level.
- **The shipyard (Tersane).** Built by the same sultan beside the Red Tower (the municipality gives 1228), it was used to build and repair ships and make sails until the 1960s. The ministry calls it the only surviving shipyard of its type from the period, and it is now a ship and maritime museum.

We found no separate official hours or fees for either, so ask at the castle ticket desk in the morning what your ticket covers and when they close.

## 17:30 – Sunset on the harbour

Finish on İskele Caddesi, looking back up at the walls you climbed in the morning. In winter the light goes early, so the harbour walk becomes your evening stroll; in summer you can swap the order and swim later.

## Summer or winter?

Winter is cooler for the walking part. The catch is shorter opening hours, so keep the castle in the morning. In summer, reverse the plan: castle at opening time, museum in the heat of the day, beach in the late afternoon. ## FAQ

### Can you see Alanya in a day?

Yes, if you stay close to the castle peninsula. The castle, museum, cave, beach and harbour are all around the same headland, and the cable car saves the steep climb.

### How much does Alanya Castle cost?

Turkish Museums lists €12 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over and 200 TL for Turkish citizens, as of 15 November 2026. The Alanya Museum is a separate €4 ticket.

### Is there a cable car up to Alanya Castle?

Yes. It runs 900 m from the Damlataş area to the castle's Ehmedek Gate and opened in 2017. Alanya Municipality publishes no current fare or timetable.

### Is the Alanya Museum worth visiting?

For an hour, yes, above all for the bronze Heracles and the finds from the castle. It costs €4 and is at the foot of the castle road, so it adds little time to the day.
