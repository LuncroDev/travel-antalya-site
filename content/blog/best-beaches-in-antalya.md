The best beaches in Antalya are spread along the whole province, from city pebbles under the Beydağları mountains to Lycian coves you reach down a cliff staircase. Antalya does not have one "best" beach. Each stretch suits a different trip. This guide goes district by district and says what each beach is like underfoot, how you get in, and what to check before you go.

A note on sources: lengths, Blue Flag status and entry rules below come from Antalya Metropolitan Municipality's beach guide, the Blue Flag programme run by TÜRÇEV, and the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's site pages, all checked on 9 November 2026. Where two official sources disagree, we say so. If you are still deciding whether Antalya suits a beach holiday at all, start with our overview, [Is Antalya good for a beach holiday?](/blog/is-antalya-good-for-a-beach-holiday-best-beaches-explained/)

## Quick picks

- **For a city break:** Konyaaltı, with its long pebble shore and mountain view.
- **For soft sand near the city:** Lara.
- **For the photo:** Kaputaş, between Kaş and Kalkan.
- **For space and dunes:** Patara.
- **For swimming beside ruins:** Phaselis and Olympos.
- **For sand in town:** Cleopatra Beach in Alanya.

## Antalya city beaches

### Konyaaltı Beach

[Konyaaltı Beach](/things-to-do/konyaalti-beach/) is the city's long west beach. The municipality describes about 7 km of shore, mostly fine pebble with some fine sand, split into more than 30 sections run by different operators. That means you can choose between a free stretch with your own towel and a section with sunbeds, showers and a café. One section, Sahil Antalya Yaşam Parkı 5, is listed by TÜRÇEV as a 2026 Blue Flag beach. Its page says it is 4.5 km long and 3 km from the centre. Expect pebbles, so water shoes help. Stay on the [Konyaaltı side](/hotels/?area=Konyaalt%C4%B1) if you want to walk to the water.

### Lara Beach

East of the city centre, Lara is the sandy option. TÜRÇEV lists "Lara Halk Plajı 1" as a sand beach about 15 km from the centre. It has held the Blue Flag from 2011 to 2017 and again from 2019 to 2026, with no entrance fee for day use. Further east the shore is lined with resorts whose beach sections serve their own guests, so a day visitor should head for the public beach. See the [Lara hotels](/hotels/?area=Lara%20%26%20Kundu) if you want sand on your doorstep.

### Mermerli Beach

Mermerli is the small beach tucked below the old town, at the Kaleiçi yacht harbour, with the Beydağları across the bay. Habertürk reported that TÜRÇEV inspected Mermerli and handed over its Blue Flag in May 2026. It is a small cove rather than a long strand. Access and pricing have changed in recent seasons: in 2025 an operator's entry charge drew complaints and the beach closed for a time. Check the current entry arrangements on the day. The [Mermerli Restaurant](/restaurants/mermerli-restaurant/) page and our [Kaleiçi Old Town guide](/things-to-do/kaleici-old-town/) help you plan the walk down.

## Kemer coast: town beaches, ruins and turtle beaches

### Kemer town

Kemer is the resort town 44 km south-west of Antalya on the highways directorate's distance table. The municipality highlights Ayışığı (Moonlight) beach, about 300 m of sand in a park of pines and palms. TÜRÇEV lists the Kemer municipal public beach as a pebble beach with Blue Flag status from 2021 to 2026. GoTürkiye notes that the district mixes pebble and sandy shores, many with the Blue Flag. Browse [Kemer hotels](/hotels/?area=Kemer) for a base.

### Phaselis

[Phaselis Ancient City](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) lies near Tekirova, about 12 km from Kemer according to the municipality. The site had three ancient harbours, and their bays are where people swim today, under pines and next to Roman stonework. Entry is through the archaeological site. When we checked, the ministry listed it as open daily 08:00–17:00 at €10. The municipality notes that a nearby bay, Bostanlık Koyu, closes in season for turtle nesting.

### Çıralı and Olympos

Çıralı is a long, low-key village beach. The municipality gives its length as 3.5 km, with fine gravel at the waterline and a sand strip 50–100 m wide behind it, 28 km from Kemer. The city's tourism portal describes it as a first-degree protected area where turtle nests are fenced and there is no night lighting. The ruins of Olympos sit at the beach's southern end. The ministry lists Olympos at €10 and 08:00–19:00, and notes that its coast is a loggerhead turtle nesting area. Swimmers coming from the Olympos side enter through the site. Pair a beach day here with the [Chimaera flames](/things-to-do/olympos-and-the-chimaera-flames/) after dark.

### Adrasan

Further south in Kumluca district, Adrasan is a bay with about 2 km of sandy shore on the Lycian Way, per the municipality, with boat tours and diving. It is a long way from the airport, so it works best with an overnight stay.

## Kaş: Kaputaş and Patara

### Kaputaş Beach

Kaputaş is the turquoise cove you have seen in photos, at the foot of a gorge between Kaş and Kalkan. The municipality says you reach it by 187 steps from the road, and puts it about 20 km from Kaş and 7 km from Kalkan. GoTürkiye says the drive from Kaş takes about 20 minutes, and that you can also come by local bus or taxi. The beach is small, so arrive early in summer. Our longer [Kaputaş Beach guide](/blog/kaputas-beach-travel-antalya/) covers parking and timing, and the [Antalya to Kaş day trip](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/) shows how to fit it into a coastal drive.

### Patara Beach

Patara is the opposite of Kaputaş: huge, open and wild. The environment ministry's page for the Patara Special Environmental Protection Area describes an 18 km dune beach up to 500 m wide. The municipality's guide gives 12 km, so the length depends on where you measure. Either way, there is room. It is a first-degree nesting beach for loggerhead and green turtles. Patara has one practical catch: the road to the beach runs through the archaeological site, so you pay the site entry even if you only want to swim. The ministry listed it at €15 when we checked. The municipality puts Patara about 42 km from Kaş and points to nearby Gelemiş village for places to stay.

## Side and Belek

### Side

Side's beaches wrap around the ancient town. TÜRÇEV lists Nar Beach 1, on the Side peninsula, as a sand-and-pebble Blue Flag beach from 2022 to 2026. Manavgat municipality says five of its eight public beaches hold the Blue Flag. Combine a swim with the temple and theatre of [Side Ancient City](/things-to-do/side-ancient-city/), and see [Side hotels](/hotels/?area=Side) for beachfront stays.

### Belek

Belek's long sandy coast is mostly lined by resort beaches. It is also a protected nesting coast: a ministry document on the Belek Special Environmental Protection Area, declared in 1990, describes about 13.3 km of first-degree loggerhead nesting beach. If you stay at one of the [Belek resorts](/hotels/?area=Belek), follow the hotel's rules on sunbed placement and night lighting in nesting season.

## Alanya: Cleopatra Beach

Cleopatra Beach runs west of Alanya's castle peninsula, beside Damlataş Cave. The municipality describes about 2 km of fine golden sand. TÜRÇEV lists "Kleopatra Batı Halk Plajı" as a sand beach about 1 km from the centre, with the Blue Flag every year from 1999 to 2026. You can walk there from the town centre. See our [Alanya district guide](/destinations/alanya/) and [Alanya hotels](/hotels/?area=Alanya).

## Blue Flag beaches in Antalya

The Blue Flag is an international award for water quality, safety and services, run in Türkiye by TÜRÇEV. Habertürk reported in May 2026 that Antalya has 232 Blue Flag beaches, more than any other city in the world, according to TÜRÇEV's Antalya coordinator. The flag is awarded to a named section of beach, not to the whole coast. Two neighbouring stretches of the same beach can differ, as at Cleopatra Beach, where we could confirm 2026 status for the west public beach only. If the flag matters to you, look up the exact section on TÜRÇEV's site.

## Sea turtles and night closures

Several of Antalya's sandy beaches are important nesting grounds for loggerhead turtles (*Caretta caretta*). News reports on the 2026 rules say 21 nesting beaches in Türkiye close to the public from 20:00 to 08:00 between 1 May and the end of September, with vehicles, tents and fires banned. Antalya beaches on that list include Patara, Çıralı–Olympos, Belek, Tekirova, Kızılot and Gazipaşa. Fines are steep. In practice: don't stay on the beach after dark in season, don't put umbrellas or sunbeds near marked nests, and never shine lights at hatchlings.

## Practical tips for Antalya beaches

- **Pebble or sand:** Konyaaltı, Kemer town and Mermerli are mostly pebble, so bring water shoes. Lara, Patara, Cleopatra and the Belek resort beaches are sandy.
- **Entry:** Most public beaches are free, and you pay only for sunbeds. Patara, Olympos and Phaselis charge site entry, which you'll need to pay even just to swim.
- **Season:** The main swimming season runs from late spring into autumn. Our [Antalya weather by month](/blog/antalya-weather-by-months-what-to-expect-before-you-visit/) guide helps you pick a date, and [Antalya in winter](/blog/antalya-in-winter/) covers the off-season.
- **Shade:** Phaselis has pine shade next to the water. On most city beaches you rent an umbrella.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is the best beach in Antalya city?

Konyaaltı is the most convenient: long, central and lined with cafés, but pebbly. If you want sand, take the short trip east to Lara's public beach.

### Are Antalya's beaches free?

Most public beaches have no entrance fee. You pay for sunbeds and umbrellas if you want them. Beaches inside archaeological sites, such as Patara, Olympos and Phaselis, require a site ticket.

### Where can I find a sandy beach near Antalya?

Lara is the nearest sandy beach to the city. Further afield, Patara, Cleopatra Beach in Alanya and the Belek coast are long and sandy.

### Can I visit turtle beaches at night?

Not in the nesting season. News reports of the 2026 rules say nesting beaches such as Patara and Çıralı close from 20:00 to 08:00 between 1 May and the end of September.
