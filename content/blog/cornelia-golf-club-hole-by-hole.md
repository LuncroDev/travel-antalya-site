This Cornelia Golf Club hole-by-hole guide walks you round all 27 holes of Sir Nick Faldo's course in Belek, using the club's own "Tips from Faldo" page, its course and price pages, all read on 27 November 2026. Cornelia plays its 27 holes as three overlapping 18-hole loops, named King, Queen and Prince. Unlike some Belek clubs, Cornelia does not publish a machine-readable scorecard: its 2025 scorecard and slope-rating sheets are image files. So this guide gives the par only where the club's own text states it, and Faldo's advice for every hole.

## Cornelia Golf Club at a glance

The club's About Course page lists three 18-hole combinations, all par 72:

| Course | Holes | Length | Par |
|---|---|---|---|
| King | 1–18 | 6,373 m | 72 |
| Queen | 10–27 | 6,411 m | 72 |
| Prince | 19–27, then 1–9 | 6,324 m | 72 |

In other words, there are three nines (1–9, 10–18 and 19–27), and each "course" joins two of them. The Queen is the longest by a few dozen metres; the Prince the shortest. The handicap limit is **28 for men and 36 for women**, and the club asks for a handicap certificate.

The club's technical-details page describes Bermuda grass overseeded in winter, **75 bunkers** and greens averaging about 600 m². Hole 7, *Faldo's Choice*, is the signature hole. Cornelia overseeds nine holes at a time, so 18 holes stay open all year, but the About Course page still shows 2024 dates for the closed loop. ## Holes 1–9: the opening nine (King and Prince)

These holes open the King and close the Prince.

- **1 – Taurus Mountains.** A relatively short opener. Keep the drive down the middle, because waste areas line both sides, and take enough club for a heavily contoured green.
- **2 – Corridor.** Long and narrow. Pick your landing spots, keep it straight and stay out of the deep greenside bunker.
- **3 – Jaws.** A short, deceptive uphill **par 3**. Club choice is everything: the elevated green is undulating, and anything short falls into the "jaws" below.
- **4 – Fox Hole.** There is more room than it looks from the tee, but don't drift right: the fairway falls away there and trees block the approach to a semi-blind green.
- **5 – Wall.** An average-length **par 5** with the green hanging over a lake. The tee shot wants to move right to left; only a drive to the higher fairway gives you a chance of reaching in two.
- **6 – Plateau.** A target **par 3** to a high plateau with steep slopes, usually played across the wind.
- **7 – Faldo's Choice.** The signature hole. Faldo offers two routes: play safe to the higher ground or take on the green. Either way, be accurate, because the green slopes towards three deep bunkers.
- **8 – Twins.** The bold line threads between two pines and two lakeside bunkers and leaves a short pitch. The safe line down the centre leaves a longer approach over a cavernous bunker.
- **9 – Straight Home.** Four fairway bunkers wait for the drive; the right side opens the view into a heavily bunkered green.

## Holes 10–18: the middle nine (King and Queen)

This nine is shared by the King and the Queen, so you play it on either.

- **10 – Waste.** A gentle left-to-right drive, but don't overdo it: waste ground fills the inside of the dogleg and a big pine guards a narrow green.
- **11 – The Long.** A true **par 5** and a very long one. Turn the drive right round the corner, then keep your second clear of the lake on the right and the mounds and bunkers around the green.
- **12 – The Dip.** The wind decides whether the carry over the dip plays short or long. Choose the club carefully and aim for the middle of the green.
- **13 – Deception.** Play down the left side to open up the green; drift right and the fairway bunkers come into play. Faldo also advises checking the yardage.
- **14 – The Swing.** A long, downhill **par 3** towards a lake. Swing it left to right: short misses run into waste ground and long ones end up wet.
- **15 – The Glen.** It looks wide open for a big drive, but a carry over waste, deep bunkers and a narrow, undulating green make it tougher than it seems.
- **16 – Pond.** An average-length but narrow **par 5**. Shape the drive right to left past three fairway bunkers. You can reach in two, but a pond on the left and a lake behind protect the green.
- **17 – Front to Back.** A **par 4** that may be short in length, but the green is narrow, raised and slopes from front to back. Find the fairway first.
- **18 – Mountain Peak.** The longest of the **par 4s**. Drive long and straight at the mountain peak, then curve the approach right to left round the pine in the middle of the fairway.

## Holes 19–27: the third nine (Queen and Prince)

This nine closes the Queen and opens the Prince.

- **19 – The Black Hill.** Drive down the right to the higher ground; the green sits on top of the hill, and from the left you face a blind pitch over a bunker.
- **20 – Eucalyptus.** From an elevated tee, turn the drive right to left round the corner. The green sits on a ridge, with a lake to the right and a pot bunker to the left.
- **21 – The Pots.** A short **par 3** with four deep pot bunkers in front and one behind. Hit the contoured green or pay for it.
- **22 – 3 Sisters.** Drive past the tall pines and fairway bunkers to leave a mid-iron, and beware the "three sisters" waiting short of the green.
- **23 – Split.** A **par 5** whose fairway is split by a belt of vegetation. Drive between two tall pines, then lay up short of the bunkers on your line of sight.
- **24 – Aquarius.** The only forced carry over water on the course, according to Faldo. The right of the two pines is the safer line; the left gives a better angle. Don't go long.
- **25 – Step.** Shape the tee shot right to left from the trees. The green has a steep step, so find the right level or risk three putts.
- **26 – A Tight Squeeze.** A narrow fairway through the trees. Drive as close as you dare to the two fairway bunkers, and think about simply playing for the green in regulation.
- **27 – Lakes.** Keep the drive central, allow for the crosswinds, aim at the old pine on the slope and carry the corner of the lake to finish.

## Which Cornelia course should you play?

You rarely get to choose freely: the club assigns the open loop, especially in the overseeding months. If you can:

- **King (1–18)** gets you the signature 7th, the long 11th and the closing Mountain Peak.
- **Queen (10–27)** is the longest loop and finishes with the forced carry at 24 and the lake at 27.
- **Prince (19–27, 1–9)** is the shortest and ends with the Straight Home 9th.

## Green fees, buggies and lessons for 2026–27

Cornelia's price page sets three price levels (A, B and C) across date bands from 1 September 2026 to the end of 2027; on the page we read, the bands and columns did not line up clearly, so confirm which applies to your dates. In euros:

- **18 holes:** €190 / €150 / €120

The technical page lists a double-ended driving range, two putting greens, three chipping and pitching greens and group practice areas. The clubhouse has a bar, a restaurant, a pro shop, lockers and bag storage, with free water at the starter and a shuttle from the Cornelia hotels. ## FAQ

### How many holes does Cornelia Golf Club have?

Twenty-seven, designed by Sir Nick Faldo. They are played as three 18-hole courses — King (1–18), Queen (10–27) and Prince (19–27 plus 1–9) — each par 72 and between 6,324 m and 6,411 m long.

### What is the signature hole at Cornelia?

Hole 7, *Faldo's Choice*. You can play safe to the higher ground or go for the green, which slopes towards three deep bunkers.

### What handicap do you need to play Cornelia?

The club lists a maximum of 28 for men and 36 for women and asks for a handicap certificate. Our [Belek handicap and dress-code guide](/blog/belek-golf-handicap-limits-and-dress-codes/) compares other courses.

### How much is a round at Cornelia in 2026–27?

The club's price page shows 18 holes at €120, €150 or €190 depending on the season, plus €50 for a golf car. Check the date band for your trip on the club's page.
