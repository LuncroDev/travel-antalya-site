A Manavgat river boat trip is the easy way to follow the Manavgat River to its end. You board in the town centre and ride downstream to Boğaz, where the river meets the Mediterranean. Add the waterfall upstream in the morning and Titreyengöl lake afterwards, and you have a gentle half day east of Antalya. This guide is the timed plan. The river boats are private businesses, and no official page publishes their prices or timetables, so we list what to ask instead. Facts come from Manavgat Municipality, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, Antalya Metropolitan Municipality and GoTürkiye, all checked on 14 November 2026. For the falls on their own, see our [Manavgat Waterfall](/things-to-do/manavgat-waterfall/) page.

## Manavgat river boat trip at a glance

- **Where:** Manavgat town. The municipality puts it 70 km east of Antalya on the Antalya–Alanya road, and 52 km from Antalya Airport.
- **The boat leg:** about 8.5 km by river from the town centre to the Boğaz area, by the municipality's count.
- **The waterfall:** a few kilometres north of the centre, with a drop of about 4 metres.
- **Boat prices and departure times:** not published by any official source we found.
- **Waterfall tickets and hours:** not published either. Details below.
- **Time needed:** no official figure. Our plan takes about five hours.

## The river in brief

The ministry's history of the district says the river was called the Melas in antiquity and marked a border of ancient Pamphylia. The official length figures do not match. That ministry text gives 78 km for the whole river. GoTürkiye says it flows south for about 90 km before it even reaches the waterfall. GoTürkiye also names two dams on the river, Oymapınar and Manavgat, and gives an average flow of 147 cubic metres a second, with a maximum of 500.

Below the falls, the river runs through the middle of town and on to the sea. GoTürkiye puts the town centre about 3.5 km from the coast, while the municipality counts 8.5 km along the river. The metropolitan municipality's waterfall booklet mentions trout, ducks and geese, with plane trees along the banks. GoTürkiye adds carp and freshwater crayfish.

## A timed half-day plan

The times below are our suggestion, not an official schedule. They assume you start in Manavgat with a car or taxi. Before you set off, check that boats are running on the day (see the boat section below). In winter, do not take that for granted.

### 09:00 – Manavgat Waterfall

Start upstream at the falls. They are low but wide. The ministry's district history describes the whole river dropping over a ledge 3 to 4 metres high, and GoTürkiye and the metropolitan municipality both say about 4 metres. The metropolitan municipality's booklet says Manavgat Municipality has laid out picnic, shopping, dining and resting areas around the falls, with a viewing terrace. The ministry and GoTürkiye both mention fish restaurants nearby. We would allow an hour.

### 10:15 – Back into town and down to the riverbank

Head back to the centre. The municipality's notices for its river bus name two boarding points in town: under the Historic Bridge (Tarihi Köprü) and at the municipal stops by Zindan Kalesi. Use them as landmarks when you look for boats. Commercial boats may leave from other spots, so ask.

### 10:45 – Downstream to Boğaz

This is the boat ride itself, about 8.5 km on the water. Before you pay, ask whether the boat stops at the river mouth, for how long, and whether it brings you back or drops you there. If the boat does not stop, you can still reach Boğaz by road afterwards.

### 12:00 – Boğaz, where the river meets the sea

The municipality counts the river mouth among the district's important places for wildlife. It also says it is one of the spots in Antalya where the most bird species are observed, so bring binoculars. The municipality says it cleans the Boğaz beach more often in the summer season, and warns that dropping litter or cigarette butts there can bring an administrative fine. The culture directorate lists Manavgat's beaches as first-degree sea turtle nesting areas. Keep away from any marked nests.

### 13:00 – Titreyengöl and the Sorgun forest

Titreyengöl means "trembling lake". The municipality describes it as a natural lake in the Sorgun neighbourhood, inside Sorgun Forest, which is protected as a first-degree natural site. The municipality has held dragon-boat canoe races on the lake, including three days in April 2015, together with a local tourism investors' association, and guests from the area's hotels joined in. From Boğaz, a taxi is the simplest way over. Take your time at the lake, then have lunch nearby or back in town. The culture directorate also calls Sorgun beach one of Antalya's finest.

### On a Monday: add the market

Manavgat Municipality says its Monday market (Pazartesi Pazarı) draws millions of tourists every year. Its renovated square is in the Aşağı Pazarcı neighbourhood, with a fruit and vegetable section. Neither news item is dated, so check locally. If you go, start at the market around 08:30 and push the rest of the plan back an hour.

## About the boats: what is official and what to ask

### The municipal river bus, in past seasons

Manavgat Municipality once ran a free "river bus" (Nehir Otobüsü) from the town centre to the Boğaz area. Its news items say two boats worked in shifts at set times, which they did not list. The first season ran through September and October with the Manavgat River Boatmen's Cooperative. None of these items carries a date, they sit in the municipality's news archive, and we found no announcement for 2026. Do not build a day around it. Ask the municipality or the boatmen on the riverbank whether it runs.

### Commercial river boats

River boat trips are sold commercially, and no official page we found publishes their routes, prices or departure times. The metropolitan municipality adds that rafting and canoeing on the river are offered by private businesses. Ask these questions before you pay:

- **Route:** does the boat go downstream to Boğaz, upstream towards the falls, or both? Does it stop at the beach, and for how long?
- **Running today:** is there a sailing on your date, and what happens in bad weather or off season?
- **Duration:** how much time is on the water, and how much is on shore or at a market stop?
- **What is included:** lunch, drinks, hotel pick-up, and the market stop. Which of these cost extra?
- **Price:** the total per person, the currency, and if cards are accepted.
- **Safety:** life jackets in children's sizes, and shade on board.
- **Who runs it:** the operator's name, and a written receipt.

## Manavgat Waterfall: distance, tickets and facilities

Official sources disagree on how far the falls are from town:

- **3 km north of Manavgat:** the ministry's English Manavgat page and GoTürkiye.
- **4 km from Manavgat, 72 km from Antalya:** the metropolitan municipality's waterfall booklet, dated September 2023.
- **2.5 km north:** the ministry's district history.

On tickets, an undated item in the municipality's current news section says its resident card, Manavgat Kart, gives free entry to the waterfall and a 10% discount at businesses inside the waterfall area. That suggests other visitors pay at the gate. We found no official page publishing a visitor fee or opening hours on 14 November 2026, so bring some cash and ask at the entrance.

## Getting to Manavgat

- **By car:** take the Antalya–Alanya road east. The municipality gives 70 km from Antalya and publishes no drive time.
- **From the airport:** the municipality gives 52 km and mentions taxis, connecting buses and some airlines' shuttles. Our [airport transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/) compares the options.
- **By bus:** Antalya's intercity bus station (Otogar) is 4 km north of the city centre, according to the airport operator, which points you to the AntRay tram to get there. Manavgat has its own bus station, the municipality says. We found no official timetable or fare for the minibuses, so ask at the station. Our [Side day trip guide](/blog/side-day-trip-from-antalya/) covers the same route.

## What else sits along the river

- **Side and its ancient water supply:** the ministry says ancient Side drew water from the Manavgat River through carved channels and aqueducts. Its district history gives about 30 km. Its English page names the Dumanlı spring, now inside the Oymapınar dam lake, about 25 km away, and counts ten aqueducts, the largest near Oymapınar. Spend the afternoon in [Side Ancient City](/things-to-do/side-ancient-city/) for the temple at sunset.
- **Green Canyon:** GoTürkiye's canyons page shows only a photo captioned "Green Canyon Antalya", with no location, access or boat details. We found no other official page about it, so it stays out of this plan.
- **Rafting:** for white water, head to [Köprülü Canyon](/things-to-do/koprulu-canyon/) instead.
- **A longer trip:** our [7-day Antalya itinerary](/blog/7-day-antalya-itinerary/) pairs Side with the waterfall on day three.

## FAQ

### How long is the Manavgat river boat trip?

The municipality gives about 8.5 km by river from the town centre to the Boğaz area. No official source publishes a trip duration for the commercial boats, so ask the operator how much of the time is on the water.

### Is there a free boat on the Manavgat River?

The municipality has run a free river bus from the centre to Boğaz in past seasons, but its notices are undated and we found nothing for 2026. Ask locally before relying on it.

### How much does Manavgat Waterfall cost?

No official visitor fee is published. A municipal news item says residents with the Manavgat Kart get in free, which suggests other visitors pay at the gate. Ask at the entrance.

### Can I combine the river with Side in one day?

Yes. Do the waterfall, the boat and Titreyengöl in the morning, then spend the afternoon in Side. Our [Side day trip guide](/blog/side-day-trip-from-antalya/) has museum hours and the theatre closure.
