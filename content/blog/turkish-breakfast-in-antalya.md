A Turkish breakfast in Antalya is not a quick coffee and a pastry. It is a table that fills up plate by plate: white cheese, olives, tomatoes, honey with clotted cream, eggs still sizzling in the pan, warm bread and glass after glass of tea. This guide explains what to order, how a serpme kahvaltı (spread breakfast) works, which Antalya products to look for, and where we can point you to one. Dish notes lean on GoTürkiye, Türkiye's official tourism site. The local products come from GoTürkiye and from the geographical-indication register of TÜRKPATENT, the Turkish patent office. We checked all of them on 13 November 2026. For lunch and dinner dishes, read our guide to [what food Antalya is known for](/blog/what-food-is-antalya-known-for-local-dishes-to-try/).

## What kahvaltı means, and why it takes all morning

The Turkish word for breakfast, kahvaltı, joins kahve (coffee) and altı (under, or before). GoTürkiye translates it as "before coffee": Turkish coffee is strong, so you eat first. The meal usually opens with a glass of Turkish tea and ends with a Turkish coffee, often with a piece of lokum (Turkish delight) on the side.

It is a big meal, too. GoTürkiye says a full breakfast is long and filling enough that a light dinner may be all you need later. Its guide to breakfast in Istanbul describes a pattern you will see across the country. On weekdays many people keep it simple, with simit and tea. At weekends breakfast starts late in the morning, covers the whole table and becomes a social occasion. So treat a weekend breakfast as an outing, not a quick stop.

## Serpme kahvaltı, breakfast plates and buffets

A few words come up again and again on menus. Here is what they usually mean:

- **Serpme kahvaltı:** the spread breakfast. Serpmek means "to scatter", and the small plates are spread across the table for everyone to share. Menus often price it per person, sometimes with a minimum of two.
- **Kahvaltı tabağı:** a breakfast plate, a single portion with a little of everything. The easy choice when you are on your own.
- **Köy kahvaltısı:** "village breakfast", a name used for country-style spreads.
- **Açık büfe:** an open buffet, the usual format in large hotels.
- **Sınırsız çay:** unlimited tea. Worth looking for on a long morning.

Hot dishes are often a separate line. A spread may include eggs, but a pan of menemen can be an extra order. Read what is included before you add more.

## What to order: a Turkish breakfast glossary

GoTürkiye stresses that regional differences are broad, so no two spreads match. These are the names you are most likely to meet.

### Cheese, olives and the cold plates

- **Beyaz peynir:** white cheese, close to feta. GoTürkiye calls it a must at any breakfast.
- **Kaşar:** a mild yellow cheese, also sold aged.
- **Tulum:** a tangy cheese, traditionally aged in a goatskin.
- **Lor:** a soft, fresh curd cheese.
- **Çeçil:** a stringy, braided cheese.
- **Zeytin:** black and green olives.
- **Domates and salatalık:** sliced tomato and cucumber.

GoTürkiye says a breakfast usually brings between one and four cheeses. Taste them side by side.

### Breads and pastries

- **Ekmek:** everyday white bread, crusty outside and soft inside, served warm.
- **Simit:** a sesame-crusted bread ring. GoTürkiye says it turns up at nearly every breakfast.
- **Açma and poğaça:** soft rolls, often filled with cheese, meat or olive paste.
- **Pişi:** fried dough, which GoTürkiye calls a savoury doughnut. Eat it with cheese or jam.
- **Bazlama:** a thick griddle flatbread, often called "village bread".
- **Börek:** filo pastry filled with cheese, meat, potato or spinach. Sigara böreği are thin fried rolls.
- **Gözleme:** thin dough folded around a filling and cooked on a griddle. GoTürkiye's Antalya food page features it, and our [local food guide](/blog/what-food-is-antalya-known-for-local-dishes-to-try/) covers it as street food.

### Eggs and hot pans

- **Menemen:** eggs cooked with tomatoes and green peppers, often served in a small copper pan, sometimes with cheese. The photo at the top of this page shows menemen at a café in Kaleiçi. It is a Wikimedia Commons image, not a picture of any place in this guide.
- **Sucuklu yumurta:** eggs cooked slowly in butter with slices of sucuk, a spiced sausage.
- **Sahanda yumurta:** fried eggs served in the small pan (sahan) they were cooked in.
- **Çılbır:** poached eggs on garlic yoghurt, topped with pepper butter.

### Sweet things

- **Bal kaymak:** honey with kaymak, a thick clotted cream. GoTürkiye calls it a luxury but an absolute must. Honey sometimes comes as a piece of comb.
- **Reçel:** jam. Sour cherry, apricot and fig are common, and Antalya has its own citrus versions (see below).
- **Pekmez and tahin:** fruit molasses and sesame paste. Swirl them together and mop them up with bread.
- **Acuka:** a spicy spread of red pepper and walnuts.

### Drinks

- **Çay:** black tea in a small tulip-shaped glass, topped up through the meal.
- **Türk kahvesi:** Turkish coffee, to finish.
- **Portakal suyu:** orange juice. Ask for it freshly squeezed (taze sıkılmış).

## Antalya on the breakfast table

Most of a spread is the same anywhere in Türkiye. A few things have Antalya roots, and they are worth asking for.

### Bitter orange and bergamot peel jams

Antalya's citrus reaches the jam jar. On 26 May 2021, TÜRKPATENT registered two Antalya peel jams as geographical indications. Both applications came from the Antalya Chamber of Commerce and Industry.

- **Antalya Turunç Kabuğu Reçeli:** bitter orange peel jam. The registration describes peels cut, rolled and strung on cotton thread, then boiled and soaked over several days to soften the bitterness, then simmered in sugar syrup. A little bitterness is kept on purpose, as part of the flavour.
- **Antalya Bergamot Kabuğu Reçeli:** bergamot peel jam, made the same way from a local bergamot variety picked in January and February. The registration says it has been made in Antalya for more than a century.

GoTürkiye's Turkish Riviera guide calls bergamot peel marmalade the city's only geo-marked marmalade. The patent office lists both jams, so go by the register and look for either one. GoTürkiye's Antalya food page also features citrus jam.

### Finike oranges

GoTürkiye describes the oranges of Finike as sweet and juicy, and says they are ready to eat from December to February. If you visit in winter, ask whether the juice is made from local oranges. Our [Finike district guide](/destinations/finike/) covers the town.

### Manavgat sesame: simit and tahini

According to GoTürkiye, Manavgat has been a major producer of sesame seeds since the 1450s. The same guide points to tahini made from the geo-marked Manavgat golden sesame (Manavgat Altın Susamı), and suggests simit at breakfast as the simplest way to taste the region's sesame. Tahini with pekmez is a classic pairing on a breakfast table. See our [Manavgat district guide](/destinations/manavgat/).

### Yörük yoghurt and tulum cheese

GoTürkiye says yoghurt is the province's major contribution from Yörük culture, the culture of the region's nomads. Yoghurt is kept creamy and drained, or saltier and dried. It is also turned into tulum cheese, which is fermented and aged in goatskin sacks. If tulum is on the cheese plate, try it first.

### Serpme börek is not serpme kahvaltı

Do not confuse the spread breakfast with **Antalya serpme böreği**. That is a pastry, registered as a geographical indication on 26 April 2021. The registration describes dough stretched by hand, without a rolling pin, until it is paper-thin. The dough is tossed in the air six or seven times, wrapped around spiced minced meat and onion or lor cheese thinned with milk, and baked. Cheese versions may get a dusting of powdered sugar. The registration ties it to weddings, mevlit gatherings and funerals. It says nothing about breakfast.

## Breakfast etiquette and timing

- **Tea comes first.** Glasses are small and refills are normal. Just ask for another.
- **Go late at weekends.** GoTürkiye describes weekend breakfasts that start late in the morning and fill the whole table.
- **Share and sample.** Serpme plates are for the table. GoTürkiye's advice is to try a little of everything rather than eat until you burst.
- **Check what is included.** Hot dishes, juice and coffee may be listed separately.
- **Check the hours.** Breakfast-only places may close in the afternoon. Avlu Kalkan's listed hours end at 15:00.
- **Finish with coffee.** Order a Turkish coffee to close the meal, as the word kahvaltı suggests.
- **Expect prices to change.** Treat any price you see online as a guide, and confirm on the day.

## FAQ

### What is serpme kahvaltı?

It is the Turkish spread breakfast. Small plates of cheese, olives, vegetables, jams, honey and kaymak, eggs and breads cover the table and are shared, usually with plenty of tea. Menus often price it per person, sometimes for a minimum of two.

### What should I order at a Turkish breakfast if I'm eating alone?

Ask for a kahvaltı tabağı, a single breakfast plate, and add one hot dish such as menemen or sucuklu yumurta. When we checked on 12 November 2026, Avlu Kalkan listed a single breakfast alongside its serpme for two.

### Is Antalya serpme böreği a breakfast dish?

Not by its official description. The geographical-indication registration links it to weddings, mevlit gatherings and funerals, and does not mention breakfast. It is a pastry, not the spread breakfast called serpme kahvaltı.

### Which Antalya products should I look for at breakfast?

Look for bitter orange (turunç) and bergamot peel jams, both registered geographical indications for Antalya. Try tulum cheese, and simit or tahini, which GoTürkiye links to Manavgat sesame. From December to February, ask for Finike oranges.
