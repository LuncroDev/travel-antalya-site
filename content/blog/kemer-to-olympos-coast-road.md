Kemer to Olympos is the classic day drive down Antalya's western coast. In one loop you can ride a cable car to a 2,365 m summit, walk a Lycian harbour town under the pines at Phaselis, explore the ruins of Olympos beside Çıralı beach and finish at dusk at the Chimaera flames. This is a driving day, not a hike: if you want to walk this coast, our [Lycian Way guide](/blog/lycian-way-near-antalya/) covers the trail. In winter the order matters, because the cable car closes early and both ruins stop selling tickets at 16:30. Hours, fees and distances below come from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, the cable car operator, Kemer Municipality, Antalya Metropolitan Municipality and GoTürkiye, all checked on 20 November 2026.

## Kemer to Olympos at a glance

- **Winter order:** Tahtalı cable car first, Phaselis late morning, the Olympos ruins in the afternoon, the Chimaera at dusk.
- **Tahtalı cable car:** the operator's timetable gives 09:00–15:00 every day from 1 November to 31 December, with the last ride up 30 minutes before closing. Kemer Municipality publishes longer hours (see below).
- **Phaselis and Olympos:** 08:00–17:00 every day from 1 November to 30 April, ticket offices close at 16:30. €10 each for foreign visitors aged 8 and over.
- **The Chimaera (Yanartaş):** a 30–40 minute climb from the entrance, according to Kemer Municipality. It says entry is paid but gives no price.
- **Distances:** Kemer is 43 km from Antalya (Kemer Municipality). Çıralı beach is 28 km from Kemer (Antalya Metropolitan Municipality). GoTürkiye puts Olympos some 80 km south-west of Antalya.
- **Tickets:** €20 per adult for the two ruins, plus the cable car and the Chimaera.

## Before you set off

The route follows the D400 coast road south from Antalya; the ministry calls this stretch the Antalya–Finike or Antalya–Kumluca road. GoTürkiye gives 45 to 60 minutes from Antalya to Kemer, so leave by about 08:00 to reach the cable car soon after it opens.

Pack for two climates: a warm layer for the summit, shoes with grip and a torch for the Chimaera path. You'll drive back after dark, so fill up before you leave Kemer.

One quirk of this coast: Kemer Municipality's pages place Çıralı in Kemer district, while the ministry lists the Olympos ruins under Yazır, in Kumluca district. That's why signs and booking sites use both names. Our [Kemer](/destinations/kemer/) and [Kumluca](/destinations/kumluca/) district guides cover each side.

## 09:00 – Tahtalı cable car

Start with the mountain while the day is young. The operator describes a 4,350 m line climbing to a top station at 2,365 m, inside Beydağları National Park. It quotes a ride of about 10 minutes; Kemer Municipality says 15. Each cabin carries 80 people. The operator's About page speaks of a ride "from sea level", but its technical page puts the lower station at 726 m, so you'll drive part of the way up first.

The main entrance is on the D400, and Kemer Municipality reckons on about 30 minutes by car from Kemer town centre. The operator stops letting vehicles onto the access road 15 minutes before the last cable car departure, so don't arrive late. Our [Tahtalı Olympos cable car](/things-to-do/tahtali-olympos-cable-car/) page has more on the ride.

### Winter hours: the sources disagree

- **The operator's [departure times](https://www.olymposteleferik.com/en/departuretimes.html):** 09:00–15:00 daily from 1 November to 31 December (and through to the end of March). The last ascent leaves 30 minutes before closing.
- **Operator's homepage:** 09:00–16:30, with no season given.
- **Kemer Municipality:** from 1 November to 30 April, rides up and down every hour between 10:00 and 17:00.

Plan around the earliest closing and aim to be at the top by mid-morning. The operator warns that times can change with the weather, so check its site before you drive up. We couldn't open its ticket page, so we can't quote fares; check them with the operator.

At the summit, the operator lists food service, walking trails with views and paragliding.

## 11:30 – Phaselis

Back on the coast, head south towards Tekirova. The ministry places Phaselis at the 58th kilometre of the Antalya–Finike road. Antalya Metropolitan Municipality puts it about 12 km from Kemer, while Kemer Municipality says 15 km from the town centre and about 20 minutes by car. There's a car park at the entrance, according to the municipality.

GoTürkiye dates Phaselis to the 7th century BC and picks out the harbour street, the aqueduct, the baths and the agora. Kemer Municipality names three harbours, North, War (or Sheltered) and South, and calls the South Harbour the most important. Walk the paved street down to it, then look for the aqueduct arches on the way back. Our [Phaselis Ancient City](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) page tells the city's story.

### Hours and tickets

- **Winter (1 November–30 April):** 08:00–17:00 every day, ticket office closes 16:30 ([Turkish Museums](https://www.turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/1970-antalya-phaselis-archaeological-site/1970/4)).
- **Foreign visitors:** €10 for everyone aged 8 and over; under-8s free.
- **Turkish citizens:** 200 TL for adults; under-18s and over-65s free.
- **Facilities:** Turkish Museums lists a café, a shop and toilets.

Allow about an hour and a half, and leave by 13:30.

## 13:30 – South to Çıralı

Continue south on the main road. The ministry's directions are simple: after Phaselis, a sign on the Antalya–Kumluca road points to the Olympos road. Antalya Metropolitan Municipality puts Çıralı beach 28 km from Kemer and describes a 3.5 km beach with restaurants, cafés and bars nearby, so this is the natural place for a late lunch.

The ministry's Turkish Museums portal notes that loggerhead turtles (*Caretta caretta*) lay their eggs on this beach. In the nesting season the sand has night-time rules; our [sea turtle guide](/blog/sea-turtle-beaches-in-antalya/) explains them, and our [Çıralı Beach](/things-to-do/cirali-beach/) page covers the bay.

## 14:30 – The Olympos ruins

Olympos sits in a wooded valley that opens onto the beach next to Çıralı. Turkish Museums puts Çıralı just east of the ruins and gives the site's address as Yazır, Olympos Mevkii, in Kumluca. No official page describes the entrances, so ask in Çıralı where to buy tickets before you set off on foot.

The ministry dates the city to the 2nd century BC. GoTürkiye lists an ancient harbour, a theatre, the remains of a bridge, an unfinished temple from the Roman imperial period, tombs and early Christian buildings, and notes that the Lycian Way passes through the site. Turkish Museums points out a temple gate about 150 m west of the river mouth, thought to belong to a small temple for Marcus Aurelius. Our page on [Olympos and the Chimaera flames](/things-to-do/olympos-and-the-chimaera-flames/) has more.

### Hours and tickets

- **Winter (1 November–30 April):** 08:00–17:00 every day, ticket office closes 16:30 ([Turkish Museums](https://turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/1967-antalya-olympos-archaeological-site/1967/4)).
- **Foreign visitors:** €10 for everyone aged 8 and over; under-8s free.
- **Turkish citizens:** 200 TL for adults; under-18s and over-65s free.
- **Facilities:** toilets and a shop.

Buy your ticket before 16:30, then take your time. Give it about an hour and a half.

## Dusk – The Chimaera flames (Yanartaş)

The day ends with fire. Kemer Municipality places Yanartaş on the rocky hillside north of Çıralı beach. It says you can drive to the entrance, then climb for 30 to 40 minutes from the steps there. The ministry's own visitor page describes about an hour's walk up a narrow, uneven slope from a stream bed beyond Çıralı, so allow for the longer figure if you're not a quick walker.

Turkish Museums explains what you'll find: a flame that never goes out, fed by gas seeping from the ground, at a site that was a cult centre of Hephaistos. The ancient sacred way and building foundations are still visible, and the portal says the view is most striking at night. GoTürkiye says the flames have burned for more than 2,500 years.

No official page gives opening hours. Kemer Municipality says entry is paid but names no price. Start the climb in the last of the daylight and come down by torch.

## The drive back, or stay the night

GoTürkiye puts Olympos some 80 km from Antalya, and in winter you'll drive all of it in the dark. If that doesn't appeal, stay over. [Olympos Lodge](/hotels/olympos-lodge-cirali/) is in Çıralı, a short walk from the ruins. Closer to Phaselis, [Rixos Premium Tekirova](/hotels/rixos-premium-tekirova/) is one of several resorts on our [Kemer hotels](/hotels/?area=Kemer) list. With a night in Çıralı you can do the Chimaera after dinner and the cable car the next morning.

## Without a car

Official pages cover parts of the route, not the whole loop:

- **Antalya to Kemer:** GoTürkiye lists dolmuş, taxi or car, taking about 45 to 60 minutes.
- **Phaselis:** Kemer Municipality says public transport can take you to the entrance, then it's about a 30-minute walk to the ancient city.
- **Cable car:** the operator runs a paid shuttle from its main entrance on the D400 to the lower station, hourly from 10:00. It also sells daily transfers from Kemer, Beldibi, Göynük, Kiriş, Çamyuva and Tekirova, booked in advance.
- **Çıralı and Olympos:** see the bus notes in our [Lycian Way guide](/blog/lycian-way-near-antalya/). From the airport, our [transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/) covers the run to Kemer.

In winter, linking all four stops this way is unrealistic. Pick two.

## MuseumPass or single tickets?

Both Turkish Museums pages advertise the MuseumPass The Mediterranean at €90 for seven days, and our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) lists Phaselis and Olympos among the sites it covers. For this day alone, though, single tickets cost €20, so the pass only pays if you're seeing a lot more of the province that week. Turkish Museums adds that MuseumPass cards aren't valid for night museum visits.

## Swap or add stops

- **Adrasan:** further south in Kumluca district. Antalya Metropolitan Municipality describes a sandy beach of about 2 km, with boat trips to nearby bays and diving. Our [beaches guide](/blog/best-beaches-in-antalya/) covers it.
- **Beycik:** one of Kemer district's villages, about four hours' walk down from Tahtalı's summit, according to Kemer Municipality. It suits walkers more than drivers; see the Lycian Way guide.
- **Keep going:** if you're heading further down the coast, our [Finike day plan](/blog/finike-limyra-arykanda-one-day/) picks up where this one ends.

## Summer, briefly

Summer days are longer and so are the hours. The operator's timetable runs the cable car 09:00–18:00 from 1 June to 30 September, and it adds sunrise trips and, on set days, sunset trips from June to October. Kemer Municipality gives 09:00–19:00 from 1 May to 31 October. Turkish Museums lists 08:00–19:00 for both ruins in summer (ticket offices close 18:30), although its summer date ranges are mislabelled, and GoTürkiye says Olympos stays open until 21:00 in summer. Our [Antalya in winter](/blog/antalya-in-winter/) guide helps if you're weighing up the seasons.

## FAQ

### How far is it from Kemer to Olympos?

Antalya Metropolitan Municipality puts Çıralı, beside the Olympos ruins, 28 km from Kemer. Kemer Municipality says the Chimaera is about an hour from Kemer.

### Can you see Phaselis and Olympos in one day?

Yes, by car. Both are open 08:00–17:00 in winter, with ticket offices closing at 16:30, and each costs €10 for foreign visitors. Do Phaselis in the morning and Olympos in the afternoon.

### Is the Tahtalı cable car open in winter?

Yes. The operator's timetable shows 09:00–15:00 daily in November and December, with the last ride up 30 minutes before closing; Kemer Municipality gives 10:00–17:00. Weather can change the schedule, so check with the operator on the day.

### Do you pay to see the Chimaera flames?

Kemer Municipality says entry is paid, but no official page we found gives the price. The climb from the entrance takes 30 to 40 minutes, according to the municipality.
