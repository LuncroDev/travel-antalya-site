A Perge and Aspendos day trip is the classic ruins outing from Antalya: two great Pamphylian cities east of the centre, one famous for its colonnaded streets and the other for its theatre. Add Sillyon, the cliff-top city in between, and you have three very different sites in one day. We checked the hours and fees on the ministry's Turkish Museums pages and read the Sillyon excavation team's news pages, all on 27 November 2026. Prices and hours below are as published that day.

## The route at a glance

All three cities sit on the plain east of Antalya, in a line that runs from the city towards Belek and the Köprüçay river:

1. **[Perge](/things-to-do/perge-ancient-city/)**, in Aksu, which Turkish Museums places 17 km east of the city centre.
2. **[Sillyon](/things-to-do/sillyon-ancient-city/)**, on a flat-topped hill in Serik, about 8 km north of the Antalya–Alanya highway according to Pamukkale University's excavation team. Our Sillyon page, from the team's location notes, gives it as about 12 km from Perge and 17 km from Aspendos.
3. **[Aspendos](/things-to-do/aspendos-theatre/)**, in Belkıs, Serik, near the Köprüçay.

You need a car, a taxi for the day or a tour. Sillyon has no ticket office or bus stop, and the official pages give no public transport details for any of the three.

## Perge: start early with the colonnades

Perge is the best first stop because it is closest to the city and opens earliest. Turkish Museums says people lived here from the Early Bronze Age and that the city may appear in Hittite texts as **Parha**. Its Hellenistic towers date from the 3rd century BC, but the golden age came under Rome in the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD, when the theatre, stadium, agora, baths and fountains were built. The ministry's blog on Antalya's ancient cities puts the theatre at about 15,000 seats and the stadium at 12,000. St Paul's journey inland to Pisidian Antioch began here, according to the museum page.

### Hours and tickets at Perge

- **Winter (1 November–30 April):** 08:00–17:00 daily, ticket office closes 16:30.
- **Summer:** 08:00–19:00, ticket office closes 18:30. The page prints the summer dates as "1–31 October", which looks like an error, so expect the long hours from spring to autumn but check.
- **Entry:** €11 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over; ₺200 for Turkish adults; free for foreign children under 8.
- **Facilities:** café, shop and toilets.

Allow about two hours to walk the gate, the colonnaded street, the baths and the stadium.

## Sillyon: the city Alexander could not take

Sillyon is the wild card. It sits on a rocky hill about 235 m high above the plain, in Kocagözler, Yanköy, according to Pamukkale University's 2019 news release, and it is still being excavated. The team describes almost continuous settlement from the 2nd millennium BC to Ottoman times. Their 2023 news item says Alexander the Great besieged the city and gave up, making Sillyon and Termessos the two cities of the region he did not take.

What you can see: Hellenistic walls and a tower, a Roman stadium and bath, a necropolis, an Ottoman fountain and the **Kale Mescidi**, a 13th-century Seljuk mosque that the team calls one of the oldest in the region. Digging began in 2020 and has run under a presidential decree since 2022, with work on the stadium, the bath and the necropolis.

### Visiting Sillyon safely

No official page gives opening hours or a fee, and there are no visitor facilities. The hill is prone to **landslides**, which is why the excavation team calls it an "archaeo-geological park", so stay on clear paths and keep away from cliff edges. Wear proper shoes and carry water. An hour is enough for the lower town and stadium; allow more if you climb to the top. Read our full [Sillyon guide](/things-to-do/sillyon-ancient-city/) before you go.

## Aspendos: finish at the theatre

Aspendos is the grand finale. Turkish Museums dates its coinage to the 5th–4th centuries BC and says Alexander took the city in 333 BC on condition of heavy tribute. It came under Rome in 133 BC and flourished in the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD. In the 12th century the Seljuks used part of the theatre as a palace.

The museum page gives the theatre a capacity of **about 15,000**; the ministry's own blog says 20,000, so take either figure as approximate. Don't skip the **aqueduct**, about 1 km long according to the museum page, or the basilica and agora on the acropolis; our guide to [Aspendos beyond the theatre](/blog/aspendos-beyond-the-theatre/) shows you where to walk.

### Hours and tickets at Aspendos

- **Winter (1 November–30 April):** 08:00–17:00 daily, ticket office closes 16:30.
- **Summer:** 08:00–19:00, ticket office closes 18:30 (the same doubtful "1–31 October" summer dates appear here).
- **Entry:** €15 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over; ₺200 for Turkish adults; free for foreign children under 8.
- **Facilities:** car park, shop and toilets. No café is listed, so eat before you arrive.

In winter, aim to be at the gate by 15:00 so you have time before the ticket office shuts at 16:30.

## A sample timetable

This plan works in winter, when both ticketed sites close at 17:00:

| Time | Stop |
|---|---|
| 08:00 | Perge opens; walk the colonnaded street and stadium |
| 10:30 | Drive east to Sillyon |
| 11:00 | Sillyon: walls, stadium, mosque (no facilities) |
| 12:30 | Lunch in Serik or near Belek |
| 14:00 | Aspendos: theatre, then the aqueduct and acropolis |
| 16:30 | Aspendos ticket office closes |

In summer the longer hours let you start later or end at Aspendos in the evening light.

## Tickets: is the MuseumPass worth it?

Perge and Aspendos together cost €26 for a foreign adult at the November 2026 prices. Both museum pages advertise the **MuseumPass The Mediterranean** at €90 for seven days, covering more than 40 sites in Antalya, Mersin, Adana and Denizli (it is not valid for night-museum openings). For this day alone the pass doesn't pay; it starts to make sense if you add Side, Termessos or the Lycian sites later in the week. Our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) runs the numbers.

## FAQ

### Can you visit Perge and Aspendos in one day?

Yes. Both are east of Antalya and open daily; in winter both close at 17:00, with ticket offices shutting at 16:30. Start at Perge at 08:00 and finish at Aspendos by mid-afternoon.

### How much does it cost to enter Perge and Aspendos?

On 27 November 2026 Turkish Museums listed €11 for Perge and €15 for Aspendos for foreign visitors aged 8 and over, and ₺200 each for Turkish adults.

### Is Sillyon open to visitors?

It is an open, unstaffed site under excavation, with no published hours, fee or facilities. Landslides are an ongoing risk, so stay on clear paths.

### How far is Perge from Antalya?

Turkish Museums puts Perge 17 km east of the city centre, in the Aksu district.
