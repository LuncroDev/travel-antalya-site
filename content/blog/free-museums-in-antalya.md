Free museums in Antalya are few but worth knowing about, especially in winter when the big Antalya Museum is closed. Three museums in the city centre charge nothing at all, for foreign visitors or Turks: the **Ethnography Museum** in Kaleiçi, the **Mevlevi Lodge Museum** beside the Yivli Minaret, and the **Atatürk House Museum** on Işıklar Caddesi. We checked each one's entry on Turkish Museums, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's official portal, on 27 November 2026; every ticket category for all three reads "Ücretsiz" (free).

## Why these museums matter now

The city's main archaeological museum is shut. Our [Antalya Museum page](/things-to-do/antalya-museum/) explains the closure and what we know about its future. That leaves the free museums, the lanes of [Kaleiçi](/things-to-do/kaleici-old-town/) and the ancient sites outside town as your options. The three free museums are small, but together they cover Ottoman daily life, the Mevlevi dervish order and the early Republic, and you can see all three in a morning.

## The Ethnography Museum (Kaleiçi)

**What it is:** two Ottoman-period mansions in the old town, which the ministry calls the Lower and Upper Mansion.

**What you see:** the Lower Mansion holds Turkish-Islamic objects, finds from excavations in Kaleiçi, Seljuk glazed tiles that the page says came from the Aspendos theatre, and İznik, Kütahya and Çanakkale ceramics. The Upper Mansion recreates Ottoman daily life, with Döşemealtı carpets and looms and displays on the life of the Yörük nomads. The garden has old gravestones, epitaphs and cannons.

**Practical details:**

- **Address:** Kılınçarslan Mahallesi, Civelek Sokak No. 20, Muratpaşa. (The older muze.gov.tr listing gives Mermerli Banyo Sokak 17 — both are in Kılınçarslan; our [Ethnography Museum page](/things-to-do/antalya-ethnography-museum/) has both.)
- **Winter hours (1 November–30 April):** 08:30–17:30 daily, ticket desk closes 17:00.
- **Phone:** +90 242 244 64 01.
- **Facilities:** toilets only.

The ministry's own "five museums to see in Antalya" blog includes it in its shortlist alongside much bigger museums.

## The Mevlevi Lodge Museum (Yivli Minaret complex)

**What it is:** the dervish lodge in the complex around the [Yivli Minaret Mosque](/things-to-do/yivli-minaret-mosque/), the fluted brick minaret that is Antalya's best-known landmark. The complex also includes a bath and the tombs of Zincirkıran Mehmet Bey and Nigar Hatun.

**History:** Turkish Museums says the lodge building has no inscription and is thought to have been built by the Seljuk sultan Alaeddin Keykubad in 1255. It became a Mevlevi lodge in the 16th century. The page dates Mehmet Bey's tomb to 1377 and estimates the bath at the 13th century from its plan; it was used as the lodge's bath from Ottoman times.

**What you see:** information boards and images about the Mevlevi order, plus costumes and objects. The page also mentions whirling ceremonies (sema), Sufi concerts and talks held here; no dates are given, so ask on site.

**Practical details:**

- **Address:** Yivli Minare Külliyesi, Cumhuriyet Caddesi No. 55, Selçuk Mahallesi.
- **Winter hours (1 November–30 April):** 08:30–17:30 daily, ticket desk closes 17:00.
- **Phone:** +90 242 244 64 00.
- **Facilities:** toilets only.

## The Atatürk House Museum (Işıklar Caddesi)

**What it is:** the house the people of Antalya gave to Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. Turkish Museums tells its long story: it served as a governor's office, then from 1939 as a girls' art school and institute building, passed to the Ministry of Agriculture in 1952 and to the Ministry of Culture and Tourism in 1984. It was pulled down in a zoning scheme and rebuilt by the Kepez Elektrik company, and has been the Atatürk House and Museum since **1986**.

**What you see:** period newspapers, photographs and a documentary on the first floor; Atatürk's personal belongings and a coin collection from the founding of the Republic to the present on the second.

**Practical details:**

- **Address:** Haşim İşcan Mahallesi, Işıklar Caddesi, Muratpaşa.
- **Winter hours (1 November–30 April):** 08:30–17:30 daily, ticket desk closes 17:00.
- **Phone:** +90 242 241 15 27.

## A note on summer hours

All three city museums list summer hours of 08:30–19:00 (ticket desk 18:30), but on 27 November 2026 Turkish Museums printed the summer season as "1–31 October" only, leaving May to September uncovered. That looks like a data error rather than a closure. If you're visiting between May and September, call ahead or check the page again nearer the time.

## A free-museum walking route

The three city museums are close together around the edge of the old town, so you can link them on foot:

1. **Start at the Mevlevi Lodge** beside the Yivli Minaret, on Cumhuriyet Caddesi above the old harbour.
2. **Walk down into Kaleiçi** to the Ethnography Museum in Kılınçarslan, through the lanes of our [Kaleiçi walking route](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/).
3. **Leave the old town by [Hadrian's Gate](/things-to-do/gate-of-hadrian/)** and head east to the Atatürk House on Işıklar Caddesi.

Allow 30–45 minutes in each museum. Finish with lunch in Kaleiçi; our [Turkish breakfast guide](/blog/turkish-breakfast-in-antalya/) and [what Antalya food is known for](/blog/what-food-is-antalya-known-for-local-dishes-to-try/) suggest what to order.

## Free further afield: Alanya's Atatürk House

In Alanya, the **Alanya Atatürk House Museum** is also free for everyone. Atatürk stayed in this three-storey Alanya mansion on 18 February 1935. A timber merchant, M. Tevfik Azakoğlu, built it; the family later donated it to the Ministry of Culture, and it opened to visitors after restoration in 1987. Inside are Atatürk's vest, shirt and shoes, photographs, a telegram to the people of Alanya and rooms on local weddings and children's games.

Its hours are the hardest to pin down. Turkish Museums lists 08:30–19:30 from 20 May to 1 October and 08:30–17:30 from 2 January to 30 April, closed Mondays, with the weekend cells marked closed too — and no hours at all for the rest of the year. The page itself says to call, as hours change by season: **+90 242 513 12 28**. The address is Şekerhane Mahallesi, Gücüoğlu Sokak No. 22. Combine it with [Alanya Castle](/things-to-do/alanya-castle/) and the [Alanya Museum](/things-to-do/alanya-museum/), which charge entry.

## When you want more than free

Free museums fill a morning; Antalya's real treasures are its ancient cities. If you plan to visit several paid sites, the **MuseumPass The Mediterranean** costs €90 for seven days and covers more than 40 sites in the region, according to Turkish Museums — read our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) to see if it pays. ## FAQ

### Which museums in Antalya are free?

On 27 November 2026, Turkish Museums listed the Antalya Ethnography Museum, the Mevlevi Lodge Museum and the Atatürk House Museum in the city, and the Atatürk House Museum in Alanya, as free for every visitor category.

### Is the Antalya Museum open?

No. The archaeological museum is closed; see our [Antalya Museum page](/things-to-do/antalya-museum/) for the latest we know.

### What are the winter opening hours of Antalya's free museums?

From 1 November to 30 April, the three city museums open 08:30–17:30 daily, with the ticket desk closing at 17:00.

### Do I need the MuseumPass for free museums?

No. The pass only helps at paid sites such as Perge and Aspendos.
