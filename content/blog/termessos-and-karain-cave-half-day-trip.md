Termessos and Karain Cave are the two great sights of Döşemealtı, the district that climbs north of Antalya into the Taurus foothills, and together they make an easy half-day trip. One is a Pisidian city high on a mountain that held out against Alexander the Great. The other is a cave where people lived over a vast stretch of prehistory. Both are run by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, and in winter both close their ticket desks in the mid-afternoon, so the order you visit them in matters. This plan sets out the timings, the fees and two Seljuk caravanserais you can add on the way. All facts were checked on official pages on 15 November 2026.

## Termessos and Karain Cave at a glance

- **Termessos:** in Güllük Dağı (Termessos) National Park. Winter hours (1 November to 30 April) 08:00–17:00, **ticket desk closes 15:30**.
- **Karain Cave:** at Yağca, about 30 km north-west of Antalya. Winter hours 08:00–17:00, **ticket desk closes 16:30**.
- **Fees:** Karain €5 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over. Termessos lists €3 for foreign visitors aged 8–18; its adult line has an obvious typo (see below).
- **Getting there:** no official page describes public transport to either site. Plan on a car or a taxi.
- **Order:** Termessos first, because its desk closes earlier and the site needs the most walking.
- **Time needed:** about six and a half hours from Antalya and back on our plan, lunch included; add an hour or two for the caravanserais.

## Why combine them

They are close, they tell very different stories, and neither needs a full day on its own. Termessos is the big outdoor visit: ruins spread over a mountain, a theatre that looks down to the coast, and a lot of uphill walking. Karain is short and focused: a cave entrance with deep human history and a small museum beside it. Doing Termessos in the cool of the morning and Karain after lunch suits both. For the full background on each, see our [Termessos](/things-to-do/termessos/) and [Karain Cave](/things-to-do/karain-cave/) pages. The [Döşemealtı district guide](/destinations/dosemealti/) covers the wider area.

## 08:30 – Leave Antalya

Start early, above all in winter. Termessos's ticket desk closes at 15:30 from November to April, and you will want at least two to three hours on the site. Bring water, a hat and good shoes, and expect a lot of uphill walking.

## 09:00 – Termessos

Turkish Museums gives the address as Bayatbademleri, Güllük Dağı, inside Termessos National Park. The park is protected for its plant cover and wildlife as well as the ruins, so stay on the paths.

**What to see.** The city first appears in history in 333 BC, when Alexander the Great blockaded it and the people of Termessos held out without surrendering. The highlight for most visitors is the **theatre**, built into the slope with a view out over the Antalya coast. Our [Termessos page](/things-to-do/termessos/) walks you through the site.

**Tickets and hours.** The Turkish Museums page lists winter opening (1 November to 30 April) from 08:00 to 17:00, with the ticket desk closing at 15:30. Its "summer" row covers only 1–31 October (08:00–19:00, desk 17:00) and it gives no hours for May to September, so recheck before a summer visit. Turkish citizens aged 8 and over pay 200 TL, and foreign visitors aged 8–18 pay €3. The line for foreign adults reads **€300**, an obvious typo; we could not confirm the real adult fee from the ministry, so check at the gate or on the Turkish Museums page before you go. Turkish citizens under 18 and over 65, foreign children under 8 and university students of art history, archaeology and museum studies enter free. The page lists toilets and a car park.

## 12:00 – Karain Cave

Turkish Museums places Karain about 30 km north-west of Antalya, at Yağca in Döşemealtı, on the eastern side of the Katran mountains, and calls it one of Türkiye's largest natural caves. Our [Karain Cave page](/things-to-do/karain-cave/) explains its long prehistory and the small site museum.

**Tickets and hours.** Winter (1 November to 30 April) 08:00–17:00, with the ticket desk closing at 16:30. Foreign visitors aged 8 and over pay **€5**, as do foreign 8–18-year-olds; foreign children under 8 enter free. Turkish citizens pay 200 TL, and those under 18 or over 65 enter free. The page lists toilets, a shop and a car park. As on the Termessos page, the "summer" row covers only October, so recheck in spring and summer.

Allow an hour. Some of the cave's finds went to the Antalya Museum, which is [closed for now](/things-to-do/antalya-museum/), so the site is what you can see.

## 13:30 – Lunch on the way back

There is no café listed at either site. Pack a picnic, or eat on the way back into town: Kepez has two grill houses on our list: [Ertan Usta](/restaurants/ertan-usta-aksu-kofte-piyaz-kepez/) for köfte and piyaz, and [Usre Restoran](/restaurants/usre-restoran-kepez/) by Kepez City Forest.

## Optional: two Seljuk caravanserais

The road north towards Burdur passes two caravanserais from the Seljuk period. Antalya Metropolitan Municipality's booklet *Caravanserais & Madrasas of Antalya* (September 2023) describes both:

- **Evdir Han** stands at the 18th km of the Antalya–Burdur highway. Its inscription dates it to the reign of Sultan Kaykaus I, between 1211 and 1219. At 3,800 m² it is, by the booklet's count, the third-largest Anatolian Seljuk inn by area: an open courtyard ringed by two rows of porticoes. The inscription is gone, and part of the portal has collapsed.
- **Kırkgöz Han** lies 14 km north of Evdir Han. Its inscription names Kaykhusraw II as builder; the date is unreadable, but comparisons with similar inns place it around 1236–1246. It has a courtyard with porticoes on two sides and a barrel-vaulted hall to the north.

We found no official opening hours or visiting rules for either han, so treat them as roadside stops to see from outside unless the gate is open.

## 15:00 – Back to Antalya

Without the caravanserais you should be back in the city by mid-afternoon. If you have energy left, finish with a walk in [Kaleiçi](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/).

## Winter or summer?

This is a good winter trip: the walking at Termessos is hard work in summer heat, and both sites keep a full day's hours in winter, apart from the early ticket desks. In summer, start at opening time and be off the mountain before midday. Our [Antalya in winter](/blog/antalya-in-winter/) guide has more cool-season ideas.

## Is a MuseumPass worth it here?

Probably not for this trip alone: Karain costs €5, and Termessos's adult fee is unclear on the ministry page. ## FAQ

### Can you visit Termessos and Karain Cave in one day?

Yes, comfortably in half a day by car. Do Termessos first: its ticket desk closes at 15:30 in winter, an hour before Karain's.

### How much does Termessos cost?

Turkish Museums lists 200 TL for Turkish adults and €3 for foreign visitors aged 8–18. The foreign adult line shows "€300", an obvious typo, so confirm the real fee before you go.

### How far is Karain Cave from Antalya?

Turkish Museums puts it about 30 km north-west of Antalya, at Yağca in Döşemealtı district.

### Is there public transport to Termessos?

No official page describes one. Plan on a car or a taxi; if you take a taxi, agree a waiting time with the driver.
