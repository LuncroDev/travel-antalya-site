Antalya on a budget is easier than it looks. Some of the city's best hours cost nothing: a morning in the old town, two free museums, a waterfall park and a sunset on the cliffs. When you do pay, most ancient sites near the city charge €5 to €15, and a bus or tram ride costs 42 TL on an AntalyaKart. This guide lists what is free, what is cheap and what is closed, with the published fee for each paid sight, checked on 13 November 2026. Fees come from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's [Turkish Museums](https://www.turkishmuseums.com/museum/detail/1963-antalya-aspendos-archaeological-site/1963/4) site and the [AntalyaKart fare page](https://www.antalyakart.com.tr/Page/Faqs). For hotel, food and transport costs in general, read our guide to [whether Antalya is expensive](/blog/is-antalya-expensive-for-tourists-cost-of-travel-explained/).

## Antalya on a budget at a glance

- **Free:** Kaleiçi's lanes, Hadrian's Gate, Karaalioğlu Park, the Ethnography Museum and the Atatürk House Museum. Further west, the ruins of Arykanda are free too.
- **Cheap:** Karain Cave €5, Phaselis and Olympos €10 each, Perge €11, Aspendos €15. Termessos is listed at €3 for visitors aged 8 to 18, with an obvious error for adults (see below).
- **Getting around:** 42 TL a ride on an AntalyaKart, 46 TL on a single-use ticket, 50 TL with a contactless bank card.
- **Children:** foreign children under 8 get into the ministry's sites free.
- **Closed:** the Antalya Museum, plus the theatres at Perge and Side.

## Free things to do in Antalya city

### Walk Kaleiçi from Hadrian's Gate

The old town is the best free morning in Antalya. Walk through [Hadrian's Gate](/things-to-do/gate-of-hadrian/), the triple arch on the eastern edge of [Kaleiçi](/things-to-do/kaleici-old-town/), and follow the lanes down towards the old harbour. The gate stands in the street, so there is no ticket. Our [Kaleiçi walking route](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/) links it with Hıdırlık Tower, the Clock Tower and the Fluted Minaret in one loop. On that route only the museums charge, and one of them is free.

### Sunset in Karaalioğlu Park

Finish the walk in [Karaalioğlu Park](/things-to-do/karaalioglu-park/), the public park on the cliffs beside Hıdırlık Tower. This is where Antalya comes for its evening stroll: palm-lined paths, benches above the sea and a view across the gulf to the mountains. Bring a flask of tea and a snack, and the evening costs nothing.

### Two free museums in the centre

The Antalya Museum is closed (more below), but two ministry museums in the centre are free:

- **Antalya Ethnography Museum.** Turkish Museums lists entry as free for every visitor group. Winter hours, from 1 November to 30 April, are 08:30–17:30 every day, and the ticket desk closes at 17:00. It is in Kaleiçi, and our [walking route](/blog/kaleici-walking-route/) describes its tiles, carpets and recreated Ottoman rooms.
- **Antalya Atatürk House Museum.** The ministry's information sheet says you can visit free of charge, every day. From 1 October to 1 April it opens 08:30–18:00. Atatürk stayed here in March 1930 and came back in 1935. The house was pulled down in 1984, rebuilt, and opened as a museum in 1986. It is in Haşim İşcan Mahallesi, a short walk from the old town.

The Kaleiçi Museum, run by Koç University, is the paid exception in the old town. Our [rainy-day museums guide](/blog/antalya-museums-rainy-day/) has its prices and its Monday closing.

### Upper and Lower Düden

The Düden stream makes two waterfalls inside the city; see our [Düden waterfalls](/things-to-do/duden-waterfalls/) page. The metropolitan municipality's waterfalls booklet, dated September 2023, describes Upper Düden as a place to eat, drink, rest and picnic, run by the municipality's own companies. Lower Düden, in the Lara area, drops 40 metres into the sea.

The booklet gives no entry fee, and we found no current official fee page. Carry some cash in case the park charges at the gate. A picnic at Upper Düden is still one of the cheapest half-days in Antalya.

### Konyaaltı and the city beaches

[Konyaaltı Beach](/things-to-do/konyaalti-beach/) runs along the city's west side, so you don't need a car. Lay a towel on a free stretch, or pay for a sunbed at one of the beach cafés. Our [best beaches guide](/blog/best-beaches-in-antalya/) explains how operators split the beach between them, and why Lara is the sandy alternative. One caution: Mermerli, the small cove below Kaleiçi, has charged entry in recent seasons, so check before you walk down.

### City events

Until 20 November 2026, the municipality's COP31 city programme includes performances at Hıdırlık and at Hadrian's Gate. Our [COP31 city events listing](/events/cop31-city-events-2026/) has the dates. The programme does not give entry rules for each activity, so don't assume every event is free. For later dates, browse the [events calendar](/events/) and read the admission note on each listing.

## Cheap ancient sites near Antalya: published fees

These are the published fees for foreign visitors on each site's Turkish Museums page, checked 13 November 2026. Hours are for the winter season, 1 November to 30 April.

| Site | Entry | Winter hours | Ticket desk closes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Arykanda](/things-to-do/arykanda-ancient-city/), near Finike | Free | 08:00–17:00 | 16:30 |
| [Termessos](/things-to-do/termessos/) | €3 (see note) | 08:00–17:00 | 15:30 |
| Karain Cave | €5 | 08:00–17:00 | 16:30 |
| [Phaselis](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) | €10 | 08:00–17:00 | 16:30 |
| [Olympos](/things-to-do/olympos-and-the-chimaera-flames/) | €10 | 08:00–17:00 | 16:30 |
| [Perge](/things-to-do/perge-ancient-city/) | €11 | 08:00–17:00 | 16:30 |
| [Aspendos](/things-to-do/aspendos-theatre/) | €15 | 08:00–17:00 | 16:30 |

Turkish citizens pay in lira instead (200 TL at Aspendos, for example), and those under 18 or aged 65 and over get in free. Foreign visitors pay in euros. Our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) explains how they are charged at the desk.

### The Termessos price

The Termessos page lists €3 for foreign visitors aged 8 to 18, but €300 for adults. That is almost certainly a typing error. The ministry's fee table gave €3 when we checked it for our MuseumPass guide on 10 November. Expect €3, and confirm at the desk. Mind the early cut-off: the ticket desk shuts at 15:30 in winter, and the path up to the ruins is steep.

### Karain Cave

Karain is the cheapest paid outing close to the city. The ministry describes a natural cave on the Katran Mountains that people used as a shelter from the Palaeolithic onwards. Skeletons, stone tools and worked flint have been found inside. The ministry's English page puts it 27 km north-west of Antalya, while Turkish Museums says 30 km. It is at Yağca, in [Döşemealtı district](/destinations/dosemealti/), the same district as Termessos.

### Children and students

Turkish Museums gives free entry to foreign children in its 0–8 age band at all these sites, and its Termessos page charges from age 8. Our MuseumPass guide quotes the ministry directive as free for children aged 8 and under, so an eight-year-old may be asked to pay. Ask at the desk. The pages also list free entry for university students of art history, archaeology and museum studies. They don't say whether that includes foreign students, or what proof you need, so carry a passport and a student card.

### Should you buy the MuseumPass?

On a budget, probably not. Turkish Museums lists the MuseumPass The Mediterranean at €90 for seven days, and says it is not valid for night openings. The six paid sites in the table add up to €54 at single prices, with Termessos at €3. Our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) shows that the pass only pays off on a touring week that covers both ends of the province.

Press reports in September 2026 said a new ministry price list takes effect on 1 April 2027. If you travel after that date, check the fees again.

### What is closed

- **Antalya Museum.** The ministry lists it as closed while a new museum is built. The newspaper Vatan reported on 10 September 2026 that the old building was pulled down in September 2025 and that the new one's foundations had not yet been laid, so don't plan around a reopening. See our [Antalya Museum page](/things-to-do/antalya-museum/) for updates.
- **Perge theatre.** Our Perge guide reports a ministry notice that the theatre is temporarily closed for restoration. The English Turkish Museums page does not mention the closure, so plan to see it from outside.
- **Side theatre.** Closed for restoration. Our [Side ancient city guide](/things-to-do/side-ancient-city/) has the details.

## Getting around cheaply: AntalyaKart fares

City buses and the AntRay tram are the cheapest way around Antalya. The AntalyaKart FAQ lists these fares, checked 13 November 2026:

| Ticket | Published price |
|---|---|
| One ride on an AntalyaKart, full fare | 42 TL |
| Single-use "Kullan At" ticket, one ride | 46 TL |
| One ride with a contactless bank card | 50 TL |
| The AntalyaKart card itself | 100 TL |
| Kullan At card | 20 TL |
| Kullan At, 2 rides / 5 rides | 112 TL / 250 TL |

### Transfers and which ticket to buy

With an AntalyaKart, you get one free transfer within an hour, or within two hours between tram lines. Kullan At tickets and bank cards get no transfers. A few lines are excluded, including 500, 501, 514, 515 and 515A.

For a couple of rides, tap your bank card. For a week, buy the AntalyaKart. It saves 8 TL a ride against a bank card, so the 100 TL card pays for itself after about 13 rides, sooner if you change between bus and tram. The FAQ does not say whether the 100 TL includes any credit. It lists card centres at Batıgar and Doğu Garajı, plus top-up points, machines and the AntalyaKart app.

Landing at the airport? Our [airport transfer guide](/blog/antalya-airport-transfers-belek-side-kemer-kas/) covers the AntRay tram and the minibuses from the bus station.

## A free and cheap day in Antalya

Our suggestion, built around the winter hours above:

1. **08:30, Ethnography Museum (free).** Go when it opens.
2. **10:00, Hadrian's Gate and Kaleiçi (free).** Follow our walking route.
3. **13:00, lunch.** Pack a picnic, or eat simply away from the harbour.
4. **14:30, Atatürk House Museum (free).** Open until 18:00 in winter.
5. **Late afternoon, Karaalioğlu Park (free).** Stay for the sunset.

On a second day, visit Perge (€11) or Karain Cave (€5) in the morning, well before the 16:30 ticket cut-off.

## Budget tips that save real money

- **Watch the ticket desk, not the closing time.** In winter most desks shut at 16:30, Termessos at 15:30.
- **Carry water and food** to the ruins. Our [Phaselis page](/things-to-do/phaselis-ancient-city/) notes that the ministry lists no café there.
- **Stay central.** A room in or near Kaleiçi puts the free sights within walking distance. Our [where to stay guide](/blog/where-to-stay-in-antalya-best-areas-for-different-travelers/) compares the areas, and the [Muratpaşa district guide](/destinations/muratpasa/) covers the centre. ## FAQ

### What can you do for free in Antalya?

Walk Kaleiçi and through Hadrian's Gate, watch the sunset from Karaalioğlu Park, and visit the Ethnography Museum and the Atatürk House Museum, which are both free. Public stretches of Konyaaltı Beach cost nothing unless you hire a sunbed.

### How much is the tram in Antalya?

The AntalyaKart FAQ lists 42 TL a ride on an AntalyaKart, 46 TL on a single-use Kullan At ticket and 50 TL with a contactless bank card. Only the AntalyaKart gives free transfers.

### Which ancient site near Antalya is cheapest?

Arykanda, near Finike, is free. Closer to the city, Termessos is listed at €3 and Karain Cave at €5. Aspendos is the dearest of the nearby sites at €15.

### Do children pay at Antalya's museums and ruins?

Not under 8. Turkish Museums gives foreign children in its 0–8 band free entry at the ministry's sites. Sources differ on whether an eight-year-old pays, so ask at the desk. Older children pay the adult fee, apart from Termessos's €3 rate for ages 8 to 18.
