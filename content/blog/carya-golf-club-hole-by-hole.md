This Carya Golf Club hole-by-hole guide covers all 18 holes of the heathland-style course at Kadriye, in Belek. Thomson Perrett & Lobb designed it, it opened in November 2008, and it plays to **par 72 and 6,605 from the black tees**. Every hole is floodlit, and the course hosted the Turkish Airlines Open from 2016 to 2018. We built this guide from the club's own scorecard, hole notes and course pages, read on 23 November 2026.

For booking notes and photos, see our [Carya Golf Club profile](/golf/carya-golf-club/). For the neighbours, read [Belek golf courses compared](/blog/belek-golf-courses-compared/) and our [Montgomerie Maxx Royal hole-by-hole guide](/blog/montgomerie-maxx-royal-hole-by-hole/).

## The course at a glance

The course information page calls Carya a heathland course set in pine forest, over 104 hectares (260 acres). The club's About page says the design took its cue from the heathland courses of Surrey in England, and names Wentworth, Sunningdale, Walton Heath and Swinley Forest. The key numbers:

- **Four tees on every hole:** Black, White, Yellow and Red.
- **75 bunkers.**
- **Four lakes**, which frame eight holes.
- **Greens** of about 600 m² on average.

The scorecard prints its own totals, and our sums of the hole figures agree:

| Tee | Front nine | Back nine | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black | 3,033 | 3,572 | **6,605** |
| White | 2,757 | 3,392 | **6,149** |
| Yellow | 2,568 | 3,182 | **5,750** |
| Red | 2,184 | 2,678 | **4,862** |

One thing to know: **the scorecard gives no unit**, in its English or its Turkish version, and none of the club pages we opened says whether these are metres or yards. We show every length exactly as printed.

The card is lopsided. The front nine is par 34, with three par 3s and one par 5. The back nine is par 38, with three par 5s and a single par 3, and from the black tees it is 539 longer than the front.

## The front nine, hole by hole

Every hole at Carya has a name, and the club explains most of them. Lengths below are **Black / Red** from the scorecard. SI is the stroke index, where 1 marks the hardest hole. The hole notes mention par on four holes (2, 8, 14 and 15), and each one matches the scorecard.

1. **Out — par 4, 391 / 305 (SI 14).** A wide fairway for the first drive. Aim at the trees in the middle and you get a good approach to a green with no bunkers, so an early birdie is on. The name recalls old seaside courses that went nine holes out and nine back.
2. **The Old Nursery — par 3, 209 / 112 (SI 16).** The longest par 3 from the back tee. The green slopes away at the front, so club choice matters and short is the miss to avoid. The name comes from the nursery that once stood near the green.
3. **Dire Straits — par 4, 416 / 303 (SI 2).** Trees in the middle of the fairway split the hole into two routes. Right of them is the easier drive but leaves a harder second shot. Left is braver and opens up the green. This is the second-hardest hole on the card.
4. **Heather Hills — par 4, 368 / 280 (SI 6).** Drive down the left for the best angle and a view of the green. From the right side the approach is blind, over the heathery mounds that give the hole its name. The club says "Heather Hills" nearly became the name of the club itself.
5. **Troubled Water — par 4, 364 / 279 (SI 4).** The fairway tilts from left to right, and a lake borders both fairway and green. The green is hard to hold: come up short and you are wet, go long and you are in trouble. The pin position changes how it plays.
6. **The Wall — par 3, 177 / 85 (SI 10).** The green sits raised just beyond a lake and a wall. Think hard about the club, especially with a front flag. From the red tee this is the shortest hole on the course.
7. **The Long — par 5, 553 / 412 (SI 8).** The longest hole at Carya. The club's advice is to hit it long and straight, and to avoid missing this long green on the right, where recovery is difficult.
8. **The Siren — par 3, 172 / 119 (SI 18).** Short, pretty and the easiest hole by stroke index, yet the club warns it still fights back. Like the sirens of myth, the beauty is the trap; take care with a pin on the left.
9. **Taurus — par 4, 383 / 289 (SI 12).** On a clear day you can see the Taurus Mountains, which give the hole its name. Ignore the view and the heather, and drive to the right side of the fairway for the best angle in.

## The back nine, hole by hole

The back nine holds three of the four par 5s and two of the three hardest holes by stroke index (the 11th and the 10th).

10. **The Plank — par 5, 496 / 394 (SI 3).** Straight, narrow and surrounded by water, like the pirates' plank it is named after. Long hitters can reach the green in two. Stay dry and it is a birdie chance.
11. **The Kraken — par 4, 343 / 273 (SI 1).** The hardest hole on the card is the shortest par 4 from the black tee. The fairway runs between forest and lake, and the club says you don't need a driver here: finding the fairway is what counts. Balls that stray too close to the water are, in the club's telling, pulled under by the sea monster.
12. **The Square — par 5, 498 / 386 (SI 13).** A natural hole that doglegs across a ridge. The shortest line is just right of the bunkers in the middle of the fairway. The square green is a nod to the old heathland courses that inspired Carya.
13. **The Centaur — par 4, 373 / 280 (SI 9).** A downhill tee shot to a fairway split in two by a step, which gives the hole its two characters. Stay on the higher right side for the best view of a tricky green.
14. **The Coffin — par 3, 157 / 109 (SI 17).** Uphill, so take an extra club. Anything short rolls back into the coffin-shaped hollow in front of the green.
15. **Greed — par 5, 494 / 374 (SI 11).** The shortest par 5 from the black tee and reachable in two, but it demands real accuracy. The name is the warning: don't get greedy, and don't lose concentration.
16. **Bosphorous — par 4, 395 / 260 (SI 15).** The club's spelling. A drive close to the bunkers on the right of the fairway leaves an easier angle and keeps you away from the deep bunkers by the green. The hole crosses water as you turn back toward the clubhouse. From the red tee it is the shortest par 4.
17. **Javelin — par 4, 406 / 297 (SI 7).** Nearly straight, as the name suggests. Keep out of the water and clear of the tall trees on the left. The green slopes in every direction.
18. **Home — par 4, 410 / 305 (SI 5).** There is room for a big drive, but favour the right to stay away from the water. The second shot needs focus: the green sits slightly raised among old pines. The club's About page says the Turkish and US presidents met on the terrace above this green during the 2016 G20 summit.

## Reading the card

Set the scorecard beside the hole notes and a few patterns stand out:

- **The hardest hole is a short one.** The Kraken (11th) is SI 1 at 343 from the back, and the club tells you to leave the driver in the bag.
- **Water.** The hole notes mention water or a lake on seven holes: 5, 6, 10, 11, 16, 17 and 18. The course page counts eight holes framed by the four lakes, so the notes don't flag every one.
- **Tee choice reshapes the 7th.** From the black tee it measures 553. From the white it is 88 shorter, the biggest step between neighbouring tees anywhere on the card. From the yellow tee (442) it is the shortest par 5, just under the 15th (445).
- **Birdie chances.** The club itself points to birdies on the 1st, 10th and 11th, so the hardest hole on the card is also one of its named scoring chances.
- **Aces on every par 3.** The hole-in-one honours board records aces made in official tournaments at Carya. It lists 18 between March 2019 and November 2024: six on the 14th, five on the 6th, four on the 8th and three on the 2nd.

## Under the floodlights

Carya says it was the first course in Europe to put floodlights over all 18 holes. The lights are set high enough to follow the ball's flight as you would by day. The floodlight page suggests an early-morning or late-evening round in July and August. ## Course records

Carya counts course records from the black championship tees, and says only four players have set or matched one:

- **2009:** Simon Edwards (England), 66, at the Beko Pro-Am.
- **2010:** Oscar Floren (Sweden), 63, at the Turkish Airlines Challenge.
- **2016:** Thorbjorn Olesen (Denmark), 62, on the way to winning the first Turkish Airlines Open played at Carya.
- **2018:** Thomas Pieters (Belgium), 62, matching the record at the Turkish Airlines Open.

## FAQ

**How long is Carya Golf Club?**
The scorecard gives 6,605 from the black tees, 6,149 from white, 5,750 from yellow and 4,862 from red, all par 72. It doesn't say whether these are metres or yards.

**Which is the hardest hole at Carya?**
The 11th, The Kraken, is stroke index 1: a par 4 of 343 from the black tee, squeezed between forest and lake. The 3rd (SI 2) and the 10th (SI 3) come next.

**What handicap do I need to play Carya?**
The course information page sets a maximum of 36 for men and 36 for ladies.

**Can you play all 18 holes under floodlights?**
Yes. Carya says all 18 holes are floodlit, a first in Europe by its own account.
