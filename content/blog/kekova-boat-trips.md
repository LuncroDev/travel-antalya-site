Kekova boat trips are the easiest way to see one of the strangest sights on the Lycian coast: the stairs and house walls of a town that slipped under the sea. Boats leave from the village of Üçağız and from Kaş. They pass over the sunken remains off Kekova Island and stop at Kaleköy, ancient Simena, where a castle stands on the hill above the water. This guide explains what you see, where to start, which rules apply in the protected area, and what the official sources leave out. Our facts come from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, its Antalya directorate, the Ministry of Environment and GoTürkiye, as we found them on 18 November 2026.

## Kekova boat trips at a glance

- **Where boats leave from:** Üçağız, where the Antalya culture directorate says boats are hired for Kaleköy and Kekova Island, and Kaş, where GoTürkiye says daily boat trips start.
- **What you see:** sunken stairs and walls off Kekova Island, a bay stop on the island, and Simena castle above Kaleköy.
- **The big rule:** the area is protected, and the directorate says diving into the water over the sunken city is forbidden. You look from the boat.
- **Simena castle:** €4 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over. Winter hours are 08:30–17:30 (Turkish Museums).
- **Boat prices and times:** no official page publishes them. Ask at the harbour or at your hotel.
- **Which district:** Simena's official address is in [Demre](/destinations/demre/), but the directorate measures Üçağız from [Kaş](/destinations/kas/), 36 km away.

## What Kekova is

Kekova is the name of an island and of a whole stretch of coast. Turkish Museums describes Kekova Island as 7.4 km long, lying about 500 m off Simena. The wider area takes in Üçağız (ancient Teimiussa), the pier at Aperlai, Aquarium Bay and Gökkaya Bay.

The coast is a Special Environmental Protection Area. The Ministry of Environment's leaflet says the Kaş–Kekova area was declared one by Council of Ministers decision 90/77 of 18 January 1990, and puts its size at 257.83 km². Turkish Museums rounds this to 260 km². The leaflet names five islands, Kekova being the largest, and counts about 1,000 marine species, among them the monk seal and loggerhead and green turtles.

The Antalya directorate's page calls the area a national park instead. The labels differ, but every source agrees that the area is protected.

## What you see on a Kekova boat trip

Routes vary by boat. Treat this as the list of sights the official pages describe, not a fixed programme.

### The sunken city off Kekova Island

According to Turkish Museums, earthquakes in antiquity sank part of the island's northern shore. Stone stairs, house walls and the remains of piers now lie 4–5 m under water. The Antalya directorate says that as boats pass over the sunken city, you can make out its outlines and its steps beneath the surface.

Look closer to the mainland, too. Between Kaleköy and Üçağız, Turkish Museums says, submerged roads and quays show through the water among small islets. People once quarried those islets, especially for stone coffins.

### A stop on the island

The directorate says boats can put in at Tersane Koyu, a bay on Kekova Island. It gives no further detail, so don't expect a timetable or facilities.

### Simena and Kaleköy

Kaleköy is built on top of ancient Simena. Turkish Museums calls it one of the rare settlements in Türkiye that can only be reached from the sea. GoTürkiye says the same. The ministry dates the town to the 4th century BC and says it belonged to a small league led by Aperlai, with Apollonia and Isinda.

Things to look for, from the shore upwards:

- **A Roman bath** near the water, with an inscription dedicating it to the emperor Titus.
- **Two sarcophagi on the path** to the castle. One has a small exedra; the other is inscribed to Mentor, son of Argus.
- **A tiny theatre** cut into the rock, with seven rows and room for about 300 people.
- **The castle**, which Turkish Museums says survives largely intact. Inside are cisterns, rock tombs and a building that served as a temple, then a church, then a mosque.
- **The necropolis** northeast of the castle, with sarcophagi and rock tombs. One house-type tomb carries a Lycian inscription.
- **The view.** On a calm day, the ministry says, Lycian sarcophagi, a jetty and building remains are easy to see in the water below.

For more Lycian tombs, see our [Lycian rock tombs guide](/blog/lycian-rock-tombs/).

### Üçağız (Teimiussa)

Üçağız stands on Teimiussa, a small Lycian harbour town. The Antalya directorate says an inscription takes its history back to the 4th century BC. Before you board, walk along the shore:

- **A house-type tomb behind the pier,** dated to the 4th century BC, with a carved figure of a nude young man.
- **Sarcophagi by the sea** on the east side, most of them Roman.
- **An ancient pier cut from the rock** at the east end of the old town, 28 m long and 8 m wide.

Demre Municipality says UN Tourism named Kale Üçağız one of its Best Tourism Villages 2025, at a ceremony in Huzhou, China.

## Üçağız or Kaş: where to start

### From Üçağız

The directorate says boats hired at Üçağız take visitors to Kaleköy and Kekova Island. GoTürkiye describes smaller fishing boats here. The advantage is obvious: you start inside the bay the boats are visiting.

The directorate's Teimiussa page says that, coming from Antalya, you turn south 18 km before Kaş, then drive for about half an hour. Its main page puts Üçağız 36 km from Kaş, and GoTürkiye says the drive from Kaş takes about 40 minutes. For the coast road itself, see our [Antalya to Kaş day trip guide](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/).

### From Kaş

GoTürkiye says daily boat trips leave from Kaş. We found no official page that gives their departure times or length. The appeal is simple: no driving, and you sleep in town afterwards. Browse [Kaş hotels](/hotels/?area=Ka%C5%9F) such as [Casa Bi](/hotels/casa-bi-kas/), [Carruba Boutique Hotel](/hotels/carruba-boutique-hotel-kas/) or [Suna Sun Hotel](/hotels/suna-sun-hotel-kas/). For dinner back in town, try [Naturel](/restaurants/naturel-restaurant-kas/), [Elia](/restaurants/elia-restaurant-kas/) or [Ehl-i Keyf Meyhane](/restaurants/ehl-i-keyf-meyhane-kas/).

### By sea kayak

GoTürkiye says many people think sea kayaking is the best way to see the underwater ruins, and that travel agencies in Kaş run kayak trips to Kekova. The no-diving rule still applies, so you stay in the kayak over the ruins.

We found no official page listing boat trips from Demre town itself.

## Rules in the Kekova protected area

### What the official pages ban

- **No diving over the sunken city.** The Antalya directorate says diving into the water here is banned ("suya dalmak yasaklanmıştır"). The protected area's marine management plan for 2014–2017 says the stretch between Kekova Island and Üçağız is closed to diving because it is an archaeological site.
- **Other no-dive zones.** The environment ministry's leaflet also bans diving off the west coast of Gata Burnu and in Bohça İskelesi Bay, because of archaeological remains. It lists 28 dive sites elsewhere in the area, with a diving season from April to January. If you want to scuba dive, ask a Kaş dive centre which sites it can use.
- **Swimming.** None of the official pages we read sets a separate swimming rule. The Turkish phrase *suya dalmak* can mean either diving or plunging into the water, so the safe reading is to stay in the boat over the sunken city. Ask the skipper where the swim stop is.
- **Anchoring.** The management plan says anchors have badly damaged the seagrass meadows, and that anchoring in sheltered bays should be restricted or replaced by buoys. On a private charter, ask the skipper to use a buoy where there is one.
- **Take nothing.** Turkish Museums lists Simena as a first-degree archaeological site. Leave stones and pottery where they lie. Our [Antalya shopping guide](/blog/antalya-shopping/) explains why antiquities can't leave Türkiye.

### Will the diving ban change?

A ministry regulation of March 2020 allows controlled tourist diving at chosen points on protected underwater sites. In November 2020, the DHA news agency reported that scientists had picked three points at Kekova, while Demre's mayor said the exact spots were not yet known. We found no official notice that any of them has opened, so treat the ban as current.

## Simena castle: hours and tickets

Turkish Museums lists these times and prices for the Simena archaeological site:

- **Winter (1 November–31 March):** 08:30–17:30. The ticket office closes at 17:00.
- **Summer (1 April–31 October):** 08:30–19:00. The ticket office closes at 18:30.
- **Price:** €4 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over, including 8–18-year-olds. Foreign children under 8 go free. Turkish adults pay 200 TL.
- **E-tickets:** the page links to muze.gov.tr. The **MuseumPass The Mediterranean** (€90, seven days) covers Simena; our [MuseumPass guide](/blog/museumpass-the-mediterranean-antalya/) shows whether it pays.
- **Facilities:** toilets only. Bring water.
- **Contact:** demremuzesi@ktb.gov.tr. The phone number on the page is incomplete, so email instead.

The ministry's other portal, muze.gov.tr, would not load its Simena page today.

## Prices, timetables and seasons: what we could not confirm

- **Boat prices.** No operator, municipality or ministry page we opened publishes them.
- **Departure times and trip length.** The same: nothing official. The Kaş district governorship's tours page would not open for us.
- **Winter boats.** No official page says how many boats run between November and March. The castle keeps its winter hours, but a boat has to get you there. Ask your hotel the day before, and expect wind to stop small boats.
- **Busy months.** The management plan names June to September as the peak, with about 50,000 visitors estimated for 2011.

Before you board, agree on these points:

1. The price, and whether it is per person or per boat.
2. How long the trip lasts, and whether it stops at Kaleköy long enough to climb to the castle.
3. Whether the €4 castle ticket is included. The ministry sells it separately, so expect to pay at the gate.
4. Where the swim stop is, if there is one.
5. What happens if the weather turns.

## Combining Kekova with Demre and Kaş

Üçağız lies off the coast road between Demre and Kaş, so Kekova fits neatly into a two-day trip.

**Day 1: Demre and Kekova.** Start at [Myra Ancient City](/things-to-do/myra-ancient-city/) for the rock tombs, then visit St Nicholas Church; our [Myra and St Nicholas guide](/blog/myra-and-st-nicholas-church-demre/) has the details. After lunch, drive to Üçağız for an afternoon boat. In winter, Simena's ticket office closes at 17:00, so don't leave it too late. Sleep in Kaş.

**Day 2: Kaş and the coast.** If you skipped Üçağız, take a boat or kayak from Kaş. Otherwise head west to [Kaputaş Beach](/blog/kaputas-beach-travel-antalya/) or [Patara](/blog/patara-beach-and-ancient-city/).

As a day trip from Antalya, Kekova makes for a very long day. Our [Antalya to Kaş guide](/blog/antalya-to-kas-day-trip/) explains why it works better with a night on the coast.

## FAQ

### Can you swim at the Kekova sunken city?

Not over the ruins, to be safe. The Antalya culture directorate says diving into the water there is banned, and the marine management plan closes the stretch between Kekova Island and Üçağız to diving. No official page sets a separate swimming rule, so swim only where your skipper stops.

### Where do Kekova boat trips leave from?

From Üçağız, where the directorate says boats are hired for Kaleköy and Kekova Island, and from Kaş, where GoTürkiye says daily trips start. Kaş agencies also run sea-kayak trips.

### How much does a Kekova boat trip cost?

No official source publishes boat prices. The only fixed price we found is Simena castle: €4 for foreign visitors aged 8 and over, on Turkish Museums on 18 November 2026.

### Is Kekova worth visiting in winter?

Simena is open all winter, 08:30–17:30, with the ticket office closing at 17:00. No official page lists winter boat departures, and weather decides a lot, so ask locally the day before.
